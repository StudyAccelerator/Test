## The day 1 call email, edit by edit

What the session wrote, and what he changed it to:

| Session | Waleed | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| "Waleed here." | "Dr Waleed here." | The title goes in the intro line, once. It's how he introduces himself in the Sonia email too ("I'm Dr Waleed"). Never repeated in the body. |
| "A report is a good start. A conversation is better, because..." | "A report is a good start, but a full strategy plan is better." | Two things. He joins the two fragments into one flowing sentence with "but"; he does not write in punchy two-beat contrasts. And he names the concrete thing: the call is a **strategy plan**, not "a conversation". |
| (nothing) | "There is no 'one size fits all' when it comes to revision." | He is comfortable with everyday phrases. Copywriters avoid them; he uses them because that's how people talk. |
| "your subjects, your school and your term dates change what I'd actually do first" | "Your subjects, your school and your study habits, all change what I'd actually do first." | Picks the more relevant item (study habits), and the comma before "all" is a spoken rhythm, a small pause. |
| "So here's the offer:" | "So here's a reward for completing the revision diagnostic and taking an active approach to your grades:" | The biggest change. The free call is not an offer he is making, it's a **reward the reader has earned** by acting. And the reader gets a compliment for acting. |
| "students who've taken the diagnostic" | "students keen to improve their grades" | He qualifies by **ambition, not by action**. The reader self-selects as keen. |
| "No cost, and I'm not going to spend the call selling to you." | "No cost, all I ask for return is that you keep me updated on the improvements you see to your grades :)" | The defensive line is gone entirely (same rule as the sales pages). In its place, a light **ask in return** that quietly assumes the improvement will happen, keeps the relationship going, and ends with a smiley. |
| (no PS) | "PS: I am a full time doctor so bear with me if I'm on-call or on nights haha, give me a few days which work best and I'll definitely make some time :))" | The PS does three jobs at once: restates the one action in a lighter voice, shows his real life (on-call, nights), and shows character (haha, the double smiley). Ian Stanley's rule, in Waleed's own hand: the PS is prime selling space and the place for personality. |

Spelling in his drafts gets corrected silently (bear, definitely, make). Nothing else in his own copy is ever "improved".

## The rules

### 1. Openers and sign-offs

- Students: "Hi {$name}," then, on the first email of any sequence, "Dr Waleed here." After that the body just starts.
- Parents: "Hi {$name}," and on first contact "I hope you're well! I'm Dr Waleed, the founder of A-Level Accelerators." Sign off "Best wishes," then "Waleed".
- Students sign off "Keep going!" or just "Waleed". Either is fine. The engine adds the photo signature after the bare "Waleed" line.

### 2. Sentences

- Complete, connected sentences. Join thoughts with "but", "and", "so", "because", "which means". Never two fragments doing a contrast ("Reports are useful. Conversations are better."). Never a one-word sentence for effect ("Genuinely." "Full stop.").
- Median sentence around 12 words. If a paragraph reads as a speech, it's wrong.
- Soft modals everywhere: "can be", "usually", "tends to", "I think", "to be honest", "genuinely", "a bit". He hedges the way people do when they mean it.
- Everyday phrases are welcome ("no one size fits all", "bear with me", "sort a time", "runs away with me"). Ornamental metaphors are not (no fog, no machine, no academic tax, no "the door stays open" as a refrain).
- Contractions always. "gonna" is fine in student emails, "wanna" is not.

### 3. Framing

- **Free things are rewards.** The call, the template, the extra teach: "here's a reward for taking the diagnostic and taking an active approach to your grades". Compliment the reader for acting.
- **Qualify by ambition.** "for students keen to improve their grades", "for parents who want to get ahead of this term", never "for students who've taken the diagnostic".
- **Name the thing.** The call is "a full strategy plan" or "your strategy plan call", never "a chat" or "a conversation". The programmes are named. The guarantee is named.
- **Confidence, never defence.** Cut on sight: "no pitch", "I'm not going to sell to you", "who shouldn't join", "keep your money", "I can't show you", "I won't pretend", "I don't have grade statistics", "I'd treat anyone promising grades with suspicion", "I'm a doctor before I'm a business owner", "not a sales trick", "no countdown timers", "if it's ever too much, the unsubscribe is at the bottom". Never name what we don't offer. Never pre-empt scepticism. State proof plainly without justifying it, in his order: "on average, our students jump two grades", then a real testimonial quote, then the satisfaction guarantee. (The old confidence statistic, 6.2 to 8.3, is retired on his instruction, 6 September: it undervalues the progress.)
- **The guarantee is the satisfaction guarantee**: join the first session, and if you're not completely satisfied you get your money back, no questions asked. Grade guarantees are never mentioned in either direction.
- **Reciprocity beats disclaimers.** Where the old copy said "no pitch", the new copy asks for something small and warm in return: "all I ask is that you keep me updated on how it goes", "tell me which profile you got".
- The free path can be mentioned as a real option ("and if you'd rather run the free version for now, that's completely fine, it works"), once per sequence, not in every email.

### 4. Warmth markers

- Students: one ":)" per email at most, "haha" now and then in a PS, "amazing", "genuinely", "to be honest", "I promise". Exclamation marks are fine.
- Parents: "I hope you're well!", exclamation marks, "please don't hesitate", "I'm confident we can help with this", "happy to". Smileys stay rare with parents; his Sonia email had none.
- The doctor's real life is allowed to show: shifts, on-call, nights, "between patients". It's credibility and character in one line, and it explains any delay in replying.

### 5. The PS

- **Every email has one** (the only exceptions are the nine-word re-engagement emails, which must stay nine words).
- Its job: restate the single action in a lighter voice, and add a bit of him. A joke, a confession, the on-call line, a reply ask.
- It may carry the link when the body has none, or the call scheduler as the secondary pointer. Never a second button. Keep total links at one or two per email for Primary-tab placement.
- It is the second most-read line after the subject line, so it never says nothing.

### 6. Honesty rules that still bind

None of the above licenses invented numbers or fabricated student results. The approved proof points are in `audience-and-offers.md`: the two-grade average is Waleed's own stated figure and is used as he gave it; testimonials are real quotes from `lib/testimonials.ts`; the guarantee is the satisfaction guarantee. Sessions never add figures he has not said himself.

## The test before loading

Read each email once as if he's saying it on a call. Then check: Hi + intro right? Any fragment pairs? Any defensive line? Is the free thing a reward? Is the call named as the strategy plan? Is there a PS with one action and some character? One smiley at most? Would he say every sentence out loud?
