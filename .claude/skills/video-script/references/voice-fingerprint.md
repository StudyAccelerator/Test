# How Waleed actually talks: the spoken fingerprint

This file is the ground truth for putting words in Waleed's mouth. It was built by transcribing and analysing 34,373 words of him speaking live, unscripted, to real students. Every quote below is verbatim from a recording. If a script line could not plausibly appear in these transcripts, it is not finished.

**Sources (Zoom cloud recordings, analysed 28 July 2026):**

- "Top 1% Study Systems Workshop", 2 May 2026, 59 min, solo presentation (11,967 words of Waleed)
- "Dr Waleed's Study Series" session 1, 6 May 2026, 65 min, solo presentation (12,908 words)
- "A-Level Accelerators Exam Skills Workshop", 20 Feb 2026, 147 min, Waleed presenting and hosting with live student Q&A (9,631 words of Waleed)

The March to May subject classes (Biology, Chemistry) are taught by his tutors, not him, so they were excluded. No one-to-one student call recordings exist in Zoom cloud for Feb to July 2026; if Waleed points at recordings of those elsewhere, fold them in and update this file. Student names from the workshops stay out of this file and out of every script.

## The numbers (measured, not vibes)

- **Speaking rate: 200 to 215 words per minute** across all three recordings (201, 208, 216). Budget scripts at 3.0 to 3.4 words per second of runtime. A 30 second video is 90 to 100 words, a 60 second video is 180 to 200. Count the words every time.
- **Median spoken sentence: 12 words.** A quarter of his sentences are 7 words or fewer. If a script's sentences average over 15 words, it reads as writing, not Waleed.
- **Sentence openers, ranked by his real usage:** So (323), And (314), Okay (106), But (94), Now (84), I (84), You (77), It's (57), Because (57), That's (43), Right (42). He chains ideas with "So" and "And" at the front of sentences constantly. Written-English connectors (However, Therefore, Additionally, Furthermore) appear approximately never.
- **Tag questions are his glue:** "okay?" 406 times, "right?" 219 times, roughly one every 55 words live. On camera keep a light sprinkle (1 to 2 per 30 seconds); captions full of "okay?" look odd, but zero of them stops sounding like him.
- **Contractions always.** "gonna" 92 times (fine to write it), "it's", "you're", "don't" throughout. He does not say "wanna" (once in 34K words), so don't write it.

## The voice moves (each one is evidenced, not invented)

**1. "Amazing!" is his energy marker.** He opens rooms and greets answers with it (49 uses). "Amazing! Welcome to the Top 1% Study Systems Workshop." Also his transition after someone does something right: "Cool. Loads of practice questions. Amazing."

**2. The second-person mirror.** He opens by describing the viewer to themselves, then asks if it landed: "I want you to think about someone who's revising every evening. Maybe they have a tutor. They're paying attention in class, they're doing all the right things... but they're still sitting at a B." Then: "Does that sound familiar? Maybe it sounds like you." This is his single most repeatable cold open for camera.

**3. Diagnose before you treat, in his exact words.** His own flagship analogy, which he flags himself: "I use it all the time, because I think it applies really well. Being a doctor... in order for me to prescribe medication or figure out what the right management of a patient is, I need to first diagnose them, right? You need to first figure out where the issue lies in order for you to fix it."

**4. The core reframe: never smart enough, always never taught.** "It isn't because they're not smart enough. It's not because they're not working hard. It's because they weren't properly taught how to perform well in exams." And its sibling: "Schools teach you what to learn, but no one actually teaches you how to perform well in exams. And those are two completely different things."

**5. "Are you someone who..."** (11 uses). His diagnostic question device: "Are you someone who can retrieve information under pressure without the notes in front of you? If you can't do that, then you don't know it."

**6. "What do I mean by that?"** (5 uses). He asks himself the question the viewer was about to ask, then answers it. "The second mistake is that you're not using active recall. Okay, what do I mean by that? Learning information and then being able to recall it for an exam are not the same thing."

**7. He voices the student's inner monologue.** "You're like, oh my god, I just did an 8-hour workday. I read through all my notes, look how fancy and neatly I wrote them... But did that actually help you improve your grades? Not really." Also the exam version: "the examiner says, 5 minutes remaining, and you're like, oh my god, I've got half the paper left."

**8. Confession before advice.** He indicts himself first, then teaches: "When I was in year 12 and year 13, I spent 6 hours in one sitting trying to create the perfect timetable. The next week, I didn't touch it again." And: "I was someone who literally used to finish every paper the second the examiner says put your pencils down." And the permission line: "I promise this is a judge-free zone. I was guilty of a lot when I was at your stage."

**9. Concrete named pictures, not abstract claims.** His explanations run on specific scenes and characters:
   - The smoking doctors: "I'm a doctor, I see people smoking in hospitals. Everyone knows smoking is bad for you. The knowledge has never been the issue, it's about the implementation."
   - Einstein: "Let's say Einstein was doing his GCSEs today... he's got all the knowledge in the world, but if he can't apply it to the question and what the examiner wants, he's not going to get the marks."
   - The examiner as a person: "Your examiner is going through dozens and dozens, if not hundreds of papers in a day... they're seeing, okay, step one, yep, done. Step two. Oh, I don't see step two here."

**10. Do less, better.** "Top students don't do more, they do things better." "They spend the same amount of time, and usually less, to be honest, on higher yield activities." "These are time-consuming activities which do not move the needle." The trap he keeps naming: "the worst thing is, with low-yield activities, they make you feel so productive."

**11. Direct instruction, warm command.** "I want you to..." (22 uses) and "I don't want you to be that person." He tells you exactly what to do next, in imperatives: "Be honest with yourselves. Which one of these are you struggling with? And that tells you which ones you need to prioritise."

**12. Peer-level credibility, lightly worn.** "I'm not much older than you, even though I may look it, but I was in your position very, very recently. So I know what you're going through." The proof point in his phrasing: "I've worked with over a thousand students." Honesty beats hype: "I've not reinvented the wheel. I've not discovered gravity." He admits the method is hard: "Things like blurting are super, super powerful, but people don't do it regularly because it's really stressful, and it's really humbling when you realise how little you know."

## Live-room devices, translated for camera

He built these habits in workshops with a chat box. Scripts translate them instead of copying them:

- "Pop it into the chat" becomes "comment below" or gets cut.
- Reading chat answers back becomes asking the camera the question and answering it himself ("Are you someone who...? If that's you...").
- "Cameras on please" and housekeeping lines get cut entirely.
- "Give me a show of hands" becomes "be honest".
- The TikTok joke survives anywhere: "I know I've lost a couple of you to the TikTok algorithm."

## The read-aloud gate (run every script through this)

1. Read the spoken track out loud once, timed. Within the format's second-count at 3.0 to 3.4 words per second? If not, cut.
2. Any sentence over ~20 words: split it or cut it. Median must sit near 12.
3. Any sentence starting with However, Therefore, Moreover, Additionally, or "In today's...": rewrite with So, And, But, Now, Because, Okay.
4. Any word from the never-list: replace it with his word.
5. At least one of his moves (mirror, confession, self-question, inner monologue, named picture) in every script. A script with none of them is generic.
6. Contractions everywhere they'd occur in speech. "Gonna" allowed, "wanna" not.
7. Tag check: one or two "okay?" / "right?" maximum per 30 seconds. Zero is also wrong.
8. The mouth test, in his own instruction from the batch-1 scripts: "Read each aloud once and cut anything your mouth trips on."
