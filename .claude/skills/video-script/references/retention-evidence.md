# Why short-form holds attention: the evidence, and what it makes the script do

Researched 28 July 2026. Every claim carries its source and a confidence label: [platform] = the platform itself or its named executives; [study] = peer-reviewed or controlled research; [benchmark] = industry dataset with a stated sample; [practice] = working-creator practice, plausible but unproven. If a claim is not in this file or the hook library, a script does not get to lean on it. Section 7 lists famous numbers that are banned because they cannot be traced.

## 1. What the platforms say they rank (their words, not folklore)

- **TikTok weights completion.** "A strong indicator of interest, such as whether a user finishes watching a longer video from beginning to end, would receive greater weight than a weak indicator." (TikTok Newsroom, "How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou", 18 Jun 2020.) The leaked internal ranking formula sums predicted likes, comments, playtime and plays (NYT, "How TikTok Reads Your Mind", 5 Dec 2021, authenticity confirmed by TikTok). [platform]
- **Instagram Reels: watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach.** Adam Mosseri, Jan 2025: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends... pay close attention to average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach." Sends matter slightly more for reaching non-followers. Earlier official ranking post: the key predictions are reshare, watch all the way through, like, audio-page visit (Instagram blog, 31 May 2023). [platform]
- **Instagram's headline Views metric counts replays** (announced 7 Aug 2024), so a video people watch 1.4 times genuinely outscores one watched 0.9 times. [platform]
- **YouTube Shorts is judged first on "viewed vs swiped away"** (official Shorts analytics metric, 2023). There is no click to earn; the first frames are a binary keep-or-discard vote. [platform]
- **Ranking is per video, not per channel.** Todd Beaupre (YouTube Growth and Discovery): "the algorithm for Discovery is focused more on individual videos... If your last video wasn't so great and your next video is great, we want to realize the potential of each video." (Search Engine Journal, 4 Mar 2024.) Same shape on TikTok: the For You page drives 7 of 10 views (Metricool 2026 study, 2.3M posts). A brand-new account is not handicapped; every video is a fresh ticket. [platform] [benchmark]

**So the script's one job is:** get the highest share of strangers to the last second, then hand the platform a replay or a send.

## 2. The first seconds (where the audience is won or lost)

- TikTok's own top-ads analysis: "Over 63% of all videos with the highest click-through rate highlight their key message or product within the first 3 seconds." Also from the same analysis: 33% of highest view-through ads break the fourth wall looking straight into the lens, 40% use text overlays. (TikTok For Business, Auction Ads Creative Tips.) **This describes what winners look like, it is not a causal lift; never quote it as one.** [platform]
- "50% of the impact from a TikTok ad is realized in the first 2 seconds," 90% of ad-recall impact by second 6 (TikTok Marketing Science, reported 27 Sep 2023). [platform]
- Meta's measured survival curve: of people who watch the first 3 seconds, 65% reach 10 seconds, 45% reach 30 (Facebook for Business, Feb 2016, feed video; dated but the only platform-published curve of its kind). [platform]
- No platform has ever published a benefit to a wind-up intro. [platform]

**So:** the first spoken line is the payoff promise or the gap, in one breath, strongest visual already on frame. Names and credentials live in the first-frame text layer and caption, not in the first sentence. (This is also the pacing rule's front-load law and the hook library's credential-in-two-seconds doctrine; three sources, one conclusion.)

## 3. The mechanisms that hold people (real science)

- **Curiosity is an information gap.** Loewenstein 1994 (Psychological Bulletin 116): curiosity is "cognitively induced deprivation that arises from the perception of a gap in knowledge"; the gap must be salient and nearly closed. fMRI work shows curiosity lights reward circuitry (Kang et al. 2009, Psychological Science), and Gruber et al. 2014 (Neuron): a high-curiosity state improves memory for what is learned during it, including incidental material, on immediate and next-day tests. **The honest educational case for hooks:** a real question opened at second 1 makes the teaching that follows stick better. Applying lab findings to 30 second videos is an extrapolation; the mechanism is proven, the transfer is inference. [study]
- **Open loops make people stay, not remember.** The 2025 meta-analysis (Ghibellini and Meier, 59 publications) found NO recall advantage for interrupted tasks (weighted ratio 0.99), but a reliable urge to RESUME interrupted tasks (67% resumption). So: promise the payoff early ("the third one saves the most marks"), resolve it at the end, and always close the loop. Anyone citing "Zeigarnik means open loops are memorable" is citing a finding that failed meta-analysis. [study]
- **Cuts and changes re-trigger attention.** Lang's Limited Capacity Model (J. Communication, 2000): structural changes (cuts, movement, new voices, arriving text) fire automatic orienting responses that pull attention back to the screen; her 2000 experiment showed more edits raised arousal, attention and memory, with an overload ceiling. A 2026 synthesis on educational video (Simsek et al., Humanities and Social Sciences Communications, 14 Mar 2026) adds: cuts reduce mind-wandering, and shot length should slow down when the content is hard. The editor folklore of "a change every 2 to 3 seconds" is [practice]; the mechanism is [study]. **So:** script a visual change per beat (punch-in, text card, prop), and give the hardest sentence the calmest screen.
- **Direct address is the best-evidenced technique in this file.** Parasocial theory (Horton and Wohl 1956), confirmed experimentally (Hartmann and Goldhoorn 2011, N=198: lens gaze plus verbal "you" intensifies the parasocial experience), replicated in educational video specifically (Beege et al. 2017, Learning and Instruction: frontal, direct-gaze address improved retention), and in marketing field studies ("you/your" raises involvement, Cruz et al. 2017). TikTok's own data agrees (33% fourth-wall cohort). **So:** every line written to one student, "you and your notes", eyes to lens. This is already Waleed's natural register; protect it. [study] [platform]
- **Fast, enthusiastic delivery wins.** The largest educational-video study ever run (Guo, Kim and Rubin 2014, 6.9 million edX sessions): engagement rose with speaking rate up to 2x, fastest speakers most engaging (corpus mean 156 wpm), with the authors' caveat that rate is a proxy for enthusiasm, so "speeding up an unenthusiastic instructor might not improve engagement". Waleed's measured live rate is 200 to 215 wpm (see voice-fingerprint.md), comfortably in the engaged range; the budget rule (3.0 to 3.4 words per second) is anchored to HIS mouth, not a folklore number. Also from Guo: shortest videos held best, and a talking head beats slides alone. [study]
- **A large share watch muted.** Meta: captions increased video view time by an average of 12% (Feb 2016 test). Verizon Media and Publicis survey (2019, n=5,616): 69% watch with sound off in public, 80% likelier to finish with captions. TikTok: 40% of top view-through ads use text overlays. **So:** the on-screen text layer is written in the script, not left to the edit, and the video must teach with the sound off. [platform] [benchmark]

## 4. Sends, replays and the ending

- Mosseri names sends per reach as a top signal for reaching non-followers; Instagram counts replays in Views; TikTok's formula contains playtime twice. [platform]
- **So every script designs one deliberate forwardable moment** (the line so accurate a student sends it to a mate, or a parent to their child: "this is you") **and an ending that rewards a second watch** (the quote re-landing with new meaning, a list worth re-reading, a close that flows back into the opening line). Loop endings as a technique are [practice]: a rational bet on platform-primary metrics, not a proven multiplier; say it that way.

## 5. The in-house receipts (Content HQ research, 17 July 2026, real metrics)

- **The hook opens the loop; the format underneath cashes it.** Identical hook lines swung from 9,931 to 955,295 plays depending on whether a numbered protocol sat underneath (hook library, howToUse). Never bolt a proven hook onto a shapeless middle.
- **Contrarian exam-technique hooks outrun account size** because ranking is per video: Mr Salles hit 596.8K views on a 26.3K follower account, roughly 23x baseline.
- **Named, copyable systems are the niche's strongest asset:** Study With Kate's named system pulled a 55 percent save-to-like ratio and spawned fan tutorials. Blurt and Fix, the five phases, and the seven archetypes are our named assets.
- **Comments are the engine:** farm questions, answer them as videos, gate assets on comment keywords (verified at scale in the niche; Hannah Kettle's named-asset post ran roughly 10x her typical).
- **The UK exam calendar is the distribution engine; timing beats polish.** Results day (13 Aug 2026) and September restart are the demand spikes.
- **Win the first 3 seconds on three layers at once:** spoken hook, first-frame text mirroring it (search reads it), and the visual.

## 6. The retention checklist (every script, before delivery)

1. First breath = the gap or the promise. No wind-up. Credential in the text layer.
2. The gap is specific and nearly closed for this viewer (Loewenstein), not vague intrigue.
3. Payoff promised early, delivered at the end, loop actually closed.
4. A screen change per beat; calmest screen under the hardest sentence.
5. Written to "you", filmed eyes to lens.
6. On-screen text scripted per beat; works muted.
7. One forwardable moment, named in the "why this should hold" note.
8. An ending worth a second watch.
9. Every number in the script traceable to this file, the hook library, or approved proof points.

## 7. Numbers that are banned because they cannot be traced

- "Average attention span is 8 seconds, less than a goldfish" (BBC traced it to a sourceless marketing chain, Mar 2017).
- "1 in 4 top TikToks are 21 to 34 seconds" / "boosts views by 1.6%" (untraceable in primary form).
- Any viewed-vs-swiped percentage threshold for Shorts virality (the metric is real, the cut-offs are invented).
- "Sends outweigh likes 3 to 5x" and "Instagram heavily weighs the first 3 seconds" (not in Mosseri's verified words).
- The TikTok 63% stat stated as a causal lift ("hooks raise CTR 63%"): it is compositional.
- Zeigarnik as a memory effect (failed meta-analysis, 2025). The resumption urge is the defensible half.
