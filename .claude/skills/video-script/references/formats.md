# The formats: ten proven shapes and how to choose one

The structures come from two places, both already validated: the six format skeletons supplied by Curtis Tilbury and Tom Natras (Viral Toolkit, Waleed's paid content advisors), whose beat labels are a standing rule, respected, never replaced; and four shapes reverse-engineered from reference reels watched frame by frame on 24 July 2026 with real numbers recorded. The worked examples for all ten live in `content/video-scripts/2026-07-24-viral-toolkit-scripts.md`; read a matching example before writing a new script in that format.

## The pacing rule (Waleed's own correction, 24 July 2026, verbatim)

The first batch was flagged too long. The rewrite rules are law:

1. **Front-load. No throat-clearing.** The first spoken line is the hook, never a run-up to it.
2. **One breath per beat.** Name the thing, give the verdict, give at most ONE short reason, move on. If a sentence has two "because" clauses, cut one.
3. **Let the screen carry the structure, the voice carries the energy.** Phase names, scoreboards and captions do the labelling so the talking stays fast.

And the closing instruction: "Short-form tight, not robotic clipped. Read each aloud once and cut anything your mouth trips on."

## Length ladder (from the Content HQ playbook, evidence-backed)

One claim, one payoff, 15 to 90 seconds. Quick exam-technique clips at 15 to 35 seconds; method explainers at 45 to 90 seconds; earn 2 to 3 minute deep-dives later once hooks are proven. Word budget at Waleed's measured rate: seconds x 3.0 to 3.4 (see voice-fingerprint.md).

## The ten formats

**Viral Toolkit skeletons (beat labels are Curtis and Tom's words, keep them):**

1. **Long Listical** | ~90s (they spec ~2 min; ours trimmed to move) | normal clothes, desk. Beats: [proven hook] then [list of ~5 linked points, framed as phases; first couple quick, detail saved for the last couple; phase names intriguing rather than descriptive, per their vague-title rule] then [close]. Use for: a whole system in one video.
2. **Short Listical** | ~25s, very fast, cut every pause | normal clothes. Beats: [proven hook] then [fast list where each item builds tension; a couple of words each] then [close]. Use for: ranked habits, mistakes, myths.
3. **Quote Unpacking** | 45 to 55s, slower | scrubs. Beats: [an interesting sentence where the meaning is not obvious; intrigue first] then [unpack the statement, keeping loops open, revealing slowly] then [the explanation, landing the quote with its real meaning]. Use for: one counterintuitive idea ("If your revision feels good, it's probably not working").
4. **Broll and voice over** | 35 to 45s, VO recorded first, broll filmed to match | scrubs for credibility shots. Beats: [proven hook] then [establish credibility] then [level with the audience, "I know how it feels to..."] then [introduce the list] then [broll of each thing, VO matching screen] then [close]. Use for: habit lists that benefit from being SEEN done.
5. **Method breakdown** | 45 to 55s | scrubs, blank page to gesture at. Beats: [proven hook] then [hype up the method] then [introduce what the method is called] then [the science explaining the problem, not the method] then [how the method works as the solution] then [how to implement it] then [close]. Use for: one named technique (Blurt and Fix, past paper loops, error logs). Named, copyable systems are the strongest asset type in the niche (Study With Kate's named system hit a 55 percent save-to-like ratio).
6. **Rankings** | 30 to 40s, fast, off-camera voice reads statements flat, cut on each statement | normal clothes. Beats: [someone off camera says a statement; you drop it into a category] then [close]. Use for: comment engines; every verdict invites disagreement and the CTA farms the comments into the next episode.

**Reference-reel shapes (source metrics recorded 24 July 2026):**

7. **Caption reel** (bella, Instagram, 127,946 likes) | 6 to 8s of quiet desk b-roll on loop, one static serif title, no talking; the caption is the content (10 tight numbered points). Cheapest film. Use for: brain-science listicles; saves and shares do the distribution.
8. **Ratings carousel** (stharzzzz, TikTok photo post, 84,700 likes, 34,704 saves) | photo carousel, not video; cover slide with identity hook, one slide per method: name, score out of 10, blunt one-line verdict, real study photos only. Use for: save-magnet method ratings.
9. **Red flag, green flag** (Tom Vorselen, Instagram, 18,856 likes, 1,849 comments) | talking head, persistent RED FLAG / GREEN FLAG scoreboard, one habit per title card, instant verdict plus ONE tight science line. 40 to 50s (the 1:44 reference runs long; ours cut to move). Use for: habit verdicts with a part-two loop.
10. **Bad, good, excellent** (sofieestudies, Instagram, 187,837 likes, 14 SECONDS) | persistent three-column scoreboard, a category flashes up, three things drop into slots as named, roughly three words per item, no explanations. 15 to 20s, the highest performer of the four references and the shortest by a mile. Use for: teaching a hierarchy at machine-gun pace; infinitely repeatable with new categories.

## Choosing the format from a one-line idea

- Idea is a **system or multi-step routine** (5 phases, a whole week): Long Listical, or Caption reel if it works as a written list.
- Idea is a **ranked or countable list** (habits, mistakes, myths): Short Listical (spoken, tension build) or Bad/good/excellent (fastest) or Ratings carousel (saves).
- Idea is **one counterintuitive sentence**: Quote Unpacking.
- Idea is **one named method**: Method breakdown.
- Idea **judges habits or invites disagreement**: Rankings or Red flag green flag (both are comment engines; alternate them, they can run weeks apart).
- Idea is **habits shown physically** (desk, page, pen): Broll and voice over.
- Idea is an **announcement or a dated moment** (results day, a free asset going live): Short Listical or straight-to-camera within the Method breakdown opening beats, plus the "[Named free asset] is up!" hook pattern from the hook library.

Two formats can carry the same idea; pick the shorter one first. A proven idea can be refilmed later in a second format.

## Production notes (apply to every script)

- Phone, low camera angle, static shot. Pen in hand, notes and textbook around, as if mid-session.
- Off-the-cuff energy: know the beats, not the exact words. Record every script twice; the second take is nearly always the looser, better one.
- Scrubs for doctor-framed scripts (quote unpacking, method breakdowns, credibility broll); normal clothes for peer-level listicals and rankings.
- Captions on everything (most viewers watch muted). On-screen hook text visible within the first second, and the spoken hook mirrored in the first-frame text layer so TikTok search can read it.
- Text overlays: one line, 5 to 8 words, plain type with contrast, no panel, and NEVER in the bottom strip (the mobile caption overlay sits there).
- Filming batches: fast formats first while energy is high, then the long ones, then VO and b-roll pickups last while the desk is still messy and real.
