Welcome to this week's Sunday Session. It's the email where I share one thing school never really teaches you, that you can use straight away.

You're probably a couple of weeks into the new school year now. That means your first proper tests are coming up soon.

And if you're in Year 13, those early tests matter even more. Your teachers use them as evidence when they set your predicted grades, so every mark counts.

So this week is about the most frustrating marks to lose: the ones you already know, but run out of time to write down.

I see it all the time. A student knows the content, sits the paper, and the last 6 mark question is still blank when time runs out.

It almost never happens because they're slow. It happens because nobody ever showed them how to plan their time.

Now, as a doctor, I think about exams a lot like triage in A&E.

When the department is busy, you don't spend forty minutes on a sprained ankle while someone with chest pain is waiting. You decide where your time matters most, and you stick to it.

Exams work exactly the same way. Here's how to set it up in about two minutes.

**Step 1: Work out your time per mark.**

When the exam starts, divide the total minutes by the total marks. For example, a 2 hour paper worth 100 marks gives you 1.2 minutes per mark.

For most A-level papers, it comes out at a little over a minute per mark.

**Step 2: Give every question a time budget.**

Multiply the marks by your time per mark. So a 6 mark question gets about 7 minutes, and a 2 mark question gets just over 2.

Then check yourself halfway. When half the time has gone, you want to be about halfway through the marks.

If you're behind, you'll know straight away, instead of finding out with ten minutes left.

**Step 3: Use the move on rule.**

If you've spent double a question's budget and you're still stuck, write what you can. Even one line can pick up a mark. Then put a star next to it and move on.

You can always come back at the end. Leaving a 2 marker half done so you can go and get a 6 marker isn't giving up, it's just good maths!

Watching the clock like this can feel a bit strange at first. But after two or three timed papers it becomes automatic, and exams feel a lot calmer because you always know if you're on track.

**Tonight, try this:** take a past paper for your hardest subject. Work out your time per mark, then write a time budget next to every question. It only takes about ten minutes.
