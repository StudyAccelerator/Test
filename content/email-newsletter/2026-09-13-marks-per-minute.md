Welcome to this week's Sunday Session, the email where I share one thing school never really teaches you, that you can use straight away.

You're probably a couple of weeks into the new school year now, so the first proper tests are just around the corner. And if you're in Year 13, those early tests are a big part of the evidence your teachers use when they set your predicted grades, so every mark in them genuinely counts.

That's why this week is all about the most frustrating marks to lose: the ones you already know, but never get the time to write down.

I see it all the time. A student knows the content, sits the paper, and the last 6 mark question is still blank when time runs out. It almost never happens because they're slow. It happens because nobody ever showed them how to budget their time.

Now, as a doctor, I think about exams a lot like triage in A&E. When the department is busy, you don't spend forty minutes on a sprained ankle while someone with chest pain is waiting. You decide where your time is worth the most and you stick to it. Exams work exactly the same way, and setting it up takes about two minutes.

**Step 1: Work out your number.** As soon as the exam starts, divide the total minutes by the total marks. A 2 hour paper worth 100 marks gives you 1.2 minutes per mark. That's your number, and for most A-level papers it comes out at a little over a minute.

**Step 2: Give every question a budget.** Multiply its marks by your number, so a 6 mark question gets about 7 minutes and a 2 marker gets just over 2. Then use halfway as your checkpoint: when half the time has gone, you want to be about halfway through the marks. If you're behind, you'll know straight away, instead of finding out with ten minutes left.

**Step 3: Use the move on rule.** If you've spent double a question's budget and you're still stuck, write whatever you can (even one line can pick up a mark), put a star next to it and move on. You can always come back at the end. Leaving a 2 marker half done so you can go and get a 6 marker isn't giving up, it's just good maths!

Watching the clock like this can feel a bit strange at first, but after two or three timed papers it becomes automatic, and exams start to feel a lot calmer because you always know whether you're on track.

**Tonight, try this:** grab a past paper for your hardest subject, work out your number and write a budget next to every question. It takes about ten minutes, and you'll walk into your next test knowing exactly how to spend your time.
