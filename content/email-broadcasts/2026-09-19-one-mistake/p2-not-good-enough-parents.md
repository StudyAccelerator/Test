Hi {$name},

I want to tell you a quick story today, because it changed how I think about every grade a student brings home.

**Year 7.**

I didn't transition very well into secondary school. Biology in particular wasn't going well for me in class, and my class tests were coming back with poor grades.

So my biology teacher, Miss Booth, asked me what I wanted to do when I grew up. I told her I wanted to be a doctor and study medicine.

She gave me this look. Then she told me I should maybe think about some other options.

At 11 years old, I was essentially told I wasn't good enough to study medicine.

Now I'm a doctor.

**This isn't about teachers being wrong.**

It's about the phrase itself. There is no such thing as being good enough.

Here's what actually happens. A child gets a grade they're not happy with, and the quiet voice in their head says the same thing every time.

"I'm not smart enough. I just need to try harder and spend more time."

But it was never about being good enough. It's that nobody ever taught them what works best for them.

**Same class, same content, same expectations.**

At school every child sits in the same room and goes through the same curriculum. Every one of them is expected to do well from it.

But there's no one size fits all with studying.

As a doctor, I see this every day. Two patients can come in with the same diagnosis and still need different treatment, because they don't present the same way.

Studying works exactly the same way. The same revision method doesn't work for every child, and nobody at school is checking which one works for yours.

**So if your child isn't at the grades they want.**

Please don't let them decide they're not smart enough. How can they be expected to do well when nobody has taught them what to do?

That's a fixable problem. Being "not good enough" was never the issue, because it was never true.

Best wishes,

Waleed
