A few weeks into term, the first class tests start coming back. So today I want to talk about the mistake I see most often when a result disappoints.

I made it myself for years.

**My own fallback.**

When I was doing my A-levels and a class test didn't go well, my answer was always the same. Work more.

Spend more time revising. More time going through the textbook. More practice papers.

I was hoping that repetition would fix it. And my grades did improve, but very slowly, and it cost me a lot of evenings.

**The mistake I see now.**

Over the last 6 years I've worked with over 1,000 students. The most common thing I hear is this: "I'm working really hard, but my grade doesn't match the effort."

And the fallback is nearly always the same. A one to one subject tutor.

Here's the problem. In most cases it isn't the content or the understanding that's costing the marks.

It's how you prepare for the exam. Whether you have the right study system, and the right exam technique.

School spends its time on what to learn. Almost nobody teaches you how to learn.

**The test of that idea.**

If one to one tutoring was always the answer, every student with a tutor would be getting A stars. They aren't.

Most of the students I work with have already tried a subject tutor. They're still not getting consistent A stars, because the tutor was fixing the wrong thing.

As a doctor, if a patient keeps coming back with the same problem, I don't just double the dose. I ask why the treatment isn't working.

**What I'd do instead.**

It's 20 to 30 minutes with me. I'll show you specifically where your marks are going, and how to fix it. Not more hours and more repetition, which mostly leads to stress and burnout without changing the grade.
