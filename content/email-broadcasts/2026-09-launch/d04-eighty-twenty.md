Hi {$name},

There's a rule that shows up everywhere once you know to look for it: 80 percent of the results come from 20 percent of the effort. Economists found it in wealth, businesses find it in customers, and I see it every single week in revision.

Because here's what I've noticed across more than a thousand students: most of a struggling student's week is low-yield. Re-reading, highlighting, making notes look right, watching someone else explain a topic you'd already understood. It all counts as revision, it all takes energy, and almost none of it turns into marks.

Meanwhile the high-yield 20 percent is always the same short list: testing yourself with the book closed, coming back to topics before they fade, doing real exam questions under time pressure, and marking them against the actual mark scheme. The activities that feel harder, and score more.

So this week, do the sort. Take your last full week of revision and put every hour into one of two columns. High-yield: did it force me to produce something from memory, or face a real exam question? Low-yield: everything else. Be honest. Most students who do this find the split is something like one hour high-yield to four hours low-yield, which means the same grades were available for a fraction of the time, or better grades for the same time.

Top students don't do more. They do things better. They spend the same hours, usually fewer to be honest, on the small set of activities that actually move the needle.

Now, sorting the hours is the WHAT, and you can do it yourself tonight with two columns on a page. Rebuilding your week around the high-yield 20 percent, for your subjects, your leak and your real timetable, is the part where a second pair of eyes helps.
