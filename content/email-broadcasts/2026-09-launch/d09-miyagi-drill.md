Today I'm going to give you the single most useful exam skills drill I know, complete, free, inside this email. And then I'll tell you its honest limit, because you deserve both halves.

**The examiner drill.** Sit one section of a past paper in your priority subject under real conditions: real timing, about a minute per mark, book closed, phone in another room. Then mark it with the official scheme, and mark it harshly. Not "I basically said that". Either your wording earns the mark or it doesn't.

Then, for every dropped mark, write one letter in a log. K if you genuinely didn't know it. R if you knew it but couldn't produce it in the moment. W if you knew it, wrote it, and worded it wrong. T if timing or misreading cost you.

Run that once a week and something changes: revision stops being guesswork. Your letters tell you exactly where your next hour should go. A pile of K says learn content. A pile of R says test yourself more. A pile of W and T, and that's the most common result by far for students stuck at a B, says your problem was never knowledge. It's conversion, and conversion trains fast.

That's the drill. It costs nothing, and if you run it weekly from now to mocks, it will genuinely move you. I mean that.

**Now the limit.** You can't fully mark yourself. The wording gaps you can see, you fix. But the ones you can't see are precisely the ones that keep costing you, because your version sounds right to you. That's not a discipline problem, it's a blind spot, and blind spots don't fix themselves from the inside.
