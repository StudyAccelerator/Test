It's the first Sunday of term, so let me tell you the thing about this term that nobody spells out until it's too late.

Your predicted grades, the ones that go on your UCAS form and frame every university conversation you'll have, get set and reviewed over the next couple of months. Teachers don't pull them from thin air. They look at the evidence in front of them: your early Year 13 tests, your classwork, your mocks. Which means the next twelve or so weeks aren't just another stretch of school. They're the weeks your paper record gets written.

Here's why that changes what you should do now rather than later. A student who fixes their revision method or their exam technique in September gives their teachers three months of better work to look at, exactly when it counts. The same student making the same fix in January still helps their real exams, but the predictions are already written by then, and with them some of the university doors that were open in October. Same fix, same effort, completely different value, purely because of the date.

I can't promise what any teacher decides, and I never will. But predictions follow evidence, and autumn is when the evidence gets made.
