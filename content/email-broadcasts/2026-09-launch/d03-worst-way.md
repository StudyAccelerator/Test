Hi {$name},

Let me tell you the worst way to revise for A-levels. I can be confident about this one, because it's also the most popular way.

Open your notes. Read through them. Highlight the important bits. Maybe copy them out again, neater this time. Close the notes feeling like you've done a solid evening's work.

That's it. That's the method most students use for most of their revision. And here's the uncomfortable truth about it: it produces almost nothing.

Here's why. When you re-read something, it becomes familiar, and familiarity feels exactly like knowing it. Same warm feeling of "yes, I've got this". But the exam never asks whether you recognise the cardiac cycle when it's in front of you. It gives you a blank box and asks you to produce it from nothing. Recognising and producing are two completely different skills, and re-reading only trains the first one.

That's the trap: the worst methods feel the most productive. Three hours of re-reading feels like a proper session. Ten minutes of testing yourself with the book closed feels awkward and slightly humbling, because you keep finding out what you don't know. So everyone gravitates to the method that feels good and scores nothing.

Want proof, tonight, in ten minutes? Take a topic you "revised" last week. Blank page, book closed, write down everything you know about it. If the page fills up, your method's working and you can ignore me. If it doesn't, you've just watched the leak happen in real time. Uncomfortable, I know, but it's better to find out on a quiet night in September than in an exam hall in June.
