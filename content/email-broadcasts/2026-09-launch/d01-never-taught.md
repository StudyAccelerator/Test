I want you to think about someone who's revising every evening. Maybe they have a tutor. They're paying attention in class, they're doing all the right things as far as anyone can tell. And they're still sitting at a B.

Does that sound familiar? Maybe it sounds like you.

Here's what almost everyone concludes: they're not smart enough, or they're not working hard enough. And both conclusions point to the same miserable fix: more hours, more pressure, more of the same.

I've worked with over 1,000 A-level students, and I can tell you the real reason is neither of those. It's this: schools teach you what to learn, but nobody teaches you how to perform in exams. And those are two completely different things.

Think about what an exam actually tests. It doesn't ask whether you attended the lessons. It doesn't ask whether your notes are neat. It hands you a blank answer box, under time pressure, and asks you to produce exactly what the mark scheme rewards, in its wording, in its order. That's a skill. It has its own techniques, its own training, its own practice schedule. And for most students, nobody has ever taught them a single hour of it.

So the hardworking student at a B isn't failing. They're playing a game nobody explained the rules of, and doing remarkably well considering.

Why am I telling you this? Because the fix for "not smart enough" doesn't exist, and the fix for "never taught" absolutely does. But it starts with knowing which part of the game is costing you, because it's different for everyone. Some students lose marks in memory, some in method, some in the exam room itself.
