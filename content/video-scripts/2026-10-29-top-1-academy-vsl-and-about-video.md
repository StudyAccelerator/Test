## Script 1: the Loom VSL (talk over the deck)

Target: 7 to 8 minutes. Spoken track about 1,550 words. Record in one take if you can; the deck carries the structure, so a stumble is fine.

**Slide 1. Title**

> Amazing. Thank you for watching this. I'll keep it short, because you've got better things to do, and so have I, to be honest.
>
> This is the Top 1% Academy. Weekly live coaching on how to study, with me. In the next few minutes I'm gonna tell you who I am, exactly what happens inside it every week, what it costs, and how you join. Okay?

**Slide 2. Who I am**

> So, who am I. I'm Dr Waleed. I'm an NHS doctor. I'm not much older than you, even though I may look it.
>
> And here's the honest version of my story. In Year 12 I was average. Not failing, not flying. I worked really, really hard and I was still sitting on Bs. And the thing is, I was doing everything school told me to do.
>
> Then I rebuilt how I studied. Same hours, completely different method. I finished in the top 10% of my year and I got into medicine.
>
> And for the last six years I've taught that method to over a thousand A-level students. That's what this Academy is. It's that system, taught live, every single week.

**Slide 3. The problem**

> Now, I want you to think about someone who's revising every evening. Maybe they've got a tutor. They're paying attention in class. They're doing all the right things. And they're still sitting on a B.
>
> Does that sound familiar? Maybe it sounds like you. Or if you're a parent watching, maybe it sounds like your son or daughter.
>
> Here's what I've learned from a thousand students. It isn't because they're not smart enough. It's not because they're not working hard. It's because schools teach you what to learn, but no one actually teaches you how to learn it. And those are two completely different things, okay?

**Slide 4. Hard work isn't the problem**

> So what do top students actually do differently? And I promise, it's not more hours. Top students don't do more. They do things better.
>
> Four things. One, they know what to prioritise. Which subject gets the hours this term, and why. Two, they learn a topic once so it sticks, instead of relearning it three times before the exam. Three, they work the high-yield stuff. The small slice of each subject that carries most of the marks. And four, they never lose the same mark twice.
>
> That's it. That's the whole system. And every one of those four is a skill you can be taught. Nobody's born with it. I certainly wasn't.

**Slide 5. What the Academy is**

> Okay, so what is the Academy. It's two live sessions a week with me on Zoom. Every single one is recorded. And between the sessions there's a community where I answer every question in public. Let me walk you through a week.

**Slide 6. Wednesday**

> Wednesday, seven till nine. The first hour, I teach. One part of the study system, properly, with a worked example from a real subject. So one week it's how to revise a topic so it actually sticks. The next week it's how to read a mark scheme like an examiner.
>
> And then the second hour is the Hot Seat. This is my favourite bit. You post your problem before the session. Your actual problem. "I can't get past six out of ten on the six markers in Biology." Or "Chemistry's my weakest subject but my Maths mock is in three weeks, which wins?" And we fix it live, in front of everyone. Because the student watching has usually got the exact same problem, right?

**Slide 7. Sunday, The Marks Clinic**

> Sunday, ten till twelve, is The Marks Clinic. And this is the thing you can't get from a recording or a textbook.
>
> You upload a piece of real work before the weekend. A timed paper you got marked. An essay. Your week plan. And I go through it on screen. Where did the marks go, what did the examiner actually want, what do you change next time.
>
> And here's the thing. You learn as much from everyone else's paper as from your own. Because the mistakes are the same mistakes, in every subject, every year. Once you've seen them in someone else's work, you start spotting them in yours before the examiner does.

**Slide 8. Between sessions**

> Between the sessions you've got 24/7 access to me in the community. Message me any time, I reply the same day, and I answer in public so everyone gets the benefit. Every session goes into the replay library the same night, so if you miss one you've lost nothing. And your Start Here course is the free workshop, split into four lessons, so you can go back through it at your own pace.

**Slide 9. Who it's for**

> So who's it for. Year 12 first, because Year 12 is the year to get this right. Year 13, you're very welcome, and the Clinic was built with you in mind. Any subjects. Because this isn't subject tutoring. I'm not teaching you Chemistry. I'm teaching you how to study, and you run it on every subject you take.
>
> And it's for students who are keen to improve their grades and want someone to show them how. Every week. Not once.

**Slide 10. Does it work?**

> Now the obvious question. Does it work?
>
> Over six years I've worked with more than a thousand A-level students. On average, my students jump two grades within four months. And 96% of my students get their first-choice university offer.
>
> I'll let the students say the rest.

**Slide 11. Testimonials**

> [Pause and let the three quotes sit on screen for a few seconds. Then:] What I love about these is nobody's talking about the content. They're all talking about how we work. Straight into the questions. Applying it, not repeating it. Even the best students finding something to fix. That's the Academy.

**Slide 12. What families usually pay**

> Okay. Let's talk about money, because I want to be completely straight with you.
>
> Most families, when a subject's going wrong, get a tutor. One tutor, one subject. Round about fifty pounds an hour, two hours a week. So that's a hundred pounds a week. Four hundred pounds a month.
>
> And most students aren't struggling in one subject. If you've got two or three subjects that need help, that's eight hundred to twelve hundred pounds a month. Every month.
>
> And here's the bit that bothers me as someone who does subject tuition myself. All that money only ever covers the content of that one subject. Nobody's teaching you how to study. So you end up paying the same again next year.
