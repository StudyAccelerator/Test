# Top 1% Mentorship: the honest read on running this at scale

*Written 10 September 2026, before student number one. This is the answer to "what breaks first, what do I change while I still have two students, and what do I have to give up".*

## The arithmetic first

What the current offer costs you per student per week, once the student fills in the scorecard and you work from the runsheet:

| Item | Minutes a week |
|---|---|
| The session | 60 |
| Reading the scorecard and writing the notes | 15 |
| The monthly parent update, spread across the month | 5 |
| Messages on the number, if bounded by the contact rules | 15 |
| **Total, bounded** | **about 95** |

So roughly an hour and a half per student per week. On a foundation-year rota that averages around 48 hours with nights and weekends, the picture is:

| Students | Hours a week | Revenue a month | What it feels like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3 | £600 | An evening. Easy. |
| 5 | 8 | £1,500 | A full day a week, every week, on top of the rota. The site's current cap, and roughly the real one for this shape. |
| 10 | 16 | £3,000 | A second job. Sessions start slipping on nights weeks and the slips are what students remember. |
| 20 | 32 | £6,000 | Impossible alongside medicine, and the personal number alone would be unmanageable before the calls were. |

That table is the whole argument. The offer as sold today (weekly hour, your personal number, fully bespoke) has a ceiling of about five students, and the site already says five. The question is whether five is the business or the pilot. Everything below assumes it is the pilot.

## What breaks first, in order

1. **The number.** The message channel breaks before the calls do, because it has no ceiling. Two students texting at 11pm before a test is fine. Five doing it in a nights week is where you stop replying, and a student who is left on read by the person they pay £300 a month to is a student who quietly starts questioning the value. It breaks at four or five students, and it breaks on a bad week rather than gradually. The contact rules in file 03 are the fix, and they only work if they are set on day one, which is why the welcome message carries them.
2. **Your calendar against the rota.** Weekly slots that have to move every time the rota changes produce missed sessions, and a missed session with nothing in its place is the single biggest retention leak in a programme like this. It breaks between five and eight students. The fix is mechanical: sessions booked four weeks ahead in the scheduler, and the missed-session protocol (a recorded ten-minute scorecard review within 48 hours), so the student never experiences a silent week.
3. **Your memory between sessions.** With two students you will remember everything. At six you will mix up whose December test is when, and the student will notice. The session-notes template with its "for a second coach reading this cold" line is the fix, and it is also what makes the third stage below possible.
4. **The parent update.** It is the strongest retention lever you have for the person actually paying, and it is the first thing that gets skipped in a busy month. It survives only as a ten-minute template filled from the scorecard page, never as a fresh essay.
5. **Quality drift.** Without a written spine, student three gets a different programme from student one, and by student six you cannot say what the mentorship is. The runsheets are the fix, and the "deviate if" lines are how it stays personal without becoming improvised.

Notice that none of these is "I cannot teach the sessions". You can. What breaks is everything around the sessions, and every one of those things is fixable with two students and very hard to fix with ten.

## What to change now, while there are two students

These cost nothing and are much harder to introduce later.

1. **Set the contact rules on day one.** The number stays, bounded: two-line questions, evening replies within a working day, nothing after 10pm, anything bigger goes on the scorecard. Students accept rules they are given at the start and resent rules introduced in month three.
2. **The student does the prep.** The scorecard is filled in before every session by the student, not reconstructed by you during it. This moves the preparation off you entirely and, more importantly, makes the student the owner of their own evidence. Say it in the welcome message and enforce it in session 2.
3. **Record every session and write the notes the same evening.** The notes are your memory now and a second coach's manual later.
4. **Book four weeks ahead, and use the missed-session protocol from the first miss.** Never let a silent week happen, even once, because the student learns from the first one what a missed week means.
5. **Build the teach library as you go.** Twelve of the runsheets carry a teach that is the same for every student (sessions 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 13, 16, 18 and 19). Record each one once as a ten to fifteen minute video the week you first deliver it, so that by March you have the mentorship's classroom. The Study Series already covers some of this ground, so several will be edits rather than new recordings. This is the single biggest lever for scale, because it turns the one-to-one hour from "teach plus review" into "review plus personalise", which is the part only a person can do.
6. **Write the cap down.** Under the current shape it is five, and the site already says so. Decide whether the first five are the pilot for the shape below. If they are, students six onwards sign to the new shape and the first five keep what they were promised for as long as they stay.
7. **Grandfather the first two.** They were sold a weekly hour and your number. Honour it for their whole time on the programme. Changing terms on your first two customers is a worse story than any capacity problem.

## What the offer becomes at each size

The price stays at £300 a month throughout. What changes is where your hours go, and the honest framing to the parent is that they are paying for the outcome and the system, not for a fixed number of your minutes.

**Two to five students (now to roughly Christmas): fully one-to-one, as sold.** Weekly hour, bounded number, monthly parent update. You use these students to prove the spine, record the teach library, and find out which "deviate if" lines fire most often. Your hours: three to eight a week.

**Six to twelve students: the two-stage shape.** Every new student gets the weekly hour with you for their first eight sessions (phases one and two, the diagnosis and the install, which genuinely need you). From session nine they move to a fortnightly 45-minute one-to-one with you plus a weekly 60-minute group session, the Mentorship Room, for every mentorship student at once: the week's teach from the library rotation, then hot seats where students bring their scorecards and you diagnose in public, which teaches everyone in the room. The number stays, bounded. Parent updates stay monthly. Your hours at twelve students, steady state with two in their first eight weeks: two hours of new-student sessions, about four hours of fortnightly one-to-ones, one hour of Mentorship Room, and two to three hours of notes, updates and messages. Nine to ten hours a week for £3,600 a month, and it holds on a nights week because the group session is one fixed slot and the fortnightly calls have slack. What the student gives up: the weekly hour after month two. What they gain: the room, which for a sixteen-year-old is often worth more than the call.

**Thirteen to thirty students: a second person.** The fortnightly one-to-ones move to a coach: a recent A-level top performer or medical student, paid around £20 to £25 an hour, working from the runsheets and the session notes, whom you train by having them sit in on your calls for a month. You keep the first eight sessions of every student, the Mentorship Room, the monthly reviews (which become a twenty-minute call with the student and you, monthly, on top of the coach's fortnightlies) and the parent updates. At twenty students your hours are around six a week; the coach's around ten, costing about £1,000 a month against £6,000 of revenue. What you give up: being in every conversation. What the student gets: more contact, not less. This stage only works if the notes and runsheets are already good, which is why stages one and two matter.

**Beyond thirty: cohorts and the community.** Monthly intakes run as cohorts of five to eight through the first eight weeks together (the group version of phase one and two, with the diagnosis still one-to-one), the Skool community you have already designed becomes the asynchronous layer between sessions, and two or three coaches carry the fortnightlies. At that size the mentorship is a programme you direct rather than deliver, which is the same move the summer page already made with subject tutors. That is a 2027 decision and it depends on the numbers from the earlier stages, not on this document.

## What you have to give up

- **The number as an unbounded promise.** It becomes a bounded channel with a reply window from day one, and at stage three it is partly the coach's channel. The site's "24/7 access to Dr Waleed" line cannot survive even stage one honestly.
- **Being in every weekly conversation after month two.** From stage two the weekly touch is the room, and the one-to-one is fortnightly. This is the hard one emotionally and it is the only one that moves the ceiling.
- **"Fully customised" meaning fully improvised.** The spine is standard; the deviations are the customisation. That is true of good medicine as well, and it is what makes the programme deliverable by anyone but you.
- **Students who need subject content.** They go to the Subject Accelerators. The mentorship stays method-only, which is what you are good at and what scales.
- **Scarcity as the only sales lever.** "Invite only, limited to five" is true today and stops being true at stage two. Cohorts of five per monthly intake keeps the scarcity honest if you want to keep it.
