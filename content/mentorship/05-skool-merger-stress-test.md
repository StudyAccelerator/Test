# Stress test: the Skool mentorship and the recorded subject layer

*Written 11 September 2026 in answer to Waleed's proposal: move the £300 mentorship into a Skool community (fortnightly 1:1, weekly group teaching, daily answers in the community instead of his phone number), and replace the live Subject Accelerator cohorts with tutor-recorded 20-hour courses per subject per year at £100 a month per subject, with weekly tutor Q&A later.*

## The verdict in four lines

1. The Skool-shaped mentorship is the right move and it is the two-stage shape from file 04 with a better home. Do it, with three conditions: the room does not open until six paying students exist, every student's first eight sessions stay one-to-one, and the daily answering happens in public threads in a fixed window, never in DMs.
2. Build the recorded subject layer, but do not sell it as £100 a month per subject on its own. At that price it is compared against Up Learn, MyEdSpace and free YouTube and it loses on paper every time. Sell it inside the mentorship as a Plus tier, where the doctor and the system are the product and the recordings are the knowledge layer underneath.
3. The tutor economics as proposed underpay the recordings and overpay the Q&A. Pay more per finished hour with a written IP agreement, pilot one subject-year first, and run tutor Q&A asynchronously until a subject has four or more paying students.
4. This is a chance to simplify, not to add. Today the ladder has Study Series, Study Accelerator, Mentorship, three Subject Accelerator bundles and the Summer Accelerator. The version that works has two paid things: Mentorship and Mentorship Plus. Everything else becomes classroom content inside them.

## Part one: the mentorship in Skool

### What is right about it

- It is the scaling shape. Weekly one-to-one caps at about five students (file 04). Fortnightly one-to-one plus a weekly room plus daily public answers holds at twenty to thirty with you alone, because answers in public teach everyone and hot seats in the room are the diagnosis done once for many.
- Public answering is a better product than the phone number, not a worse one. A student's question answered in a thread becomes a searchable library, other students learn from it, and you answer once instead of five times. It is also the safeguarding-sound version: sixteen-year-olds and a doctor's personal number is a worse arrangement than a room with parents' knowledge and a written record.
- Your time becomes bounded. Twenty to thirty minutes a day in the community at a fixed time, one room session a week, fortnightly calls with slack. That survives a nights week; weekly calls do not.
- The room solves the month-three dip better than a call does. A student who sees another student's scorecard line going up believes it more than when you say it.

### Where it breaks

- **The room needs bodies.** A weekly group session with two students is an awkward call, and with three it is still not a room. Do not open it below six. Until then, the two students get what they were sold, weekly one-to-one, and the room launches when the number exists. Sell the Skool shape from student three onwards; deliver one-to-one until six.
- **The first eight sessions cannot be a group.** The diagnosis, the hours audit and the install (phases one and two of the runsheets) need you and one student. A student who goes straight into a room in week one gets the teach without the diagnosis, which is the Study Series at £119, not the mentorship at £300. Keep sessions one to eight weekly and one-to-one, then move to fortnightly plus the room.
- **The spine and the room do not line up.** Students join in different months, so the room cannot follow the 26-week spine for everyone. The fix is the teach library on a twelve-week rotation (the twelve standard teaches in file 04) plus hot seats, while the fortnightly one-to-one follows each student's spine. A student who has already had a teach in one-to-one gets it again in the room as the second pass, which is fine and is how spacing works.
- **Value perception at £300 a month.** A parent can compute "fortnightly 45 minutes with Dr Waleed is 90 minutes a month, that's £200 an hour". The answer is never hours. The page has to sell the deliverables: the diagnosis, the scorecard, the monthly review page, the parent update, the room, answers within a day, the library, and the four-month commitment to see the grade move. Your own framing does the work: three subjects of one-to-one tutoring is £300 a week and covers tier one; this is £300 a month and covers all four tiers.
- **It collides with the Skool plan you already have.** The August pack (content/skool/) designs a free community plus a £29 to £39 "Locked In" tier with a hard rule of no one-to-one anything. A £300 mentorship inside Skool with fortnightly calls is a different product. Two paid tiers on the same platform at £29 and £300 will confuse the market and cannibalise the top one. My recommendation: drop the £29 tier for now. Skool becomes a free community for lead generation and a single private paid group, the mentorship, at £300. The £29 tier can return later as a step between free and £300 if the funnel shows the gap.
- **Skool's terms for under-18s.** The August pack flagged it and it is still unchecked: confirm at signup that sixteen and seventeen-year-olds can be members, and get parental consent in the joining questions regardless.
- **Skool costs money.** Roughly $99 a month per group plus card fees on payments taken through it. Trivial at ten students, not at two. Another reason not to open it before six.
- **Community churn is real.** Paid communities typically lose a meaningful share of members every month once the novelty fades. The one-to-one anchor and the four-month minimum are what hold the number. Never let the mentorship become "a community with occasional calls", because that is what churns.

## Part two: the recorded subject layer

### What is right about it

- It removes the cohort-fill problem. One student signed up for the September Subject Accelerator is the fill problem in one number. Recordings sell any day, need no minimum, and cost nothing to deliver after production.
- It matches the audit's conclusion: £1 million is never a solo teaching diary, and tutor-delivered content that runs without you is the fourth asset the business needs.
- The four tiers argue for the bundle. Tier one, knowledge, has to come from somewhere. A mentorship student whose stuck-topic test says "understanding gap" needs a place to send them that is not "ask your teacher". A classroom of your own tutors' walkthroughs is exactly that place.
- Family economics get strong. A student on the mentorship plus two subjects is £500 a month, £4,500 across a nine-month year. That is the audit's premium annual shape (Shape B) priced above its range, with proof to do the selling.

### Where it breaks

**The price positioning.** £100 a month per subject is £1,200 a year for twenty hours of recordings. Up Learn is about £950 a year for three subjects with an adaptive engine (audit figure). MyEdSpace runs live weekly classes for roughly £25 to £30 a month. PMT, Seneca and the big YouTube channels cost nothing. A parent shopping the recorded course on its own sees a doctor's tutoring brand charging two to three times the technology leader for a fraction of the content. It is not a fair comparison, because your recordings are exam-craft walkthroughs by tutors who got the grades, but it is the comparison they will make, because standalone pricing invites it. Inside a bundle with your mentorship the comparison never happens, because nothing else on the market is "a doctor rebuilds how you study, plus your subjects taught to the mark scheme".

**The promise.** "A prerecorded programme covering the syllabus" cannot be true at twenty hours a year. A-level content is roughly 180 guided learning hours per subject per year. Twenty hours is about a tenth. What twenty hours can be, and what your tutors already do live, is the high-yield topics taught to the mark scheme with exam questions. Call it that on the page (the content rules require it) and the product is honest and strong. Call it syllabus coverage and the first parent who checks will tell every Facebook group.

**It reprices the live product to zero.** The live twelve-week Subject Accelerator is £339 for one subject. A recorded course at £100 a month costs the same over three months. If both exist, the message is that live small-group teaching from a specialist is worth nothing over a recording. Either the live cohort retires (honestly, because it is not filling) or it becomes the premium layer above the recordings, never the same price.

**The recordings will be consumed in weeks, then churn.** Twenty hours at two or three hours a week is gone in eight weeks. The realistic lifetime revenue per standalone subscriber is £200 to £300, not £1,200, and every month after the content is finished is a cancellation conversation. That is fine if the subscription is an add-on to a mentorship that holds the student for the year. It is a poor business if it is the product.

**The tutor pay does not work for the recordings.** £1,000 for a twenty-hour finished year is £50 per finished hour. Recording, preparing and editing a good hour of teaching takes three to five hours, so the tutor earns £10 to £17 an hour, below what you pay them to teach live. Expect refusals, or twenty hours of unedited Zoom. Pay £75 to £100 per finished hour (£1,500 to £2,000 per subject-year), with a written agreement that the recordings and the rights belong to A-Level Accelerators, that they are not published elsewhere, and what happens if the tutor leaves. Without the IP line you are renting content you think you own.

**The tutor Q&A pay works the other way.** £50 a week per tutor is £200 a month per subject-year track. Biology, Chemistry and Maths across Year 12 and Year 13 is six tracks, £1,200 a month fixed, before a single student pays. At £100 a month you need thirteen subject subscriptions just to cover the Q&A. Run Q&A asynchronously first (the tutor answers a subject thread in the community twice a week on a small retainer, say £100 a month), and switch a track to a live weekly Q&A only when it has four or more paying students. And a live weekly Q&A with a specialist tutor is live teaching, which is what the Accelerator was; bundled at £100 it undercuts the live product again.

**The production timeline.** Six subject-year recordings at twenty hours each is 120 finished hours from tutors who are teaching and, for some of them, studying. That is a term or two of work, not "the very near future". And the Year 13 recordings are the urgent ones (exams in May), which is the harder content to produce well quickly.

**The September obligation.** One student has paid for a live Subject Accelerator starting 13 September. They get what they bought or an honest conversation and an upgrade. The nineteen draft campaigns in MailerLite carry a full promo week for that cohort; if the cohort is not running, none of those can be scheduled.

## What I would do instead

**One product family, two tiers, everything else inside it.**

| | Mentorship | Mentorship Plus |
|---|---|---|
| Price | £300 a month, four-month minimum | £450 a month, four-month minimum |
| One-to-one with Dr Waleed | Weekly for the first eight sessions, then fortnightly | Same |
| The room | Weekly group session, teach rotation plus hot seats (opens at six members) | Same |
| Answers | Daily, in public threads, within one working day | Same, plus subject threads answered by the specialist tutors |
| Method library | The Study Series and Study Accelerator teaches, as the classroom | Same |
| Subject classroom | | All their subjects: the tutors' high-yield walkthroughs, Year 12 and Year 13 |
| Tutor Q&A | | Async in the subject threads; live weekly per track once it has four students |
| Reviews and parents | Monthly review page, monthly parent update | Same |

The family maths on the page: three subjects of one-to-one tutoring at two hours a week each is about £300 a week and covers content only. Mentorship Plus is £450 a month and covers all four tiers in every subject. Over a nine-month year that is £4,050 against roughly £10,800.

If you would rather keep subjects a la carte, price the add-on at £50 a month per subject inside the mentorship only, never sold on its own. Per-subject pricing at any level invites the Up Learn comparison, which is why the bundle is better.

**What happens to the existing products.** The Study Series (£119) and Study Accelerator (£499) become the method classroom inside both tiers; their pages retire or redirect to the mentorship page. The live Subject Accelerator either retires (the honest reading of one sign-up) or becomes a premium live layer at its current price for tracks that fill. The Summer Accelerator stays a separate summer product. The £29 Skool tier waits.

**Sequencing.**

1. Now to Christmas: the two students run on the one-to-one plan exactly as sold. You record the twelve standard teaches as you deliver them, which is the method classroom. Do not open Skool for two people.
2. From student three: sell the Mentorship as described above (the page changes are listed in file 04). Deliver one-to-one until six students exist, then open the room.
3. Pilot one subject-year recording, not six. Pick the subject with the most demand in the diagnostic leads (the CRM has the numbers) and Year 13 first, because the urgency is real. Pay £1,500 to £2,000 with the IP agreement. Sell it only inside Plus. Measure completion and whether Plus students stay longer than Mentorship students before commissioning the rest.
4. Async tutor Q&A from day one of Plus; live weekly Q&A per track at four students.
5. Tell the September student the truth this week, and freeze the cohort promo drafts.

## The numbers, side by side

| Scenario | Students | Your hours a week | Revenue a month | Fixed costs a month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current shape (weekly 1:1, phone) | 5 | 8 | £1,500 | 0 |
| Skool mentorship, room open | 12 | 8 to 10 | £3,600 | about £80 (Skool) |
| Skool mentorship, room open | 25 | 12 to 14, needs a coach for fortnightlies | £7,500 | £80 plus coach about £1,000 |
| Plus tier, one subject recorded, async Q&A | 12 (6 on Plus) | 8 to 10 | £4,500 | £80 plus £100 tutor retainer, recordings already paid |
| As proposed: £100 per subject standalone, six live Q&As | 10 subject subscriptions | your time unchanged | £1,000 | £1,200 Q&A plus £6,000 one-off recordings |

The last row is the one to stare at. The proposal's fixed Q&A cost exceeds its revenue until the thirteenth subscription, and the recordings need six months to produce before any of it exists. The Plus row gets to the same place with one recording, no fixed Q&A, and a product parents cannot price-compare.

## The one thing to decide first

Whether the mentorship is the business or one product among seven. Everything above assumes it is the business, with the free tools feeding it and the tutors' content living inside it. If that is the decision, the ladder collapses to two tiers, the site gets simpler, the email engine gets one destination, and every piece of content you make sells the same thing. If it is not, the proposal adds a Skool tier and six subscriptions to a ladder that already has more products than customers, and the honest stress test says that is the risk, not the pricing.
