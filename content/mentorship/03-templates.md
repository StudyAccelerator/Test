# Top 1% Mentorship: templates

*Everything the runsheets refer to. Copy, fill, send. Messages are in Waleed's email voice (see `.claude/skills/content-studio/references/email-style-waleed.md`); the scorecard and notes are working documents and can be as plain as you like.*

## 1. The scorecard (one per student)

Keep it wherever you will actually open it: a Google Sheet shared with the student is the simplest, because they fill it in before every session and you both see the same page. One row per week. The student owns the weekly rows; you own the monthly page.

**Header block (filled in session 1).** Name. Year and track (Year 12 or Resit). Subjects, boards, spec links. Target grades and the reason behind them (their words). Diagnostic profile and the five scores. Exam dates. School test and mock dates. Resit: exam centre, entry deadline, entry confirmed (date). Contact rules agreed (date). Parent name and email.

**Weekly rows.** Paste this header into the sheet:

```
week,date,hours_planned,hours_done,hours_high_yield,hours_low_yield,topics_struggling,topics_shaky,topics_solid,blurts_done,reblurt_gap_first,reblurt_gap_second,recall_sessions,review_sessions,paper_name,paper_score,paper_max,paper_pct,time_used_min,time_allowed_min,grade_equiv,error_entries_added,error_entries_retested,error_entries_cleared,sessions_started_on_time,sessions_planned,what_changed
```

Column notes:
- `hours_high_yield` is blurt, recall, review, papers and log retests. `hours_low_yield` is reading, rewriting, watching. Week 1 is the leak; every later week is whether it closed.
- `reblurt_gap_first` and `reblurt_gap_second` are the gap counts on the same topic, first blurt vs re-blurt. One topic per week is enough; name it in `what_changed` if needed.
- `grade_equiv` only from the real published boundaries of that paper. Blank if none exist, never estimated.
- `what_changed` is their sentence, typed in the last five minutes of the session.

**Monthly page (built live in sessions 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24).** Four charts from the weekly rows (hours split, Struggling count, paper percentage, error entries cleared), the school evidence that landed this month, three sentences on what changed, and one sentence on what next month adds. This page is attached to the parent email.

## 2. Session notes (one file per student, one entry per session)

```
Session N, date
Scorecard headline: (the one number that moved, or did not)
Real reason behind the number: (what you heard, not what they reported)
Teach done: (as per runsheet / swapped for ... because ...)
Their one line:
Next week's work: (specific days, topics, sessions)
Flags: (drift, environment, wellbeing, parent, admin)
For a second coach reading this cold: (one sentence on where this student is)
```

The last line is not optional. It is what makes file 04 possible.

## 3. Welcome message to the student (sent the day they sign)

Subject: Your first mentorship session, and five things before it

Hi {name},

Dr Waleed here. Amazing to have you on board, and genuinely well done for taking this on, because most students never get further than knowing something needs to change.

Our first session is a diagnosis, not a lesson. I want to see exactly how you study right now so that everything we build afterwards fits you rather than a textbook idea of a student. So here's what I need before we speak:

1. Take the revision diagnostic at alevelaccelerators.com/revision-diagnostic and send me a screenshot of your profile and the five scores.
2. Download the spec PDF for each of your subjects and the exam board's summer 2027 timetable.
3. For the seven days before our call, keep an honest log of your revision: what you did, how long, and whether it was reading or watching (put an L next to it) or testing yourself (put an H). Paper or the notes app on your phone, either is fine.
4. Have your notes and folder for your worst subject open on your screen for the call.
5. Book your first four sessions here: {scheduler link}.

One thing about how we work. You've got my number, and it's there for the moment you're stuck between sessions and a two-line answer would save you a week. I'm a full time doctor, so I reply in the evenings and within a day, and if I'm on nights it might be two. Anything bigger than two lines goes on your scorecard and we do it properly on the call. That's not me being distant, it's how I make sure every question gets a real answer.

All I ask in return is that you do the between-session work, because that's where the grades actually move :)

Keep going!

Waleed

PS: The hours log is the bit people skip, and it's the most useful thing you'll bring to the first call. Be honest on it, I promise it's a judge-free zone. I was guilty of everything on it at your age.

## 4. Welcome message to the parent (sent the same day)

Subject: {child}'s mentorship: how it works and what you'll hear from me

Hi {name},

I hope you're well! I'm Dr Waleed, and I'm really glad {child} is joining the mentorship. I wanted to send you a personal note about how it runs, so you know what to expect.

Every week {child} and I have an hour on Zoom. The first session is a diagnosis: I look at exactly how they study now, where the hours are going, and what's stopping the effort turning into marks. From there we rebuild it in order: how they revise, how they keep what they've learned, where they spend their time, and finally how they perform in the exam itself. None of it is subject teaching. It's the system that sits underneath every subject, which is why it moves all of them at once.

You'll hear from me at the end of every month with a short update: what changed, the numbers behind it, what next month adds, and one or two things you can do at home. I'll send it whether the month went well or not, because you're paying for the truth as much as the sessions.

Two small things that help enormously. First, {child} keeps a scorecard that they fill in before each session; if you can protect a quiet ten minutes on Sunday evenings for that, it makes a real difference. Second, if there's anything going on at school or at home that I should know about, please don't hesitate to email me.

I'm confident we can help with this, and I'll keep you updated every step of the way.

Best wishes,

Waleed

PS: I'm a full time doctor, so if a reply takes a day or two it's a night shift rather than anything else. I do always reply.

## 5. The monthly parent update (sent after sessions 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24)

Subject: {child}, month {N}: what changed

Hi {name},

I hope you're well! Here's {child}'s month {N} update, with the page from their scorecard attached.

**What changed this month.** {Three sentences, plain. Example for month 1: "In week one, about eight of {child}'s eleven revision hours were reading and rewriting notes. By this week that had flipped: nine of twelve hours were self-testing, which is the single biggest change a student can make. Their first timed section came back at 54 percent, and the second, on the topics they'd blurted, at 68."}

**The numbers.** {Two or three lines from the page: the Struggling count, the paper percentages, the consistency figure. No estimates, no grade predictions.}

**What next month adds.** {One sentence, from the runsheet's preview line. Example: "Month two is about making the fixes stay, so you'll see me talking about returns and the calendar rather than new methods."}

**One thing at home.** {One specific, doable thing. Examples: "Ask to see the blurt sheet, not the notes." "Sunday evening at seven is the scorecard ten minutes; protecting it is the most useful thing you can do." "Don't ask how revision is going; ask what the gap count was."}

Any questions at all, just reply to this email. I'm always happy to talk it through.

Best wishes,

Waleed

PS: {One line of him. Example: "The blurting is genuinely humbling at first, so if {child} comes out of a session looking deflated in week two, that's the method working, not failing. It gets better fast."}

## 6. The missed-session message (within 48 hours of any missed session, yours or theirs)

Hi {name},

Sorry we missed this week. Rather than leave a gap, I've recorded a ten-minute walk-through of your scorecard here: {link}. It covers what moved, the one thing to change this week, and your week plan, so nothing slides. Watch it before Sunday and reply with your one line for the week :)

Your next slot is {date}. If that's a problem, grab another here: {scheduler link}.

Keep going!

Waleed

PS: The between-session work still stands, especially the returns. A missed call is fine; a missed week isn't.

## 7. The contact rules (agreed in session 1, on the scorecard header)

- The number is for the between-session question that a two-line answer solves. Anything bigger goes on the scorecard for the call.
- Replies in the evening, within one working day; two when on nights. No replies after 10pm, in either direction.
- Sessions are booked four weeks ahead. A missed session becomes a recorded scorecard review within 48 hours, never a silent gap.
- Every session is recorded to the Zoom cloud and available to the student.
- Parents know the channel exists and can see the monthly page. Nothing about the programme is private from a parent, and nothing personal a student shares is passed on unless it is a safeguarding matter, which as a doctor you handle the way you would at work.

## 8. The one-line bank (for the "what changed" moment when a student is stuck for words)

Never fill it in for them, but if they freeze, offer two and let them pick or rephrase:
- "I found out I recognise things instead of knowing them."
- "I did the topic I've been avoiding."
- "I stopped making notes and started testing."
- "My week has a plan and I ran most of it."
- "I came back to a topic and it was still there."
- "I lost fewer marks to wording."
- "I finished the paper with time to check."
- "I ran the session myself."
