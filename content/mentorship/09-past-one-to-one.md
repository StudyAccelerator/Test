# Scaling past one to one: the clinic model and the tier ladder

*Written 30 September 2026, at £2,400 MRR and about 12 students within a fortnight. This answers the question "how does this scale past £10k when one to one does not", and it supersedes the delivery shape in file 07 for any student who joins from now on.*

## Where you actually are

You are about two months ahead of the base case in file 07, which put the doctor's salary milestone at November or December. Hitting it at the end of September means the structural decision arrived early, and early is the good version, because changing the shape for twelve students is a conversation and changing it for twenty five is a renegotiation.

One arithmetic flag before anything else. Twelve students at £300 is £3,600, not £3,000. If £3,000 at twelve is right then the average is £250, which means somebody is on a discount or a founding rate that was never written down. That gap is £600 a month, which is a coach. Check the Stripe subscription list against the student list this week and write the real average on the scorecard, because every projection below runs off it.

## The one line item that breaks

It is not the weekly one to one. It is the fortnightly one.

At 33 students the weekly onboarding calls cost about 5 hours a week and the fortnightly calls cost about 10.5, on top of the room, the community and the admin. The fortnightly call is the biggest number in the table and it is the one that grows linearly with every student you add, forever. Any answer that keeps a private recurring call for every student caps out somewhere between 20 and 25 students no matter how the rest is arranged.

So the answer is not to make the fortnightly call shorter or to hire someone to do it. It is to replace what the fortnightly call is for.

## What the fortnightly call is actually for

Strip it back and it does four jobs:

1. Read their scorecard and say what the numbers mean.
2. Diagnose what is going wrong this fortnight.
3. Teach the next technique.
4. Hold them accountable to the week ahead.

Job three is already identical for every student, which is why file 08 says to record the sixteen teaches once. Job one and job four are short. Job two, the diagnosis, is the thing they pay for, and it is also the thing that **teaches better in public than in private**, because the pattern repeats across students and watching three other people get diagnosed teaches the pattern three times over. A student who watches you find someone else's recall leak learns to find their own.

That is the whole insight. The private call is the wrong container for the most valuable thing you do.

## The clinic model

Replace the fortnightly one to one with a **weekly clinic**: 60 minutes, 8 students, same group and same slot every week.

- **First 15 minutes, the teach.** One technique from the sixteen, on the rotation. Everyone gets it at once.
- **Remaining 45 minutes, four hot seats of about 11 minutes.** A student brings their scorecard on screen. You read the numbers out loud, diagnose, name the fix, set their fortnight. The other seven watch and the chat is open.
- **Rotation.** Four hot seats a week means each student in a clinic of eight gets one every fortnight. That is the same personal attention cadence they have now.

What the student gains: 52 contact hours a year instead of 26, three extra diagnoses watched every week, and peers who are visibly running the same system. What you gain is the arithmetic below.

**Session one stays one to one and always will.** The four tier mark loss analysis on their own paper is the diagnosis, the sell and your IP, and it is the thirty minutes that makes a parent believe. Book it privately, then the student joins their clinic the following week. Onboarding drops from four private hours per student to one.

**The monthly review becomes their fourth hot seat.** Every fourth time a student is in the chair, the hot seat is the monthly review: the four tier chart, the trend, what next month adds. The parent still gets the written update the same day, and the written update is what the parent actually reads.

### The hours, side by side

| Students | Current shape (file 07) | Clinic model | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | about 11.5 hours a week | **about 7** | Two clinics a week replace nine fortnightly calls |
| 20 | about 16 (needs a coach) | **about 8.5** | Three clinics; still no coach needed |
| 33 | about 21.5 alone | **about 11** | Four clinics, 1.2 hours of new-student diagnoses, community and admin |
| 60 | not possible alone | about 16, or 9 with one coach on two clinics | Six to eight clinics, cohort onboarding |

At 33 students the clinic model delivers £10k MRR in about eleven hours a week, on your own, before a coach exists. That is the answer to the question.

And the immediate effect matters more than the long one: switching at twelve students takes you from about 11.5 hours a week to about 7, at the same revenue, while you are still a full-time doctor.

## The tier ladder

The clinic fixes the hours. Tiering fixes something else: right now every student costs you the same and pays you the same, so a parent who would pay double cannot, and a family who cannot afford £300 has no option but to cancel.

| | Room | Core | Private |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | £149 a month | £300 a month | £600 a month |
| Entry diagnosis, one to one | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Weekly clinic | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Guaranteed hot seat | No, questions in chat | Every fortnight | Every fortnight |
| Private call with you | | | Fortnightly, 45 minutes |
| Community and classroom | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Monthly written review | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Parent update | Quarterly | Monthly | Monthly |
| Places | Open | Open | Capped at 8 |

Core stays the default and the thing you sell. The other two do specific jobs.

**Room is the churn catcher.** Today, a student who hits month five and whose parent is feeling the cost has exactly one option, which is to cancel and become £0. With Room they become £149, they stay in the system, they keep the assets, and a good term brings them back up. Every retained downgrade is revenue you currently lose entirely, and it costs you no extra hours because they are already in a clinic you are already running.

**Private is the ceiling raiser.** Some of the parents already on your list would pay double for a private fortnightly call with a doctor. Capping it at eight keeps it deliverable (about 1.9 hours a week at eight students) and makes the cap honest rather than a sales tactic.

A worked £10k at 33 students: 8 on Room, 20 on Core, 5 on Private is £10,192 a month for about 12.6 hours a week. The same revenue as the flat model at roughly the same hours, with a downgrade path underneath it and headroom above it.

## What to do, in order

**This week, at twelve students.**

1. Reconcile Stripe against the student list and write down the real average price.
2. Tell the current twelve nothing yet. They were sold a fortnightly one to one and they keep it. Grandfather them by name, in writing, on the scorecard.
3. Pick the clinic slot. One evening, 60 minutes, same time every week.

**From student thirteen.** Every new student is sold the clinic model from the first call: private diagnosis, then a weekly clinic with a fortnightly hot seat. Nothing about the price changes yet. The site copy changes at the same time (file 04 lists the four lines that already do not match what you deliver, and "fortnightly calls" is one of them).

**At fifteen to eighteen students.** Start the first clinic with the new-shape students only, so the grandfathered twelve are undisturbed. Six to eight students is the minimum for a clinic to feel like a room rather than an awkward call. Then offer the original twelve a swap, framed honestly as more contact rather than less: weekly instead of fortnightly, and they keep the hot seat cadence they have now. Most will take it. Anyone who does not keeps their private call for as long as they stay.

**At twenty students.** Introduce Room and Private. Room goes to anyone giving notice, as a save offer, before it goes on the site. Private goes to the parents who have already asked for more of you.

**At twenty five to thirty.** Hire the first coach. What makes this possible now and did not six months ago is that the system is written down: the spine, the runsheets, the sixteen teaches, the templates. A coach runs two clinics a week from file 08 and you keep every entry diagnosis, every Private call and the community. At about £25 an hour for four to five hours a week, that is roughly £500 a month against £10k.

**Beyond fifty.** Cohort onboarding (the entry diagnoses batched into two afternoons a month), two or three coaches, and the recorded subject layer from file 05 as the Plus tier. That is a 2027 shape and it depends on these numbers holding.

## The honest read on leaving medicine

£3,000 MRR is not a replacement for a £3,000 to £3,500 doctor's salary, and it would be an expensive mistake to treat it as one. Revenue is not take-home. Against that £3,000 sit the ad spend that produced it, Stripe and Skool fees, and eventually a coach, and what is left is taxed with no pension, no sick pay and no paid leave behind it.

Working it through on your own numbers: at £6,000 MRR, minus roughly £900 of ads at the current cost per lead, minus a part-time coach and tools, leaves something in the region of £4,500 gross, and after tax and National Insurance that lands close to a £3,000 to £3,500 take-home. So **£6,000 MRR is the honest replacement number, and £8,000 is the one that gives you a cushion.** That is 20 to 27 students, which the clinic model delivers in eight or nine hours a week.

The useful part: under the clinic model you reach the number that lets you leave without ever needing more than about nine hours a week, which means you can hold a full-time rota all the way to the decision instead of being forced out early by delivery load.

## What not to do yet

- **Do not raise the ad budget past £30 a day until the first clinic has run twice.** Delivery capacity is the constraint, and cheap leads that cool are worse than no leads.
- **Do not build the recorded subject layer yet.** File 05 still stands: pilot one subject-year, inside a Plus tier, after the clinic model is running.
- **Do not hire a coach before you have run six clinics yourself.** The clinic needs a format you can hand over, and you only have that once you have found out what breaks in it.
- **Do not change anything for the existing twelve.** They are the proof, the testimonials and the case studies. Their experience should get better and never cheaper.
