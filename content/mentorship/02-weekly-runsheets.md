# Top 1% Mentorship: the weekly runsheets

*Sessions 1 to 26, 14 September 2026 to 28 March 2027, plus the sketched run-in to the exams. Open at the week. Every runsheet has the same seven headings. You need this page, the student's scorecard and the Revision Tracker, nothing else.*

*Calendar assumptions, to overwrite with each student's real dates in session 1: session 1 in the week of 14 September; autumn half term the week of 26 October; schools break for Christmas around 18 December and return 4 January; spring half term the week of 15 February; Easter holidays 29 March to 9 April (Easter Sunday is 28 March 2027); A-level exams from early May 2027; Year 12 end-of-year exams in May or June, school dependent. The resit student's exam centre sets its own entry deadline ahead of the JCQ deadline of 21 February.*

---

## Before session 1: the onboarding week

Send both students (and each parent) the welcome message in file 03 as soon as they sign. It asks for five things before the first call, so session 1 can be a diagnosis rather than a form-filling exercise:

1. Take the revision diagnostic at alevelaccelerators.com/revision-diagnostic and send you the profile name and the five scores (they can screenshot the report).
2. Fetch the spec PDF for each subject and the exam board's timetable for summer 2027. Resit: also the results slip from last summer and any component breakdown the centre gave them.
3. Keep an honest hours log for the seven days before the call: what they did, how long, and whether it was reading or watching (L) or testing themselves (H). Paper or the Notes app, it does not matter.
4. Bring their notes and folder for their worst subject to the call, on screen.
5. Book the first four session slots in the Zoom scheduler.

Set up the scorecard for each student (template in file 03) with the baseline row empty. Start a session-notes file per student. Read the diagnostic report before the call: the archetype tells you the shape of the conversation before it starts.

## Session 1, week of 14 September: The diagnosis

**Why this week.** You cannot fix what you have not watched. Every student says they revise. Session 1 is you watching how, and the three diagnostics below expose the leak in under an hour. Nothing gets installed this week on purpose: a method taught before the diagnosis is a guess, and the student needs to see the problem themselves before they will change anything.

**The number that should move.** Nothing moves yet. This session sets every baseline: the hours split (high-yield vs low-yield), the five diagnostic scores and the profile, the topic count in the worst subject, and this week's baseline timed section score and time.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 15, the story.* Why are they here, what grades do they want, what course and where. Resit: "Walk me through last summer, paper by paper. What happened in the room?" Year 12: "How did you revise for GCSEs, honestly, and how is it going three weeks into A-levels?" You are listening for the archetype in their own words. Write down their target grades and the reason behind them; that reason comes back in month three.
- *15 to 25, the report.* Walk through the diagnostic: the five scores, the bottleneck, the profile. Your line: "This isn't about how smart you are or how hard you work. It's about where the hours go."
- *25 to 35, show me last week.* Their hours log on screen. Go through it line by line and classify every block H or L together. Add it up. Say the split out loud: "So of your eleven hours, about eight were reading and rewriting." That number is the leak, and it is the first thing the scorecard records.
- *35 to 45, the live blurt.* "Give me a topic you're confident on." Blank page, notes closed, five minutes writing everything they know. Then open the notes and count the gaps in a different colour. Frame it before they start: "This is a method test, not a knowledge test. It's humbling for everyone, I promise." Count the gaps together. That count is the second baseline.
- *45 to 55, set up.* Fill the baseline row of the scorecard together. Agree the contact rules (file 03: what the number is for, when you reply). Book the four slots if they have not. Set this week's work.
- *55 to 60, the one line.* "What changed today?" Type their answer into the scorecard.

**What they walk away with.** Their profile in one sentence, their hours split as a number, one blurt sheet with red on it, a scorecard with a baseline row, and the contact rules.

**Between-session work.**
- One timed section under real conditions, marked against the mark scheme straight after. Record the score, the percentage and the time used. Year 12: a set of exam questions on the topics taught since September, about 30 to 40 minutes. Resit: one full paper, the one they scored worst on last summer.
- List every topic in the worst subject from the spec, no ratings yet. Their job, from their spec.
- Keep the hours log going daily, with H or L against every block.

**Deviate if.** The diagnostic comes back Optimiser (every score 70 plus): skip the hours audit, spend the time making retrieval harder (whole-topic blank page, explain it aloud) and treat the whole plan as a sharpening programme. Resit student visibly flat or ashamed about last summer: give the story twenty minutes and say the reframe plainly ("something in the method went wrong, and that's fixable"), but the live blurt still happens, because it is the first evidence that the problem is mechanical.

**Year 12 note.** Their leak is usually a GCSE method (rereading, neat notes) that worked when the content was small. Say so.
**Resit note.** Pin down the exam centre and its entry deadline today and write both on the scorecard. Ask for the results slip and any per-paper breakdown: the paper they lost most on is where the baseline paper comes from.

## Session 2, week of 21 September: Blurt and Fix

**Why this week.** Method is the upstream system: until they can produce a topic from memory, planning the week is planning re-reading, and spacing is scheduling nothing. The live blurt last week showed them the gap between recognising and remembering. This week installs the method that closes it. Not later, because everything in months two to six is built on this one habit.

**The number that should move.** Three Blurt and Fix sessions done this week, each with a gap count. The hours split starts shifting from L to H.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.* The baseline section: score, time, and how it felt. The hours log. The topic list.
- *10 to 40, the teach, on their worst topic, live.* Recognition vs recall: "Are you someone who can produce this with the notes closed? If you can't, you don't know it yet, you recognise it." Then the four steps, done together on screen: blurt for 15 to 20 minutes on a blank page; check against the notes in another colour for 15; short break; relearn only the red for about 30; finish by re-blurting the red from memory. The reframe that makes it stick: "The red is not failure. The red is the diagnosis. It's the only bit of the topic you need to study." The phone rule: another room, before you sit down. And for note makers: "The exam never asks to see your notes. For the next fortnight, no new notes. Blurt sheets only."
- *40 to 55, build the week.* Three blurt slots on three named topics on three named days. Ninety minutes each with a break. Every other study session ends with ten minutes closed-book on what was covered.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The Blurting Template (the free PDF from the site), one completed blurt sheet from the session, three blurt slots in their week, the closed-book rule.

**Between-session work.** Three Blurt and Fix sessions on the three worst topics; keep every sheet; record the gap count for each on the scorecard. Hours log continues. Ten minutes closed-book at the end of every session.

**Deviate if.** Scholar profile with a strong Method score (they already self-test): cut the teach to ten minutes and spend the rest on harder retrieval standards (blank page whole topic, teach it aloud to you). Perfectionist: add the no-new-notes rule and say why (production is not revision). Grinder: this is their week; go slow and let the gap count land.

**Year 12 note.** Blurt the topics taught in the last three weeks. This is how a Year 12 keeps up: a blurt within days of each lesson week is the cheapest revision they will ever do.
**Resit note.** Blurt the topics from the worst paper first. Expect brutal gap counts and say so in advance: "This is the map of what faded, and it's smaller than you think, because relearning from a gap list is fast."

## Session 3, week of 28 September: The topic audit and the week

**Why this week.** Retrieval is installed; now it needs a week to live in. An audit before they could blurt would have been a guess. Now they have felt three topics' gaps, so the ratings will be honest. This is the first pass at Consistency and Targeting together, done through the tracker so the week builds itself.

**The number that should move.** Every topic in the worst subject rated Struggling, Shaky or Solid (all subjects if there is time). The Struggling count becomes the clearest line on the whole scorecard. Sessions planned this week, with a capacity cap that is respected, not exceeded.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.* Three blurts, three gap counts, how the closed-book minutes went. "Which one hurt most?" That is the first Struggling topic.
- *10 to 40, the audit and the week.* Rate every topic live, with the one criterion: "Book closed, could you answer exam questions on it right now?" Anything they hesitate on is not Solid. Then the dose per rating (the tracker's own rule: Struggling gets a blurt, two recalls and a review; Shaky a full cycle; Solid is kept warm), the ceilings (two and a half hours on a school day, six on a free day, and topics that do not fit are parked for next week, never crammed), lunch and dinner as anchors, the Open Hour, and the Sunday reset: ten minutes every Sunday to re-rate and rebuild. Build the week in the Revision Tracker together on screen.
- *40 to 55, make it real.* Move sessions around their actual commitments (school, work, sport, family, faith). Download the poster. Print it if they can.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** Their first real week plan as a poster, the full topic audit, the Sunday reset as a ritual.

**Between-session work.** Run the week as built and tick each session. Sunday: re-rate, rebuild next week, record planned vs done on the scorecard.

**Deviate if.** Crammer profile: pull session 6 (starting) into the last block today, because their week will not survive to Sunday without it. Everything rated Solid: pick one and blurt it in the session; the ratings get honest fast.

**Year 12 note.** Rate all subjects. The week includes lessons, so the school-day ceiling is the binding one. The lesson-debrief blurt (20 minutes the same evening) goes in as a standing block.
**Resit note.** No school means the danger is six-hour days every day and burnout by week five. Cap it at five study days, keep both anchors, use the Open Hour as structure, and if they work, build around the shifts. A resit year with no structure is the single biggest risk on their scorecard.

## Session 4, week of 5 October: Active recall, the error log, and the month 1 review

**Why this week.** Blurts find gaps in what they know; questions find gaps in what they can do with it. The error log is where wrong answers become the highest-yield material they own. And month one closes with the hours leak before-and-after in front of them, because that is the first proof the programme has and the student needs to see it before the discomfort of month two.

**The number that should move.** The month 1 page: hours split week 1 vs week 4, Struggling count, blurt gap counts trending down on repeated topics, second timed section vs the baseline, and the error log started with at least three entries.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.* Planned vs done, the Sunday re-rate, and the second timed section.
- *10 to 30, the teach.* Active Recall sessions: past paper questions on a topic, cold, mixed types, marked straight after each one rather than at the end. Then the error log: one page per subject, and every dropped mark goes on it as four things: the question reference, what actually went wrong, the corrected answer in the mark scheme's wording, and a retest date. Introduce the six error categories now, even though the audit comes in session 10: did not know it, could not recall it, misread the question, method or working, wording, ran out of time. Put three entries on the log from this week's section, live.
- *30 to 45, the month 1 review.* Build the page together: the four numbers side by side, the four "what changed" lines read back, and what month two adds: "You can find the gaps now. Month two is making the fixes stay."
- *45 to 55, build the week.* Recall sessions placed per the tracker's dose; the log retest slot on Sunday; the next timed section placed.
- *55 to 60, the one line.* Send parent update 1 tonight (template in file 03).

**What they walk away with.** The error log started, the month 1 page, and the parent email in their corner.

**Between-session work.** Recall sessions as scheduled, every dropped mark onto the log, the log retested on Sunday, the next timed section marked and logged.

**Deviate if.** The hours split has not moved: the review becomes the honest conversation instead of a celebration. Ask why, listen, and pull the consistency material (session 6) into the second half. The student is flying: do not change anything, and praise the numbers rather than the person ("look at that split") so the credit stays with the method.

**Year 12 note.** The second section should include the topics they blurted in weeks two and three, so the line from retrieval to marks is visible on their own paper.
**Resit note.** Second full paper, a different paper in the same subject. Compare the error categories, not just the score. The category that dominates is usually the diagnosis of last summer.

## Session 5, week of 12 October: Spacing, the calendar that remembers

**Why this week.** Month one installed retrieval; by now they have re-blurted a topic and been surprised by how much went. That surprise is the entry point. Spacing before a retrieval method would have been scheduling re-reading; spacing retrieval is the whole trick. This is the week that turns "I learned it" into "I still have it".

**The number that should move.** Re-blurt gap counts against the first blurt on the same topic (shrinking). Spaced Review sessions done. A live retention check: a week-2 topic blurted cold in the session.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 40, the teach.* The curve, in plain words: come back the next day, then a few days later, then weekly, and the topic stays; miss both windows and it resets. Show them where the tracker already places the recall and review blocks and why they sit where they do. Then the retention check, live: pick a topic from week two, blurt it cold for five minutes, compare against the original sheet. The finding is usually good and the point is the comparison. The rule from here: "Before you relearn anything from scratch, test what's still there. You know more than it feels like." And the reframe: "This is a scheduling problem, not a memory problem."
- *40 to 55, build the week.* Reviews are the sessions students skip first, so they are placed first this week. Every blurt gets its next-day recall and its day-five review written in before anything else.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** A re-blurt comparison (two sheets side by side, dated), and the return rule.

**Between-session work.** Every blurt with its returns in the plan; re-blurt two week-2 topics and record the gap counts; the log retest on Sunday.

**Deviate if.** Re-Learner profile: this is their week. Spend the whole session on it and pull the stuck-topic test from session 8 forward. Crammer: session 6 first, then this one next week; there is no point spacing sessions that do not start.

**Year 12 note.** September's lesson content is already fading. The returns this week are on that, and the Year 12 edge for the rest of the year is a blurt within days of each lesson followed by two returns.
**Resit note.** Returns are how faded content comes back. The re-blurt on a faded topic is nearly always better than they fear, and this is the week they see it.

## Session 6, week of 19 October: Starting, the system that survives your moods

**Why this week.** The novelty is gone by week six and the scorecard will show skipped sessions. Consistency decides whether months three to six happen at all. Now, because you have three weeks of planned vs done data, so this is a diagnosis of their pattern rather than a lecture about discipline.

**The number that should move.** Sessions started on time, as a percentage of planned. The streak. Open Hour used as intended.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard, read for pattern.* Which sessions got skipped? Evenings? After school? The ones on a Struggling topic? Say the pattern back to them.
- *10 to 40, the teach.* Starting is the whole problem: every session that has not been decided in advance costs a decision, and decisions are what run out. The night-before card: topic, task and the first question, written down before bed. The two-minute start: open the template, write the title, and you are in. Phone in another room before sitting down, not after. The 45-minute unit for bad days: "Shrink the session, never skip it. A finished 45 beats a planned 90 every time." Hardest topic in the first slot while energy is highest. The Open Hour as the pressure valve so the plan bends instead of breaking. Your confession belongs here: six hours making the perfect timetable, never touched it again.
- *40 to 55, rebuild the week.* Sessions moved to the times they actually start. A bad-day version of each day written next to the full one.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The night-before card (three lines), the bad-day week, the two-minute start.

**Between-session work.** A night-before note every evening, photographed onto the scorecard. Started-on-time recorded yes or no per session. One deliberate bad-day 45 this week, to prove it counts.

**Deviate if.** Consistency already above 90 percent: fifteen minutes on this, then use the rest for a harder retrieval standard (teach it aloud). The real problem is environment (siblings, no desk, shifts): spend the time on the place and the time (library, college, a fixed morning), because no card fixes a room.

**Year 12 note.** The after-school slump is the pattern to expect. The lesson-debrief blurt at a fixed time (on the bus, or before dinner) is the fix.
**Resit note.** Nothing external starts their day, so the day needs a start time and a place. The danger is the two o'clock start. Anchor mornings, and if they work, the study block goes before the shift, not after.

## Session 7, week of 26 October: Half term, the free-week protocol and the first mixed paper

**Why this week.** Half term is the first stretch of free days and most students either waste it or burn out in it. It is also the first chance for a longer, mixed paper, because there are six weeks of retrieval to consolidate and nothing new arriving. The free-week protocol is a reusable skill: Christmas, February and Easter all use it.

**The number that should move.** Free-day hours capped at six with three deep blocks maximum. The first mixed timed paper: score, percentage, time used. The Struggling count at the end of the week.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 40, the teach.* The free-week plan: consolidate, do not cover. Every Struggling topic gets a full cycle across the week, three deep blocks a day at most, one mixed paper midweek, one full day off with no guilt. Then the paper debrief method, which they will use for the rest of the programme: mark it straight after, categorise every lost mark, name the top three reasons marks went, log the entries, re-rate the topics it touched. Run a mini debrief on last week's section live so they have done it once with you.
- *40 to 55, build the half-term week in the tracker* (free days, both anchors, the day off placed first).
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The half-term plan, the debrief routine on one page.

**Between-session work.** The week as built, the mixed paper, the debrief, the day off.

**Deviate if.** Their half term is a different week: swap sessions 7 and 8. No half term at all (resit): run it as the consolidation week anyway, a heavier week followed by a lighter one.

**Year 12 note.** The mixed section covers everything since September. It will feel like the first real test of the system, and it is.
**Resit note.** Full paper three, under conditions, with the full debrief. This is the paper that starts the trajectory line on the scorecard.

## Session 8, week of 2 November: Month 2 review, and when a topic will not stick

**Why this week.** Month two closes tier two: the student can find gaps, fix them and keep them. The review proves it with the Struggling count and the re-blurt gaps. The teach handles the topics that stay Struggling after two full cycles, because those are usually an understanding gap, which is tier one, which is not your job. Knowing when to send them elsewhere is part of the method.

**The number that should move.** The month 2 page: Struggling count week 3 vs week 8, re-blurt gap counts, papers one, two and three side by side, consistency percentage.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 30, the teach.* The stuck-topic test: "Can you explain it with the book open?" No means an understanding gap: write one precise question and take it to the teacher, a worked example, or a Subject Accelerator. Yes means a recall gap: more cycles with a harder retrieval standard. Then teach-it-aloud (explain the topic to you as if you were a Year 10; where they stall is the gap), and interleaving two stuck topics in one session so the brain has to choose the method. Apply the test to their stuck topics live.
- *30 to 45, the month 2 review,* built together. Preview month three: "You can keep things now. Month three aims the hours at the marks."
- *45 to 55, build the week.*
- *55 to 60, the one line.* Parent update 2 tonight.

**What they walk away with.** The stuck-topic test, the month 2 page, a written precise question for every genuinely stuck topic.

**Between-session work.** The stuck-topic test on everything still Struggling after two cycles; the precise questions taken to the teacher or the textbook; the rest of the week as built.

**Deviate if.** Nothing is stuck: skip the teach and use the time on the review and a harder retrieval standard.

**Year 12 note.** They have teachers. The precise-question habit turns every lesson into their second pass and makes teachers notice them, which matters for predicted grades later.
**Resit note.** No teacher, so the route is worked examples, the free resources they already use, or a Subject Accelerator if a whole topic is an understanding gap. This is where the programme honestly refers out, and saying so builds trust rather than losing it.

## Session 9, week of 9 November: Targeting, send the hours where the marks are missing

**Why this week.** With recall installed, the remaining leak is where the hours go: to the comfortable topics. Earlier, an avoid list would have drifted because nothing was being measured. Now there are eight weeks of ratings and three papers showing exactly where the marks fall, so targeting is arithmetic rather than willpower.

**The number that should move.** The avoid list written and its topics opened. Hours on Struggling topics vs Solid topics. A marks map of one paper (which topics carried the marks).

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 40, the teach.* The avoid list, ten minutes, honest: "Every topic you'd dread seeing in an exam tomorrow. That list is your syllabus now. Everything else is maintenance." The marks-per-hour idea: a topic at 80 percent has almost no marks left to give, and the next grade is in the topics they avoid. The worst-topic-first slot: the first deep block of every day is an avoid-list topic, while energy is highest. Then the marks map: take one past paper and count the marks by topic; the student does this, it is their spec, and it usually shows three or four topics carrying half the paper. Build the avoid list live and start the map on screen.
- *40 to 55, build the week.* Every deep block opens with an avoid-list topic. Re-rate on Sunday and watch the avoided ones climb.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The avoid list on the wall, a marks map of one paper.

**Between-session work.** The avoid list drives the week. Count the marks by topic across three papers and bring the map. Sunday re-rate.

**Deviate if.** Comfort Reviser profile: this is their key week, and it is fine to give it two. Already weakest-first by habit: fifteen minutes, then pull the error-log audit (session 10) forward.

**Year 12 note.** The marks map uses the spec's weightings plus any AS papers, and it changes as content arrives; redo it at Christmas.
**Resit note.** The map from last summer's papers plus three recent ones. It is usually obvious, and it is usually the same three topics that cost the grade.

## Session 10, week of 16 November: The error log, properly

**Why this week.** The log has thirty-plus entries by now. It is either a graveyard or the most valuable page they own, and the difference is the retest ladder. Now, because there is enough in it to see a pattern, and December tests are three weeks away.

**The number that should move.** Retest pass rate. Entries cleared. The category count (which of the six categories dominates), which decides the next month's focus.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 40, the teach.* The retest ladder: an entry is retested at day two, day seven and day twenty-one, and cleared after three clean retests. Then the category audit, done live on their log: count the entries in each of the six categories. The dominant one is the next four weeks' focus and the runsheets are built to take it: could not recall means more cycles; wording means session 11; time means session 13; misread means session 16. The same-mistake-twice rule: a repeat entry gets its own drill that week. And the honest line: "The wrong answers are the highest-yield material you own. Everyone else throws them away."
- *40 to 55, build the week.* A twenty-minute log retest slot twice a week, placed before anything else.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** Their category audit, the retest ladder.

**Between-session work.** Retest twice a week, clear entries, feed the log from every paper and section.

**Deviate if.** The log is thin (under ten entries): the week is about why. Not doing papers? Not marking? "Feeling bad and moving on"? Fix the feeding before the ladder.

**Year 12 note.** Class test mistakes count as entries. Ask for the tests back.
**Resit note.** The dominant category is usually the diagnosis of last summer in one number. Say so, and mean it kindly.

## Session 11, week of 23 November: Mark schemes as a language

**Why this week.** The first pure exam-craft teach. The log's wording entries and the papers show marks lost with the knowledge present. Now, because December tests or the self-set mock are two weeks away, this is the fastest-scoring skill in the short term, and because recall is installed there are genuinely marks to convert.

**The number that should move.** Lost-mark answers rewritten in mark scheme wording. Wording entries on the log. The December test itself.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 40, the teach.* The examiner as a person: dozens of papers a day, looking for the phrase, ticking step one, step two, "oh, I don't see step two here". Command words: state, describe, explain, evaluate, compare, calculate, and what each one wants on the page. The phrase bank: for their Struggling topics, the exact wording the scheme rewards, copied out by hand into one place per subject. Then mark like an examiner, live: they mark one of their own answers against the scheme, then rewrite it in the scheme's words and say the phrases aloud. "It's a language. Learn it like one."
- *40 to 55, build the week.* One mark-scheme study hour, the phrase bank started for each subject, every lost-mark answer from this week's section rewritten.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The phrase bank started, one rewritten answer, the command-word list.

**Between-session work.** The phrase bank, the rewrites, the wording entries retested.

**Deviate if.** Scholar profile: sessions 11, 13 and 16 are their core; consider pulling timing (13) into next week ahead of the mock. Maths-heavy student: the language is working and method marks: show the steps, the units, the significant figures, the marker's tick points. Same session, different vocabulary.

**Year 12 note.** Aim it at the December tests. Teachers mark to the same schemes.
**Resit note.** The phrase bank is built from the papers they have already sat. This is very often where last summer's marks went.

## Session 12, week of 30 November: The test run-in and the month 3 review (the dip week)

**Why this week.** December assessments (Year 12) or the self-set full mock (resit) are next week or the week after. The three-week mini run-in is the whole exam run-in in miniature, and learning it now means April is a repeat rather than a first attempt. And this is the month-three dip. Name it before it happens.

**The number that should move.** The month 3 page. A written prediction of the test score, to compare with the actual (calibration is a skill).

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 30, the teach.* The test run-in: a coverage map where every topic gets a final pass in order of weakness, two mixed papers, and the last 48 hours as retrieval only, no new content, early night. Then the prediction, written on the scorecard. Then the dip, said plainly: "This is the week most people quietly stop. The habit isn't automatic yet and the school hasn't caught up with what you can do. Here's your line. It's going up. Here's what month four adds."
- *30 to 45, the month 3 review,* built together, with the target grade and the reason they gave in session 1 read back.
- *45 to 55, build the two-week run-in.*
- *55 to 60, the one line.* Parent update 3 tonight, with the line that the December tests are the first school evidence and what to expect from them.

**What they walk away with.** The run-in plan, the written prediction, the month 3 page.

**Between-session work.** Run the run-in, sit the tests, record predictions vs actuals.

**Deviate if.** No December tests (some schools mock in January): set a mixed paper as the mock anyway. The student is already drifting: the dip talk becomes the whole session, and you go back to the reason they gave you in week one.

**Year 12 note.** Ask the school which assessments count towards anything.
**Resit note.** Two full papers under conditions across two weeks are their mock, sat at the time of day the real exam will be.

## Session 13, week of 7 December: Timing, the paper pace plan

**Why this week.** Timing is the most common tier-four leak and it shows up the moment full papers or tests happen under a clock. Now, because craft on installed recall converts straight into marks, and timing is trainable in weeks.

**The number that should move.** Time used vs time allowed per section. Marks lost in the time category. Unanswered questions per paper.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard,* with any test results that are back.
- *10 to 40, the teach.* Marks per minute: paper minutes divided by marks, usually about a minute a mark, adapted per paper. Checkpoints: the clock time written next to question five, ten, fifteen before starting. The move-on rule: stuck for sixty seconds, mark it, move on, come back. The last five minutes: every unanswered question gets something, then units, significant figures, command words. Your confession: finishing every paper the second the examiner says put your pencils down, and learning the hard way to use the time. From now, papers at 90 percent of the official time, so the real thing feels like a relief. Set checkpoints on a real section live.
- *40 to 55, build the week.* Two timed sections at 90 percent with checkpoints.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The checkpoint card, the move-on rule.

**Between-session work.** Two timed sections at 90 percent, time entries on the log.

**Deviate if.** Timing is already fine (they finish with time to spare and score well): use the session for the December test debrief by category and raise the retrieval standard instead.

**Year 12 note.** Sections, not full papers, until February.
**Resit note.** Whole papers, and the checkpoint card is per paper.

## Session 14, week of 14 December: The December debrief and the Christmas keep-warm plan

**Why this week.** The tests are back or the mock papers are marked, and this is the most important debrief of the term. Then the break: two weeks without you and without school, and the plan has to keep the system warm without guilt, because the January re-rate depends on it.

**The number that should move.** Test or mock results vs the prediction. Error categories on the test. The keep-warm plan agreed (three sessions a week for two weeks).

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 35, the debrief.* Treat the test like a paper: where the marks went by category, what the re-rate says, the teacher's comments decoded into which tier they are pointing at.
- *35 to 50, the keep-warm plan.* Three 45-minute sessions a week: one blurt on a Struggling topic, one log retest, one short mixed set. Nothing new. Full days off, Christmas Day and the family days off completely, no guilt. The January restart date on the scorecard.
- *50 to 55, this week.*
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The debrief page, the keep-warm plan.

**Between-session work, two weeks.** The keep-warm plan. One two-line check-in message from you in the second week ("How's the keep-warm going? One number for me."), nothing more.

**Deviate if.** The test went badly: the debrief takes the whole hour and the keep-warm plan goes by message. It went well: do not inflate it. "The system worked. December tests are tier two tests. The exam is tier four, and that's what the spring is for."

**Year 12 note.** Reports and parents' evening often land here. Ask for the teacher comments verbatim.
**Resit note.** Confirm exam entry status before the break, because the centre will be closed over Christmas and the deadline is close after it.

---

## Weeks of 21 and 28 December: no sessions

The keep-warm plan runs. One check-in message. Nothing else. A student who does nothing over Christmas is not a problem for the plan; it becomes the cleanest evidence in session 15.

## Session 15, week of 4 January: The January re-rate, what the break protected

**Why this week.** The break is the cleanest experiment of the year: two weeks of minimal input. The re-rate shows exactly what spacing kept and what faded, which is the most persuasive retention evidence the student will ever see. The year's shape also changes now: the resit exams are four months away, the Year 12 end-of-year exams about five.

**The number that should move.** The re-rate vs the 14 December ratings: topics that held vs topics that faded. Sessions started in the first week back. The calendar to the exams, on the scorecard.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard,* the keep-warm truth.
- *10 to 35, the re-rate,* live, every topic. Then the comparison: the topics that had two or more returns held; the ones that never had a return faded. "That's the whole method on one page."
- *35 to 50, the term map.* Weeks to the exams, school dates, the paper calendar from here (resit: one full paper a week from now; Year 12: sections building to full papers by February half term). Reset the week.
- *50 to 55, this week.*
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The held-and-faded page, the term map to the exams.

**Between-session work.** Returns rebuilt for everything that faded. The week. Paper one of the term.

**Deviate if.** January mocks (some Year 12 schools): this session becomes the mock run-in from session 12, and the re-rate moves to next week. They did nothing over Christmas: no guilt, and the re-rate is still the data; everything fading is the case for spacing made by absence.

**Year 12 note.** Redo the marks map with the autumn's content in it.
**Resit note.** If the exam entry is not confirmed in writing, this is now urgent and goes to the top of the session.

---

## Session 16, week of 11 January: Decoding the question, and the month 4 review

**Why this week.** With recall, targeting, scheme language and timing in place, the remaining common loss is answering the wrong question: misreads, missed data, long answers written without a plan. Now, because the misread category on the log and the December debrief show it, and because full papers start in earnest from here.

**The number that should move.** Misread entries on the log. Long answers planned before writing. The month 4 page.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 30, the teach.* The question as a set of instructions: underline the command word, circle the data, note the marks, and ask "what does the examiner want to see?" before writing a word. The sixty-second plan for any answer worth six marks or more: a skeleton of the points in scheme language, then write. The re-read: after writing, read the stem again and check the answer is to that question. The twist questions and the data or graph routine. Do it on two of their own questions.
- *30 to 45, the month 4 review,* with the held-and-faded page as the headline.
- *45 to 55, build the week.*
- *55 to 60, the one line.* Parent update 4 tonight.

**What they walk away with.** The decoding routine on a card, the month 4 page.

**Between-session work.** Every question decoded before answering. One full paper (resit) or the longest available section (Year 12) at 90 percent.

**Deviate if.** Essay subjects: the whole teach is planning and structure, still without content: the structure the scheme rewards (point, evidence, link, evaluation), the plan before the essay, the timing per paragraph. Sciences and maths: the data routine and the "show every step" rule.

**Year 12 note.** Full papers begin next month; this is the last sections-only fortnight.
**Resit note.** One full paper a week from here to the exams. The paper is now the unit of training.

---

## Session 17, week of 18 January: The A* gap, where the last marks are

**Why this week.** Month five begins with the habits installed and the papers showing a stable pattern. The last ten to fifteen percent of marks are nearly always two or three specific things, and before now the pattern was noise. This is the precision phase, and it is the reason a student who "knows all this now" keeps paying: the scorecard moves fastest from here.

**The number that should move.** The lost-marks breakdown across the last three papers, by category and by topic. Three named fixes with a number each. The grade equivalent trend from real boundaries.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 40, the three-paper audit.* Lay the lost marks out on a grid: category down the side, topic across the top. Then the question: "Which three things, fixed, take you over the next boundary?" Name them, with a number each (marks). Then the precision drills: whole-topic blank page rather than prompted cards, teach it aloud, mixed cold sets across topics, and the examiner's favourite questions per topic, which the student compiles from the pattern across papers (pattern, not content). Ninety percent time is the standard from now.
- *40 to 55, build the week.* Each of the three fixes gets a slot with its name on it.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The A* gap page: three named fixes, each with the marks it is worth.

**Between-session work.** The three drills, one paper at 90 percent, the gap page updated after it.

**Deviate if.** The papers are under fifty percent: the gap is not precision, it is recall on specific topics. Say so plainly, go back to phase two on those topics, and treat it as the diagnosis it is.

**Year 12 note.** Use the topic questions and one full AS-style paper. The gap page is built for the end-of-year exam format, and the three fixes are what the teacher will see improve before predictions.
**Resit note.** Three full papers. The gap page is, in effect, the plan to the exams.

---

## Session 18, week of 25 January: Full papers as training, the weekly paper rhythm

**Why this week.** From here to the exams the paper is the unit of training. One full paper a week gives a dozen data points before May and turns craft skills that were taught separately into one performance. Now, because every craft skill exists and papers can finally be practised whole.

**The number that should move.** Paper percentage and grade equivalent, weekly. Time used. The rewrite count.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 40, the teach.* The paper week: sit it at the same time of day as the real exam, under conditions; mark within 24 hours; a thirty-minute debrief by category; every lost mark rewritten; the log fed; the topics it touched re-rated; and next week's targeting taken from it. The inversion that changes everything: "The paper decides what you revise. Not the other way round." Questions on topics not yet taught: skip, note, pro-rate the score.
- *40 to 55, build the week.* The paper slot is the first thing placed, then everything else around it.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The paper-week routine, the paper tracker on the scorecard.

**Between-session work.** Paper one of the rhythm, marked, debriefed, rewritten, logged.

**Deviate if.** Paper supply is thin (a new spec): specimen papers, then the closest other board's papers for craft training (they train timing and decoding, not content), and reuse papers after six weeks.

**Year 12 note.** Full papers from February half term; until then the longest sections available, pro-rated.
**Resit note.** This is now the spine of every week. Nothing displaces the paper.

---

## Session 19, week of 1 February: Exam day, the blank, the panic and the reset

**Why this week.** The log's panic and misread entries and the timed papers show how they behave under pressure. Now, before the February half-term mock week, so the protocol gets practised under real conditions rather than read about.

**The number that should move.** Panic-category errors per paper. The five-minute check done on every paper. The routine practised, yes or no.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 40, the teach.* What a mind blank is: retrieval under stress, a cue problem rather than a knowledge problem, so the fix is the cue: write the topic's skeleton words in the margin, move on, come back. The sixty-second reset: pen down, breathe, next question. The first five minutes: read the whole paper, mark the easy ones, plan the order. The last five: something on every question, then units and command words. The night before: retrieval only, early, phone away, kit ready. The morning: no new content, the "three things I always forget" card. And when the paper is horrible: "Everyone's paper is horrible. The boundaries move. Your job is the next question." Practise the reset live on a hard question.
- *40 to 55, build the week.* The routine written into this week's paper.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The exam day card: before, first five, during, last five.

**Between-session work.** Paper two with the routine; panic entries logged.

**Deviate if.** The anxiety is clinical (panic attacks, sleep gone, not eating): that is a GP or school counsellor conversation. You are a doctor and know the line. Signpost, do not treat, and note it.

**Year 12 note.** The card is for the end-of-year exams, and for every class test between now and then.
**Resit note.** Last summer probably contained a version of this. Ask, and build the card around what actually happened in the room.

---

## Session 20, week of 8 February: Month 5 review and the half-term mock week

**Why this week.** The month 5 page carries five weeks of full papers, the first time the line is unmistakable. It is also when the predicted-grades conversation belongs, and the half term next week is the second free week: two full papers under conditions is a real mock.

**The number that should move.** The month 5 page and the trajectory chart. The mock-week plan. Resit: the exam entry confirmed in writing.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 25, the month 5 review,* with the twenty "what changed" lines read back.
- *25 to 45, the grade conversation.* Year 12: how schools set predicted grades (usually from the end-of-Year-12 exams in the summer term), what the teacher needs to see by then, and the plan to that exam. Resit: what the papers say against the target and what the last twelve weeks have to close. Then the mock week: two papers at exam time of day, the full debrief, a day off between them.
- *45 to 55, build the week.*
- *55 to 60, the one line.* Parent update 5 tonight, with the trajectory chart and the predicted-grades note.

**What they walk away with.** The month 5 page, the mock-week plan.

**Between-session work.** The half-term mock week.

**Deviate if.** Their half term is a different week: swap 20 and 21.

**Year 12 note.** This is the session where the parent update explains predicted grades. Most parents do not know when or how they are set.
**Resit note.** If the entry is still not confirmed, nothing else happens in this session until it is.

## Session 21, week of 15 February (half term): The mock debrief, and the second pass

**Why this week.** Two papers under conditions are the best data of the programme so far. Then the teach splits by track: Year 12 gets the second-pass system (pre-read next week's topics so lessons become the second time they see it, the cheapest revision there is and the Optimiser's move); the resit student gets per-paper strategy (which paper carries the grade, and where the last ten weeks go).

**The number that should move.** Both mock papers: percentage, grade equivalent, time used. The three fixes on the A* gap page updated.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 35, the mock debrief.* Both papers on the category-by-topic grid. The A* gap page rebuilt from it.
- *35 to 50, the teach.* Year 12: twenty minutes before each lesson week on next week's topics from the textbook, then the lesson, then the blurt. Resit: the paper strategy: which paper has the most recoverable marks, how the weekly paper rhythm splits across papers from here.
- *50 to 55, build the week.*
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The updated A* gap page; the second-pass routine (Year 12) or the paper strategy (resit).

**Between-session work.** The three fixes, paper four, and for Year 12 the first pre-read week.

**Deviate if.** The mock went badly on one paper only: the whole session is that paper.

## Session 22, week of 22 February: The run-in architecture, the ten-week plan to the exams

**Why this week.** The exams begin in early May for the resit student and in May or June for Year 12. Ten weeks out is when the run-in gets built, not two, because a run-in built late is a cram with a nicer name. The plan is a grid: every topic's final passes dated, the paper calendar, the rest days, the Easter fortnight, the last week before each exam.

**The number that should move.** The run-in plan exists: every topic has a dated final pass and the weekly capacity is respected on paper.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 40, build the run-in live.* The real exam dates from the board's timetable. Count backwards. Topics down the side, weeks across the top, weakest topics with the most passes. The paper calendar: one or two a week per subject. Easter as the heavy fortnight, six-hour days with the cap and three deep blocks. The week before each exam: retrieval and papers only. The day-before rule from session 19.
- *40 to 55, this week,* which is week one of the run-in.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The run-in plan on one page.

**Between-session work.** Run-in week one.

**Deviate if.** Year 12 exams are in June: the run-in is fourteen weeks and lighter, and because summer-term content is still arriving, the plan is a hybrid of keeping up and running in.

**Year 12 note.** Build it around the school's exam week, which they should ask for now.
**Resit note.** Build it around the board's exact dates and the gaps between papers.

## Session 23, week of 1 March: The student runs the session

**Why this week.** The test of independence. They present the scorecard, diagnose themselves, choose the teach they think they need, and set the week. You only correct. If they can do this, the system is theirs. If they cannot, you find out now, with ten weeks left, rather than in the exam hall.

**The number that should move.** Their self-diagnosis against yours: how many points agree. The week they set against the week you would have set.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 45, they run it.* You write notes silently and intervene only on errors that would cost them the week.
- *45 to 55, your corrections,* out loud, briefly.
- *55 to 60, two one-liners:* theirs, and yours on what you would have changed.

**What they walk away with.** Proof they can self-coach, and a short list of what they missed.

**Between-session work.** The week they set, with your corrections.

**Deviate if.** They cannot run it: the session becomes a diagnosis of what they missed, and the self-run is repeated in session 25.

## Session 24, week of 8 March: Month 6 review, the before and after

**Why this week.** The six-month page: week one against week twenty-four on every line. The decision to continue into the run-in is made with the exams on the calendar in front of them, and the parent gets the biggest update of the programme.

**The number that should move.** The six-month page. Run-in week two. The continue decision, recorded.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 35, the six-month review,* built together: hours split, Struggling count, papers and grade equivalents, school evidence, and all twenty-four "what changed" lines read back in order.
- *35 to 50, the run-in with you and without you.* With: weekly paper debriefs, the exam-week protocols, the results-day plan. Without: they have every tool, and here is the risk (the bad-week spiral, nobody reading the papers). Say both honestly. The programme's own numbers make the case; you do not have to.
- *50 to 55, run-in week two.*
- *55 to 60, the one line.* Parent update 6 tonight, with the six-month page attached.

**What they walk away with.** The six-month page.

**Between-session work.** Run-in week two.

**Deviate if.** The numbers are not there: the review is still honest, the last ten weeks are the plan that closes the gap, and a student who has not done the work gets the plain conversation. You also get the data on who this programme is for.

## Session 25, week of 15 March: The bad-week protocol, and the second self-run

**Why this week.** The run-in will contain a week where the student is ill, exhausted, or has a terrible paper. Without a protocol a bad week becomes a bad month. And if session 23 showed gaps, the self-run is repeated here.

**The number that should move.** The bad-week protocol written on the scorecard. This week's paper.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 30, the protocol.* A bad week shrinks to: the returns kept (never dropped), one paper, one log retest, and nothing else, with no guilt and no catching up afterwards. The rule: "You don't make up a bad week. You restart the plan on Monday."
- *30 to 55, the second self-run* if needed; otherwise the paper debrief and the week.
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The bad-week protocol on a card.

**Between-session work.** Run-in week three, the paper.

**Deviate if.** No gaps in session 23: give the whole second half to the paper debrief and the A* gap page.

## Session 26, week of 22 March: The Easter fortnight and the exam-week protocols

**Why this week.** Easter is the heaviest fortnight of the year and the last long stretch before the exams. It needs the free-week protocol at full scale. And the exam-week protocols, written now so nothing is decided on the day: the night before, the morning, and between papers.

**The number that should move.** The Easter plan built. This week's paper. The exam-week card written.

**The sixty minutes.**
- *0 to 10, scorecard.*
- *10 to 35, the Easter plan.* Six-hour days with the cap, three deep blocks, a paper every other day per subject, two full days off, the returns kept, the A* gap fixes each given a day.
- *35 to 50, the exam-week card.* The night before and the morning (from session 19), and the rule between papers: no post-mortems, the next paper's retrieval only, the phone away from the group chat that dissects the paper.
- *50 to 55, this week.*
- *55 to 60, the one line.*

**What they walk away with.** The Easter plan, the exam-week card.

**Between-session work.** The Easter fortnight, with one check-in message from you in the middle.

**Year 12 note.** Easter is also when the summer-term content plan gets folded in.
**Resit note.** The Easter fortnight is their last full-scale revision block. Make it the best-planned two weeks of the year.

## The run-in: sessions 27 to 36, 12 April to the exams (sketched, to be written in full in February)

Write these properly in February, after you have watched two real students through phases one to four. Until then, each week has one shape: the paper debrief plus the week's targeting, with one protocol layered on top.

| Session | Week of | The teach layered on the paper debrief | The number |
|---|---|---|---|
| 27 | 12 Apr | The Easter debrief: what held, what the fortnight's papers say, the fixes for the last four weeks | Papers over Easter, grade equivalents |
| 28 | 19 Apr | Peak-week capacity: the hardest fortnight without burning out, sleep as revision | Started on time, hours within cap |
| 29 | 26 Apr | The last-week-before-the-first-exam protocol, rehearsed: retrieval only, papers only, the card | First exam date on the scorecard |
| 30 | 3 May | Exam season begins (resit): between-papers rule live. Year 12: the end-of-year exams four to six weeks out, run-in in full | Panic entries, the between-papers rule kept |
| 31 | 10 May | Mid-season debrief without post-mortems: only what changes the next paper | Next paper's targeting |
| 32 | 17 May | Year 12: the end-of-year exam run-in's last fortnight. Resit: holding the routine | Paper equivalents |
| 33 | 24 May | Half term: the free-week protocol at exam intensity | Hours within cap, two days off kept |
| 34 | 31 May | Year 12 exams in progress: the exam-week card live. Resit: the final papers | The card kept, sleep kept |
| 35 | 7 Jun | The last papers. What to do the day after: nothing | Done |
