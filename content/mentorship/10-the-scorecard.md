# The scorecard, in full

*Written 30 September 2026, because twelve students are running and the scorecard is not. This replaces the scorecard in file 03, which had twenty seven columns and was built around a session 1 that has since changed. Everything below is built on the four tier mark loss analysis you actually run.*

## What it is, and why it is the product

One shared spreadsheet per student. The student fills in a row before every session. You read it in sixty seconds at the top of the session, and once a month it becomes a chart and a parent email.

It is not admin. Three things in the business are impossible without it.

**Retention.** A parent renewing at month four is not buying more sessions, they are buying the line on the chart. A student who can see marks lost to exam technique fall from 14 to 4 across five months does not cancel. Without the scorecard you are asking them to renew on a feeling, and feelings in March are worse than feelings in October.

**The clinic.** File 09 puts eight students in a room with four hot seats of about eleven minutes. That only works if the student arrives with their numbers already on a screen. A hot seat where you spend six minutes asking what they did last fortnight is not a hot seat, it is a slow private call with an audience.

**A coach, later.** A coach can run a diagnosis from a scorecard. A coach cannot run one from what you remember about a student.

## The one rule

**The student fills it in, never you.** Before the session, not during it. It takes them under ten minutes if the fields are the right ones, and it does three jobs at once: it moves preparation off you, it forces the student to look at their own week honestly, and it makes them the owner of their own evidence. A student who fills in their own numbers argues with the numbers, which is exactly the conversation you want.

This is also why the sheet below is short. The twenty seven column version in file 03 was a spreadsheet nobody completes. Every field here earns its place by feeding the diagnosis, the chart or the parent email. If a field stops doing that, cut it.

## Part one: the header block

Filled in once, at signup, mostly by you on the first call. It sits at the top of the sheet and never changes.

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Name, year and track | Year 12, Year 13 or Resit. The track decides the calendar. |
| Subjects, boards, spec links | The actual spec PDF URL per subject. You will use these every week. |
| Current grades and target grades | Per subject. From their own evidence, not their hopes. |
| The reason behind the target | Their words, verbatim. Medicine, a course, a parent, a rival. You read this back in month three when they wobble, and it is the most useful sentence on the sheet. |
| Exam dates | Real dates from the board timetable. |
| School test and mock dates | Ask the school. Most students do not know them. |
| Baseline four tier split | From session 1. Marks lost to Knowledge, Recall, Application, Exam technique, as counts and percentages, with the paper named. |
| Diagnostic profile and five scores | If they took the revision diagnostic. Optional. |
| Contact rules agreed | Date. So there is no argument later about the reply window. |
| Parent name and email | Where the monthly update goes. |
| Resit only: exam centre and entry deadline | Plus the date entry was confirmed in writing. |

## Part two: the weekly row

Eight fields. The student fills these in before every session.

| Field | What it is | Where the student gets it |
|---|---|---|
| `date` | Week ending | |
| `sessions_planned` / `sessions_done` | Study sessions from their plan, and how many actually happened | Their week plan, ticked |
| `hours_high` / `hours_low` | Hours testing themselves (blurt, recall, papers, log retests) against hours consuming (reading, rewriting, watching) | Their own log, one letter per block |
| `paper` | Name of the paper or section sat this week | |
| `marks_lost_K` / `_R` / `_A` / `_E` | The four tier tally on that paper. This is the important one, and it is explained in full below. | Marking the paper, then classifying each lost mark |
| `paper_pct` | Score as a percentage | Score divided by max |
| `errors_added` / `_cleared` | Error log entries created this week, and entries that passed a third clean retest | Their error log |
| `rung1_topics` | Topics they can now produce from a blank page with no prompt | Their topic list, rated by the retrieval ladder (file 08, session 4) |
| `what_changed` | One sentence, their words | They write it, you never write it for them |

That is it. Roughly eight minutes for a student who has already marked their paper, because the four tier tally is the only new work and it takes five minutes.

**Adherence is the field everyone underestimates.** `sessions_done` over `sessions_planned` as a percentage is the earliest warning you get. A student who drops under about half is not a student who needs the next technique, they are a student who needs the conversation about why, and the scorecard tells you that a fortnight before the paper scores do.

## The four tier tally, in detail

This is the heart of it, so teach it properly once and the student does it unaided forever.

After they mark a paper against the mark scheme, they go back over every mark they dropped and put it in exactly one bucket. Give them this decision tree, in this order, and tell them to stop at the first yes.

1. **Did I simply not know this content?** If they had never learned it or could not have explained it even with the book open, it is **Knowledge**.
2. **Did I know it, but fail to produce it under pressure?** They knew it, they would recognise it instantly, it would not come out on the page. That is **Recall**.
3. **Did I know it and could recall it, but fail to apply it to this question?** Familiar content in an unfamiliar context, the wrong method chosen, no idea where to start. That is **Application**.
4. **Did I have the right idea and lose the mark anyway?** Wrong wording, missed the command word, misread the stem, ran out of time, no units, no working. That is **Exam technique**.

Four rules that keep the data honest:

- **One bucket per mark, never two.** The order above resolves ties, and it resolves them upstream: a mark that fails both Recall and Exam technique is a Recall mark, because fixing the wording of something you could not remember changes nothing.
- **Be harsh.** Students want everything to be Exam technique, because it feels like bad luck rather than a gap. "I knew that really" is a Recall mark, not an Exam technique mark. Say that out loud the first time.
- **Count marks, not questions.** A six mark question where they got three is three lost marks, and they may not all land in the same bucket.
- **Record the total.** Marks lost matters as much as the mix, for the reason in the chart section below.

The first time, do it with them on the call. The second time, they do it and you check. From the third time it is theirs.

## Part three: the monthly page and the chart

Once a month (sessions 5, 9, 13, 17 in the file 08 numbering, or every fourth hot seat in the clinic) the student sits a **benchmark paper**: a full paper of comparable format and difficulty to the session 1 one. That is the formal data point. The weekly tallies show the trend in between, but the benchmark is the fair comparison, because papers vary and a flattering paper flatters the chart.

**The chart.** One stacked bar per benchmark, one bar per month, side by side.

- The height of the whole bar is total marks lost.
- The four segments are Knowledge, Recall, Application, Exam technique, in that order every time.
- One fixed colour per tier, identical on every chart forever, taken from the brand palette so the parent email matches the site.

**Why stacked absolute marks and not percentages.** This matters and it is the mistake to avoid. If a student loses 40 marks with 30 percent on exam technique, then improves to 20 marks lost still with 30 percent on exam technique, the percentage chart shows a flat line and the student concludes nothing changed. The absolute chart shows the bar halving. Show percentages underneath as a second row of numbers if you like, but the picture is the stacked bar, because the picture is what gets remembered.

**What a good chart looks like over six months.** The total bar falls steadily. Knowledge shrinks first and usually fastest, because it is the easiest to fix and it is the one school is also working on. Recall falls through months two and three as retrieval installs. Exam technique falls fastest of all once you start teaching it, because it converts immediately. Application is the stubborn one and it is the B to A jump, so it is often the last segment standing, and that is a normal and explainable shape rather than a failure.

**The monthly page** is the chart, plus the paper percentage trend, plus adherence, plus three sentences on what changed and one line on what next month adds. That page is the attachment on the parent email, unchanged.

## How you read it in sixty seconds

In a hot seat or the top of a session, four numbers in this order:

1. **Adherence.** Did the week happen? If not, stop here. This is the session.
2. **Marks lost, total.** Falling, flat or up?
3. **The mix.** Which segment is biggest now, and is it the one you have been working on?
4. **Their sentence.** What they think changed, which is often not what the numbers say, and the gap between the two is the most interesting thing on the page.

Then diagnose. That is the whole read, and it is why eight students fit in an hour.

## Building it

**One Google Sheet per student.** Not one workbook with a tab each, because the student needs edit access to their own and must not see anyone else's. Share it with the student as an editor and, if the parent wants it, with the parent as a viewer.

Two tabs in each sheet: **Weekly** (the header block at the top, then one row per week) and **Benchmarks** (one row per benchmark paper, which is what the chart is built from). Build the chart once on the Benchmarks tab and it updates itself forever.

**One master tracker for you.** A separate sheet, one row per student, that you update in thirty seconds after each hot seat: name, tier, start date, MRR, last benchmark date, marks lost at last benchmark, adherence, next hot seat date, flag. Do not try to make it pull from the student sheets with cross-file formulas; at this size that is fragile and manual is faster. This sheet is also your renewal radar, because a falling adherence number three weeks running is the earliest cancellation signal you will get.

Two CSV files are in this folder, ready to import straight into Sheets as a new sheet: [scorecard-template.csv](scorecard-template.csv) and [student-tracker.csv](student-tracker.csv).

## Installing it this week, with twelve students already running

You cannot ask twelve students to backfill three weeks, and you should not try.

1. **Set up one sheet per student today.** Header block filled by you from your session notes. Ten minutes each, two hours total, once.
2. **The baseline is the session 1 four tier split.** If you wrote it down for a student, use it and date it. If you did not, do not invent it: their next benchmark paper becomes the baseline and the chart starts from there. A baseline set in week four is completely fine. A fabricated one is not, and it will fall apart the first time a parent asks about it.
3. **Send it with the framing that it is theirs, not yours.** Something close to: "I'm sending you the thing that shows your progress. Fill it in before each session, takes ten minutes, and in a month you'll see your own chart." Never call it admin or a form.
4. **First week, one row only.** No history, no catch up.
5. **Teach the four tier tally in their next session**, on their own marked paper. Ten minutes, once, and they own it.
6. **Then hold the line.** From the following week, the session starts with the scorecard on screen. Every time. The habit is set in the first three weeks or it never sets at all.

## When a student does not fill it in

Treat it as data, not as rudeness. It usually means one of three things, and each has a different fix.

- **They did not do the week.** The scorecard is empty because there is nothing to put in it. This is the consistency conversation, and it is the most valuable session you will run with that student.
- **They did the week but not the paper.** No paper means no tally. Get the paper back into the week before anything else, because the paper is the measurement instrument.
- **They forgot.** Send the link an hour before the session with one line. If it happens twice, it is not forgetting, it is the first case in disguise.

Never fill it in for them to keep the session moving. The moment you do, it becomes your admin instead of their evidence, and it will quietly die.

## What it unlocks later

- **The renewal conversation at month four** stops being a sales conversation and becomes reading a chart together.
- **Testimonials and case studies** write themselves from five benchmark bars and a parent's reply.
- **A coach** can pick up a student cold from the sheet plus the session notes.
- **The clinic** works at eight students because every hot seat starts with the numbers already up.
- **Your own product decisions** get real data: across twelve students you will see which tier moves fastest, which teach precedes the biggest drop, and which profile of student churns. None of that exists without the sheet.
