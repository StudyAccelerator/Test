# From "life as an F2" to "sign up for my free workshop", without the gear change

For the KCL work experience workshop with The Medic Life, Saturday 24 October 2026, 350 students. Written 23 October 2026. Read it the night before, say it out loud once, then speak it in your own words. Nothing here needs saying verbatim except the URL.

## The one idea

Do not transition from your talk to a sale. Transition from your talk to a **confession and a gift**. The confession is how you nearly did not get here. The gift is the free workshop. If the A-level bit is the natural ending of the F2 story, there is no gear change to feel, because the room hears one story about one person.

The rule for selling a free thing to 350 teenagers: they are not deciding whether it is worth money, they are deciding whether it is worth their attention and whether it is for people like them. So you win on three things: it feels like it was made for the person in the seat, the next step takes 30 seconds, and there is an honest reason to do it right now rather than later.

## Part 1: plant the seed in your first two minutes

Before you say a word about F2, take the room's temperature with hands up. It wakes them up, it tells you who is in the room, and it opens a loop you close at the end.

> Quick show of hands before I start. Who's in Year 12? Year 13? Anyone in Year 11? Who wants to do medicine or dentistry? Okay. And one more, and be honest: who feels like they're working hard right now, but the grades don't quite match the hours yet?
>
> Keep that hand up in your head. I was that student, and I'll come back to you at the end.

Now do your F2 talk exactly as planned.

## Part 2: three anchors to drop inside the F2 talk

You do not need to change the talk. You need three sentences inside it that the ending will call back to. Use whichever fit where you were going anyway.

**Anchor 1, when you talk about a busy shift or a night on call.**
> The thing nobody tells you about being a doctor is that you can't do everything, so you learn to triage. Chest pain before the sprained ankle, every time. Keep that word in your head, triage, because it's going to come back.

**Anchor 2, when you talk about exams in medicine (finals, the endless assessments, the portfolio).**
> Medicine is basically exams that never stop. What saved me wasn't working harder than everyone else. It was that somewhere in sixth form I'd worked out how to learn, and most people never do.

**Anchor 3, when you talk about seeing a patient.**
> We never treat before we diagnose. It sounds obvious, but hold on to it, because almost every student I meet does the opposite with their revision.

## Part 3: the pivot (about 90 seconds)

This is the join. It comes at the natural end of the F2 story, in the slot where most speakers say "so that's life as an F2, any questions". Slow down here. Drop your voice a bit. It is a confession, so it should sound like one.

> Right. I said I'd come back to the people who put their hand up at the start, the ones working hard without the grades to show for it yet.
>
> Here's the honest bit, and it's the reason I'm standing here. I nearly didn't get into medicine. In Year 12 I was an average student. I was working harder than most of my mates, and my grades were sitting in the middle, and the hours just kept going up while the grades stayed put.
>
> What changed wasn't effort. I had plenty of effort. What changed was realising that studying is a skill with a method, exactly like taking a history or reading an ECG, and that nobody had ever taught me the method. School had taught me what to learn. Nobody had taught me how.
>
> I rebuilt how I studied, I finished top of my year, and I got in. And for the last six years, alongside the day job, I've taught that system to over a thousand A-level students. On average, they jump two grades.
>
> The Medic Life have very kindly said I can tell you about what I do. So I'm going to, but I'm not going to try and sell you anything today. Instead I'd rather just give you the first lesson. All of it. For free.

That last paragraph does the work. You have named the sponsorship openly, which removes the awkwardness, and then immediately turned it into a gift. Nobody in the room is being sold to, so nobody puts their guard up.

## Part 4: the offer (about two minutes)

Now be specific. Vague free things get ignored; specific free things get signed up for. Say what it is, when it is, what they will leave with, who it is for, and then show the QR code. Have the slide ready to click to on the word "workshop".

> Next Saturday, the 31st of October, 1pm, I'm running a free online workshop. Ninety minutes on Zoom. It's called the Free Academic Accelerator Workshop, and it's specifically about getting ahead of Year 12 without burning out by February, which is when it hits most people.
>
> You'll leave with four things. One: a priority map, which of your subjects should actually get your hours this term and why, because almost everyone splits their time equally and that's exactly how the weakest subject stays weak until mocks. Two: the high-yield topics in each of your subjects, the triage I was talking about earlier, what carries the marks and what eats your evenings for nothing. Three: a week that doesn't collapse, how many hours Year 12 actually needs and where they go. And four: how to learn, not just what. The method. The thing nobody teaches.
>
> If you're in Year 12, this is for you, this is your year. Year 11, this is the head start before you arrive, so you walk in already knowing how to do it. Year 13, the prioritising and the method go straight into your mocks, so come. And bring your parents. Genuinely. The plan is far easier to keep when the people at home have heard it too, and they ask the best questions.
>
> Everyone who signs up gets the replay, so if you can't make 1pm on Saturday, sign up anyway.

Then the QR slide, and the most important 60 seconds of the day.

> Phones out, everyone. Scan that, or type alevelaccelerators dot com slash K C L. It takes about thirty seconds: your first name, your email, your year and your subjects. There's an optional box if you want to join a free WhatsApp community where I answer study questions between now and then. Tick it if you want it, leave it if you don't.
>
> I'm going to give you a minute right now to do it, because I know what happens if you leave it until later. Hands up when you're in.

Then actually wait. Count the hands. Say something about the number ("that's a lot of hands, brilliant"). Hands going up is social proof for the people still deciding, and it is the only moment all day when 350 people have their phones out for you.

## Part 5: the honest reason to do it now

Students need a reason to act in the room rather than "later". Use the ones that are true for you, and only those. Candidates, in order of strength:

1. **The Zoom room has a cap.** Check your plan before tomorrow. If it is 100 participants and you have 200 sign-ups, the cap is real and you should say so ("the Zoom room holds a hundred, so it's first come first served"). If your plan allows 500, do not claim a cap.
2. **Your rota.** If this is genuinely the only one you can run before Christmas, say that. "I'm an F2, so this is the one I can run this side of Christmas."
3. **The replay only goes to people who signed up.** Always true, always worth saying.
4. **The WhatsApp community is free advice they can start using tonight.** True once the link exists.

Never invent scarcity. The room includes future medics and they can smell it.

## Part 6: the hand-off

- Make the QR slide your **last slide**, so it stays on the screen during questions and after you finish.
- Ask The Medic Life host, before you go on, to put the QR back up at the next break and to mention it at the end of the day. Hosts always say yes.
- Say the URL aloud twice. Lecture theatre Wi-Fi is bad and some phones will not scan. "alevelaccelerators dot com, slash, K, C, L."
- In the break, when students come up to talk to you (they will), end each chat with "have you signed up yet? do it now while I'm here".

Free, ninety minutes, online, I'll teach you how to prioritise your subjects and how to actually learn. Scan the code or go to alevelaccelerators dot com slash K C L. Bring your parents. See you Saturday.

## Things that kill sign-ups

- **Apologising for it.** "Sorry, quick plug" tells the room it is a plug. You are giving them something.
- **"If you're interested."** Nobody is interested until you have made them interested. Say "this is for you" to Year 12 and mean it.
- **A ten second QR code.** The slide needs to be up for a full minute with you waiting, and again at the end.
- **Making it sound like tutoring.** No subjects, no prices, no programme names from the stage. One workshop, one date, four outcomes.
- **Splitting the room.** Do not talk about GCSE or "if you're not doing A-levels". Everyone in the room gets one message.
- **Talking over the signup.** When you say "phones out", stop talking. Let them do it.
