{$name},

There's a quiet disaster happening in most students' revision, and it's this: the topics they learned last week are disappearing while they learn this week's.

Memory fades fast at first, then slower. A topic you rebuilt on Tuesday is mostly gone within days unless you come back to it, and "come back to it" is exactly what a normal timetable never schedules, because new content always feels more urgent than old content. So students pour effort in at the front while it drains out the back, and then wonder why mock revision feels like starting from zero.

That's what the 30 minute Spaced Review block on your tracker is for. It's the smallest block on your plan and it might be the most valuable one, because it protects everything the bigger blocks built.

How to run it: the block will sit a few days after you covered its topics, which is deliberate, that's the window where a cheap top-up still works. Book closed, re-blurt the topic or answer a handful of questions on it from memory. Most of it will come back, and the act of pulling it back is what resets the fading. Whatever's gone missing gets patched on the spot, in the second colour, and anything that's gone missing twice earns a line in the error log.

Thirty minutes. That's the entire cost of keeping a topic, versus the hours of re-learning it from scratch in November.

And this is why I keep saying trust the tracker's plan even when it looks odd. When it schedules a review of something you did last week instead of more of the new thing, it's not wasting your time. It's stopping last week's hours from evaporating. The students who seem to remember everything at mocks aren't cleverer. Their old topics just never fully faded, because something kept bringing them back at the right moments. For you, that something is the plan on your wall.

Today: run what's scheduled, and if it's a Spaced Review, notice how much comes back. That feeling of "oh, it's still there" is your evidence the system's working.

Keep going!
