Your whole tracker week was built from three little words: Struggling, Shaky, Solid. The weakest topics got the most time. Which means the plan is exactly as good as your honesty was, and honesty about our own knowledge is where every human brain cheats.

Here's the cheat in action. You look at a topic, you recognise it, the recognition feels like knowledge, and you click Solid. But recognising a topic on a list and producing it in an exam are different skills. The exam only pays for the second one. Every topic flattered from Shaky up to Solid quietly steals its sessions and gives them to something that needed them less. The plan still looks perfect on the wall. It's just aimed slightly wrong.

The 10 second honesty test, for any topic you're about to rate: could I write half a page on this right now, book closed, and get it mostly right? Not "have I seen it". Not "did we do it in class". Produce it, now, from nothing. If the honest answer is "probably not", that's Shaky, whatever your notes look like. If the honest answer makes you slightly nervous, that's Struggling, and it just earned its Blurt and Fix.

Nobody sees these ratings but you. There are no marks for a flattering dashboard. The entire return on the tracker comes from telling it the truth, and the students who improve fastest are the ones whose first week looks worst on paper, because they were honest enough to aim the hours where they were needed.

And when Sunday comes, re-rate before you build next week. Topics you rebuilt this week move up. Anything that faded moves back down without shame, that's just the curve doing what curves do, and next week's plan gets built on fresh evidence instead of last week's optimism.

Today's move: your scheduled block, plus one glance back at your ratings. If one of them was flattering you, demote it now. Ten seconds, and next Sunday's plan gets sharper.

Keep going!
