Week one of any system runs on novelty. Week two is where it actually gets decided, because novelty is gone and the plan has met reality: the session that got skipped, the topic that took longer than its block, the Wednesday that disappeared entirely. If your week went imperfectly, congratulations, you're running a real system, not a fantasy one.

The move now is the 15 minute Sunday rebuild, and it's the highest-yield quarter hour of your week.

**First, re-rate your topics with fresh eyes.** Anything you rebuilt with a Blurt and Fix probably moves up a level. Anything that faded moves back down, no shame attached, fading is what memory does when returns get missed. The honesty rule from my last email applies double on Sundays.

**Second, deal with the parked topics.** If the tracker parked topics last week, they weren't cancelled, they were queued. They come first in the new week. The cap that parked them is the same cap that kept your week achievable, so trust it: a plan that fits gets done, and a plan that impresses gets abandoned by Thursday.

**Third, rebuild the week around what's actually coming.** New test announced? That subject's topics get re-rated with it in mind. Sports fixture eating Saturday? Tell the tracker your real availability and let it fit the plan around the truth. The plan serves your week, never the other way round.

Then print or screenshot the new week, up on the wall over the old one. There's something quietly satisfying about a stack of finished weeks, and by October that stack is the evidence that you're a student who runs a system. Very few people get to say that.
