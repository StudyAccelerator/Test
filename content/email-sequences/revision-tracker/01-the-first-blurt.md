Somewhere on your week there's a block called Blurt and Fix, 90 minutes, sitting on one of your weakest topics. Today, exactly what to do inside it, because "revise the topic" is precisely the vagueness the tracker exists to kill.

**Minutes 0 to 10: the blurt.** Blank page, book closed, phone in another room. Write everything you know about the topic. Definitions, diagrams, mechanisms, half-remembered fragments, all of it. Ugly is correct. You're not making notes, you're taking an X-ray of what's actually in your head.

**Minutes 10 to 15: the mark.** Open the notes or textbook and check your page like an examiner. Tick what's right. Everything missing or wrong goes back on the page in a second colour. That second colour is the whole point: it's a map of exactly what this topic needs, drawn from evidence instead of vibes.

**Minutes 15 to 70: the fix.** Work through the second-colour material properly. Not by re-reading it, by rebuilding it: work the examples, draw the diagram from scratch, explain the mechanism out loud like you're teaching it. Out loud matters. If you can say it to an empty room, you can write it in an exam hall.

**Minutes 70 to 90: the proof.** Book closed again. Fresh page. Blurt the topic one more time, and watch how much of the second colour made the jump into memory. Whatever still didn't stick is tomorrow's ten minute warm-up, and knowing that is a win, not a failure.

That's the session. Notice what's missing: no highlighting, no copying notes out neatly, no four hour marathon. One topic, one honest rebuild, proof at the end.

Your move today is whatever block your plan has scheduled. If it's a Blurt and Fix, you now know exactly how to run it. If today's a rest day, enjoy it with a clear conscience. Rest is on the timetable because it's part of the system, not a break from it.

Keep going!
