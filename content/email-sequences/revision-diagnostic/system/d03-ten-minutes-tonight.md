{$name},

Quick one tonight, because the task is quick.

The day before yesterday I asked you to blurt one topic: blank page, book closed, gaps in a second colour. If you did it, that page has an appointment tonight. If you didn't, do the blurt tonight instead and read the rest of this email tomorrow. Either way you're on the plan, and there's no guilt attached. Guilt doesn't help anyone revise.

**Tonight's session: the day 2 return. Ten minutes, all in.**

Take the topic from two days ago. Fresh page, book still closed. Write out what you remember, and pay special attention to the second-colour material, the stuff that wasn't there the first time.

Then check. Most of it will be there now. That's not luck, that's just how memory works: you pulled it out once, so pulling it out again tonight is easier. Anything still missing goes back in the second colour and gets an extra thirty seconds of attention.

Ten minutes. That's the whole session.

Here's why this tiny session matters more than it looks. Right now, the topic is still fresh, so keeping it costs you ten minutes. Skip tonight and come back on Saturday, and you won't be topping up a fresh memory, you'll be rescuing a faded one, and that costs far more time for the same result. Same topic, same brain, completely different price depending on when you come back.

And that's the whole trick behind students who seem to remember everything. They're not trying harder to remember. They're coming back at the right moments, when it's cheap.

Do your ten minutes, then take tomorrow off from this topic. The next return isn't due until around day 5, and rest is part of the system, not a break from it.

Keep going!
