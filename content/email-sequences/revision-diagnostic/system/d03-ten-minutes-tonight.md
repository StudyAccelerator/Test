Quick one tonight, because the task is quick.

The day before yesterday I asked you to blurt one topic: blank page, book closed, gaps in a second colour. If you did it, that page has an appointment tonight. If you didn't, do the blurt tonight instead and read the rest of this email tomorrow. Either way you're on the plan, no guilt required. Guilt is a terrible study partner.

**Tonight's session: the day 2 return. Ten minutes, all in.**

Take yesterday's topic. Fresh page. Book still closed. Write out what you remember, paying special attention to the second-colour stuff, the material that wasn't there first time.

Then check. Most of it will be there now. That's not a fluke, that's the mechanism: you pulled it out of memory once, so the pathway is thicker, so tonight's pull is easier. Anything still missing goes back in the second colour and gets thirty extra seconds of attention.

Ten minutes. That's the whole session.

Here's why this tiny session matters more than it looks. The day 2 return is where the forgetting curve is steepest, which makes tonight the cheapest this topic will ever be to keep. Skip it and by Saturday you're not topping up a warm memory, you're rescuing a cold one, and the rescue costs five times the top-up. Same topic, same brain, wildly different price depending on the date.

That's the entire trick behind students who "just seem to remember everything". They're not remembering harder. They're returning cheaper.

Do your ten minutes, then put tomorrow off duty: the next return isn't until around day 5, and rest is part of the system, not a break from it.

Keep going!
