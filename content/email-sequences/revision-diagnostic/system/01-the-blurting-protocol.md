Hi {$name},

Your report flagged {$diag_bottleneck|default('the way you revise')} as your biggest leak. So I want to start with the method that fixes it fastest.

It's called blurting. You might have seen it on TikTok. But almost no one does it right, and doing it right is the whole difference.

First, why it works. You'll stick with a method you get, and drop one you're taking on faith.

When you re-read your notes, the content starts to feel familiar. And familiar feels just like knowing it. Same warm feeling of "yes, I know this".

But the exam never asks if you'd know the cardiac cycle when you saw it. It gives you a blank answer box and asks you to produce it from nothing.

Spotting it and producing it are two skills, and they're not the same skill. School only ever trained the first one.

That's where "I revised loads and it went badly" comes from. And to be honest, it's not a you problem, because no one taught you this.

There's only one way to get better at pulling things out of your head. That's practising pulling things out of your head. Blurting is just that, done right.

**Step 1: Pick one small topic**
"Biology" is not a topic. "The cardiac cycle" is. Go smaller than feels normal.

**Step 2: Blank page, book closed, phone in another room**
Write down all you know about the topic. Key terms, diagrams, equations, half-remembered bits.

It's meant to look messy. Speed matters more than neatness. Give it five to ten minutes.

**Step 3: Check it honestly**
Stop, open your notes, and check your page. Tick all the bits you got right.

Anything missing or wrong goes back on the page in a second colour. That second colour is the whole point. It shows you what you don't know yet, which no revision guide can tell you.

**Step 4: The part everyone skips**
Close it all again. Re-blurt just the second colour bits, book closed, right now, not tomorrow.

This is where "I saw the right answer" becomes "I can produce the right answer". It only takes a few minutes.

**Step 5: Keep the page and date it**
You'll come back to this topic tomorrow for ten minutes, and again in about four days.

In a couple of days I'll show you how to book those returns in, so they really happen. There's a trick to it: your calendar does the remembering, not your willpower.

**The two rules that keep it honest**

Each session ends with the book closed. Whatever you studied, the last ten minutes are you writing out the core of it, book closed.

If you can't produce it now, three metres from your notes, you couldn't produce it in an exam hall in June. It's better to find that out tonight.

And each wrong answer earns a line in an error log. One page per subject: the question, your wrong answer, and the right one next to it. Retest the log on Sundays.

Wrong answers you've fixed are the highest-yield stuff you own. Each one is a mark you've already lost once.

That's it. No highlighters, no four hour note-making sessions, no fancy apps. A pen, a messy page, and finding out what's really in your head.

That part is humbling, and it's also why it works.

**Tonight, try this:** one topic, one blurt, both colours, the second pass. Twenty-five minutes, all in.

Then reply to this email with one word for your first blurt page. Mine, at 17, would have been "humbling" :)

Keep going!
