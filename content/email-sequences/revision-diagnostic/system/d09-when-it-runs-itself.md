{$name},

I want to describe an evening to you, because it's the real point of all the method I've sent you this week, and it's easy to lose behind the mechanics.

It's a Tuesday in October. At 6pm you sit down, and there's no negotiation, because tonight's topic was decided on Sunday. You run the session: a blurt, the fix, book closed at the end to prove it stuck. Forty-five minutes. Then a ten minute return on Thursday's topic, because the calendar said so, and it comes back easily, because day 2 returns always do.

And then, at some point before 8pm, you're done. Actually done. Not "stopping because I can't face any more", not "stopping but feeling guilty about the things I didn't do". Done, because the plan said this was today's work and today's work happened. You close the books and your evening is yours, and there's no low hum of dread underneath it.

That's the part nobody tells you about fixing your method. Everyone talks about grades. But the first thing that actually changes is the feeling of your weeks: revision stops being this endless, shapeless thing you're always behind on, and becomes a bounded thing you do and finish. The guilt goes. The Sunday night panic goes. And because the topics you learn actually stay learned, the panic doesn't come creeping back at mocks.

None of that needs me, by the way. It's the system you already have: decided in advance, blurted, returned to, capped. What it needs is three honest weeks of running, and week three is the hard one.

So tonight's move is unglamorous on purpose: run today's session, whatever your plan says it is, and stop when it's done. The October version of your evenings is built out of exactly that.

Keep going!

Waleed
