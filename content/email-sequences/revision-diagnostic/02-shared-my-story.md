Hi {$name},

A week of me sending you drills and methods, so today let me tell you who I actually was at your age, because it's the whole reason these emails exist.

At 17, I was the hard worker. The one teachers liked. I did the hours, I paid attention, and I made notes so neat they could have been framed. If effort was what got graded, I'd have been untouchable.

And in exams, I kept leaving marks behind. I was the student who finished every paper the second the examiner said put your pencils down, feeling like I'd emptied everything I had onto the pages, and then getting results that didn't match what I knew. Nobody could tell me why. The advice was always the same, which was work harder, so I did, and to be honest it mostly made me more tired, not better.

Here's the part that took me years to see. Nothing was wrong with me. I was spending huge hours on things that felt like revising, like re-reading, rewriting and making everything neat, and almost none of it was training the two skills exams actually test: pulling knowledge out of my head under pressure, and putting it down in the exact words that earn marks. Nobody had taught me that, and to be honest nobody teaches anyone that.

I found out the hard way at medical school, where the volume of content is so big that inefficient studying simply doesn't survive. I had to learn how learning actually works: testing myself instead of re-reading, coming back to material before it fades, and working to the marking criteria. And the frustrating thing was how simple it all was. Everything I'd needed at 17 could have been taught to me in a fortnight.

So that's what I do now, alongside being a doctor. Over the last six years I've worked with more than 1,000 A-level students, and the pattern is almost always mine: the effort is there, and nobody ever taught them where to point it.

That's what this fortnight of emails is, really. It's the two weeks of teaching I wish someone had given me, and your diagnostic result tells you which parts matter most for you.

Keep going with the plan. You're further ahead of 17 year old me than you probably realise :)

Waleed
