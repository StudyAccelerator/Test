{$name},

Two weeks ago you took a twenty question diagnostic. Since then I've sent you a drill, a dictionary, a marking method and a September plan. This is the last email of that run, so let's take stock of what you now own.

You can sit a timed section under real conditions and mark it the way an examiner would. You can sort every dropped mark into K, R, W or T, and you know what each letter tells you to do next. You can build a dictionary of the exact phrases your board pays for. And you know the calendar truth about autumn: predicted grades follow evidence, and the evidence gets written between September and November.

That's more exam craft than most students learn across two whole years. It's all free, it all works, and none of it needed me after the day you learned it.

From here, one email a week: something school never taught you, usable that evening. No daily emails, no countdown timers. If a cohort date or something genuinely time-bound comes up, I'll tell you plainly, once.

One last thing before the weekly rhythm starts. Reply to this email with your letter distribution, even a rough one. "Mostly W, some T, two K" is plenty. I'll write back with what I'd do next in one paragraph. Free, no strings, and it's honestly my favourite kind of reply to get: it means the drill is alive.

And if the letters pile up W after W and you want them trained properly, the September programme and the free call are both exactly where I said they'd be. No chasing from me. The door just stays open.

Keep going!

Waleed
