**"Isn't my predicted grade basically decided already?"**

Mostly not, and this is worth understanding properly. Predicted grades get set and reviewed in the autumn term, based on the evidence teachers have in front of them: early Year 13 tests, mocks, classwork. So a student who spends September to November producing exam-quality answers gives their teacher new evidence at exactly the right time. Predictions follow evidence, and autumn is when that evidence gets made. If you wait until after mocks to fix your exam skills, the predictions have usually already been written.

**"Will it clash with school, sport, life?"**

It's built not to. Maths is Saturday 1pm to 3pm, Biology Sunday 10am to 12pm, Chemistry Sunday 1pm to 3pm. Nothing touches the school week, and the subjects never overlap each other. If a weekend goes wrong, the session's recorded. Between sessions, budget two to three hours of independent work, which is mostly the drill you already run, now with coached answers feeding it.

**"I'm one person in a group. How does that fix MY wording?"**

Fair question, and the answer is in how the sessions are designed. Groups are kept small on purpose, and the sessions are built around real answers, yours included, being worked on against the scheme live. And here's the thing students don't expect: watching someone else's answer get re-worded teaches you almost as much as your own, because everyone makes surprisingly similar mistakes. The same missing "because", the same vague verb. Within two sessions you'll hear your own mistakes coming out of other people's answers, and that's when the wording starts fixing itself mid-exam.
