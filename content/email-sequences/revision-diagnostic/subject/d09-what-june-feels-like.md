Hi {$name},

I want to describe a feeling to you, because it's the actual point of everything I've sent you this week, and it gets lost when we only talk about drills and mark schemes.

It's June. You turn the paper over, and the first thing that happens is nothing. No cold jolt, no scanning for the questions you can do, because you've sat so many timed sections that a fresh paper is just another Tuesday, and your hands already know the routine.

You hit a six mark explain question and you don't write everything you know about the topic. You clock the command word, you build the chain, cause, because, effect, and you move on, knowing roughly what you just scored. That's the strange gift of training with the mark scheme open: you stop hoping about marks and start counting them, in the room, as you go.

And when time's called, you walk out without that awful corridor conversation where everyone reconstructs the paper and your stomach drops. Not because you were perfect, but because for once there's no gap between what you knew and what you wrote, and whatever the grade turns out to be, you left nothing behind in that hall.

That's what exam skills buy you: the end of losing marks on things you knew. And that feeling in June is built in the autumn, one harshly marked section at a time, exactly the way you've been practising this week.

So tonight's move is the same as it's been all week, and that's deliberate: run the drill, log the letters. The June version of you is just this version, plus repetitions :)

Keep going!
