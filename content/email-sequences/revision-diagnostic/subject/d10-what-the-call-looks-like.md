A few students have replied asking some version of the same thing: "what actually happens if I book the call?" Which is a polite way of asking "is this where the sales pitch lives?" Fair. Here's the agenda, so you can decide with the lights on.

**Minutes 0 to 5:** you talk, I listen. Your subjects, your year, what the diagnostic said, and what's actually going on when you sit a paper. If you've been running the drill, have your letter log open. If you haven't, that's fine too, come as you are.

**Minutes 5 to 20:** we read the evidence together. Your K, R, W, T distribution if you have one, your report if you don't. I tell you what I'd fix first and, just as usefully, what I'd ignore for now. Most students are trying to fix four things at once, which is why none of them move.

**Minutes 20 to 30:** you leave with a two week plan, written down. Which drill, which days, which topic first. Specific enough that Sunday-you knows exactly what Monday-you is doing.

That's the whole call. If the Subject Accelerator genuinely fits what your log shows, I'll say so in one sentence and you can ask about it or not. If it doesn't fit, I'll say that instead. I'm a doctor before I'm a business owner, and I'm not interested in prescribing things people don't need.

**Who shouldn't book:** if you haven't done the diagnostic's plan or a single timed question yet, and you know you won't before the call, spend your 30 minutes on that first. The call works best with even one week of evidence in it.
