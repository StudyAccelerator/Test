A few students have replied asking the same thing in different words: "what actually happens if I book the call?" Which is a polite way of asking whether this is where the sales pitch lives. Fair question. So here's exactly what happens, and you can decide for yourself.

**First five minutes:** you talk, I listen. Your subjects, your year, what the diagnostic said, and what actually happens when you sit a paper. If you've been running the drill, have your letter log open. If you haven't, that's fine too. Come as you are.

**The middle twenty:** we look at the evidence together. Your K, R, W and T letters if you have them, or your report if you don't. I tell you what I'd fix first, and just as usefully, what I'd ignore for now. Most students are trying to fix four things at once, and that's exactly why none of them move.

**The last five:** you leave with a plan for the next two weeks, written down. Which drill, which days, which topic first. Specific enough that when Monday comes, you already know what you're doing.

That's the whole call. If the Subject Accelerator genuinely fits what your log shows, I'll say so in one sentence, and you can ask about it or not. If it doesn't fit, I'll say that instead. I'm a doctor before I'm a business owner, and I've got no interest in prescribing things people don't need.

**Who shouldn't book:** if you haven't done the diagnostic's plan or a single timed question yet, and you know you won't before the call, spend your 30 minutes on that first. The call works best with even one week of real evidence in it.
