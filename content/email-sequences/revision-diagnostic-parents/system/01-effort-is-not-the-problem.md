{$name},

The diagnostic put {$diag_child_name|default('your child')}'s biggest leak in **{$diag_bottleneck|default('the method itself')}**, and that means something important for you: this isn't a subject problem, so a subject tutor probably isn't the first fix.

Here's the pattern as it looks from the outside. They work, sometimes a lot. The grades don't follow. So everyone concludes more work is needed, the pressure goes up, they work more, and the grades still don't follow. Everyone ends up more tired and nothing moves. Does that sound familiar?

The reason is that revision has a method underneath it: how you learn something, when you come back to it, how you check it actually stuck. If the method is wrong, the hours going into it get wasted, however many there are. Their diagnostic estimated that {$diag_low_yield_hours|default('a real share')} of their {$diag_hours_per_week|default('weekly')} hours are currently going into work that feels productive, reading, highlighting, making notes look neat, but scores almost nothing. Adding more hours to that just wastes more.

**Tonight's question**, asked with genuine curiosity, not as a trap: "when you revise something, how do you check it actually stuck?" If the answer is any version of "I go over it again", you've just heard the problem out loud. The fix is testing themselves with the book closed, and the report's 7 day plan starts exactly there.
