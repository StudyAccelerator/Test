Hi {$name},

Yesterday I gave you a question to ask {$diag_child_name|default('your child')}: "When you revise something, how do you check it actually stuck?"

If you asked it, you got one of four answers. Here's what each one means, because they point different ways.

**"I go over it again."**

This is the most common answer, and it's the problem itself, said out loud. Re-reading makes the topic feel familiar. And feeling familiar feels exactly like knowing it, until an exam asks them to write it from nothing.

The move: don't correct them tonight. The report's plan does the correcting, one blank page at a time. It lands much better coming from the plan than from a parent.

**"I just know if I know it."**

That's confidence standing in for proof. To be fair, everyone's memory works like this, adults included.

That's a useful thing to say to them. It makes the fix feel like an upgrade rather than a criticism.

**"I test myself."**

Better than most. The follow-up that matters is "book open or closed?"

Open-book testing is really just re-reading with extra steps. If it's truly closed-book, their method is better than the grades suggest. The problem is likely in when they return to topics, which the report also measures.

**A shrug.**

Often not rudeness. Nobody has ever asked them to have a method, so there's no answer to give. And this is the easiest starting point of the four, because there's nothing to unlearn.

Whichever answer you got, the next move is the same and it's small. The report's plan starts with ten minutes and a blank page. One topic, tonight or tomorrow, and let them pick it.

This gets fixed one honest page at a time, not in one big talk.

Tomorrow I'll send you what other parents have found. In the meantime, if the answer surprised you, reply and tell me what they said. I read each one, and I can usually tell you what it means.

Best wishes,

Waleed

PS: The shrug is my favourite answer to get, honestly. A student with no method yet has nothing to unlearn, and they tend to move fastest once they're shown one!
