{$name},

Yesterday I gave you a question to ask {$diag_child_name|default('your child')}: "when you revise something, how do you check it actually stuck?"

If you asked it, you got one of four answers. Here's what each one means, because they point in different directions.

**"I go over it again."** The most common answer, and it's the problem itself, said out loud. Re-reading makes material familiar, and familiarity feels exactly like knowing it, right up until an exam asks them to produce it from nothing. The move: don't correct them tonight. The report's plan does the correcting, one blank page at a time, and it lands much better coming from the plan than from a parent.

**"I just know if I know it."** That's confidence standing in for evidence. And to be fair, everyone's memory works like this, adults included, which is a genuinely useful thing to say to them. It makes the fix feel like a human upgrade rather than a personal criticism.

**"I test myself."** Better than most. The follow-up that matters is "book open or closed?" Open-book testing is really just re-reading with extra steps. If it's genuinely closed-book, their method is better than the grades suggest, and the problem is probably in when they return to topics, which the report also measures.

**A shrug.** Usually not rudeness. Nobody has ever asked them to have a method, so there's no answer to give. And honestly, this is the easiest starting point of the four, because there's nothing to unlearn.

Whichever answer you got, the next move is the same and it's small: the report's plan starts with ten minutes and a blank page. One topic, tonight or tomorrow, and let them pick it. This gets fixed one honest page at a time, not in one big talk.

Tomorrow I'll send you the evidence behind the fix. In the meantime, if the answer you got surprised you, reply and tell me what they said. I read every one, and I can usually tell you what it means.

Waleed
