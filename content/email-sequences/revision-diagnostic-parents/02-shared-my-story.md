Hi {$name},

You've had five days of practical emails from me. So today I want to tell you something more personal: why an NHS doctor spends his spare time teaching A-level students.

It's a fair question, and the answer is where all of this comes from.

At A-level, I was the student your child might be. I worked hard, my notes were neat, and my teachers told my parents I was diligent.

But my results kept coming back lower than what I knew. Nobody could tell me why. The only advice anyone had was "work harder", which isn't much use to a student who is already working hard.

I made it to medical school anyway, and that's where I finally learned what had been wrong.

Medicine forces the issue. There is so much to learn that you can't get through it with poor study methods. So they teach you how learning really works.

You test yourself instead of re-reading. You go back to material before it fades. You practise for the exam you'll actually sit, marked the way it'll actually be marked.

These are simple things. They could have been taught to me at 17 in two weeks, and never were.

That's why I do this. Over the last six years, alongside medicine, I've worked with more than 1,000 A-level students.

The pattern I see is almost always my own. A young person working hard, with all of that effort going into methods nobody ever checked.

That still gets to me. It's why the diagnostic exists, why these emails exist, and why so much of what I make is free.

So when I say I'm confident we can help {$diag_child_name|default('your child')}, that's where the confidence comes from. I've been the student. I've been the person who finally worked out what was wrong, and I've watched it make sense for a great many families.
