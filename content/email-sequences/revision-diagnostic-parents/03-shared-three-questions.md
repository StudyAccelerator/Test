Across a thousand-plus students, the parents I speak to ask me the same three questions more than everything else combined. So here they are, answered the way I answer them on calls.

**"Is it too late?"** Almost never, and the panic behind this question usually does more damage than the timing. What's true: the earlier in the year the method gets fixed, the more of the year benefits, and autumn fixes help predicted grades in a way spring fixes can't. What's also true: I've watched students turn things around from January, from March, even between mocks and the real thing. The right question isn't "is it too late", it's "what's the best move from exactly where we are", and that always has an answer.

**"Should we get a tutor, or do this?"** Depends entirely on what's broken, which is why I keep banging on about diagnosis. A genuine content gap, a topic never understood, wants a tutor or a subject programme. A method problem wants the method fixed, because a tutor pouring more content into a leaking method is the most expensive way to stand still. If you're not sure which one {$diag_child_name|default('your child')} has, that's not a reason to buy either. It's a reason to find out first.

**"How do I actually help without making it worse?"** Shorter version of everything I've written to you: ask about the process, not the hours. Put the plan somewhere visible and let it answer your questions. React to evidence, not to moods. And say the sentence they most need to hear from you, which is some version of "I know the effort is real". A teenager who feels believed accepts help. One who feels accused defends themselves instead.
