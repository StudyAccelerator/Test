This is the last email in this series, so let me be a doctor about it and close the consultation properly.

Two weeks ago the diagnostic named the thing you'd been sensing: {$diag_child_name|default('your child')} isn't short of effort or ability, they're losing marks in the gap between knowing it and scoring it. Since then you've had the ten minute check, the conversation script, the evidence sorted honestly, the money done in plain arithmetic, and the what-ifs answered. Whatever you decide, you're deciding informed, which is all I ever wanted these emails to do.

From here, I stop writing daily. No drip, no countdown, no "final chance" theatre. When something genuinely matters, a cohort date, something new and free that fits their profile, I'll write once, plainly.

One small ask before I go quiet. Reply and tell me where you landed, even in five words. "Enrolled." "Running the free drill." "Still torn." "Decided against it." I read every reply, I don't argue with decisions, and it genuinely helps me make this whole thing better for the next family. If you're still torn, say so and I'll help you get untorn, which sometimes takes one question and thirty seconds.

The programme, the free weekly drill and the half hour call all stay exactly where they've been. Doors don't expire around here.

Thank you for taking twenty questions' worth of honest looks at your child's revision. Most parents never get that far.

Best wishes,
