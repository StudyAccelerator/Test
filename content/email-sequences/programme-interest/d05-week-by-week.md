What do the twelve weeks feel like from the inside? "Live weekly sessions" tells you the shape and nothing about the feel.

**Weeks one and two feel like relief.**

Mostly because of what the sessions aren't. No one re-teaches the textbook at you, and no one is asked to answer in front of the group unless they choose to.

You work real exam questions with the mark scheme open. The first time a specialist re-words an answer from "that's roughly right" to "that scores", you see a thing that no amount of revising alone can show you.

Students often leave session one a bit annoyed that no one showed them this earlier.

**Weeks three to five take the most effort.**

The newness has worn off, and the between-session practice is a real two to three hours. This is where you find out the gap between liking the idea and doing the work.

It's also where the recordings prove their worth when life gets in the way of a weekend. I'd rather tell you this stretch exists than have it surprise you. Every programme worth doing has one.

**Around week four, the wording starts to arrive on its own.**

That's what students tell us, time after time. Mark scheme phrases start to show up in answers without being forced. Most students make the same mistakes, and by now you've watched dozens of them get fixed, your own included.

**Weeks six to ten settle into a rhythm.**

This is where the timing pays off. It's deep in the autumn term, the school tests that feed predicted grades are being sat, and the coached answers go straight into them.

The programme stops feeling like a thing on top of school and starts feeling like the training ground for it.

**Week twelve ends quietly.**

Which is how it should end. No big send-off, just a student who sits timed papers as a routine, marks their own work strictly, and speaks mark scheme as a second language.

The skills stay after the sessions stop, and that's the real product.
