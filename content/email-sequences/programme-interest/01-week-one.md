Yesterday I sent the structure and the prices. Today, the thing the page can't quite show you: what the first week actually feels like, because "twelve week live programme" sounds bigger from outside than it is from inside.

The first session opens with the specialist mapping the topic against the exam: what the board actually asks, where the marks concentrate, what high-scoring answers do differently. Twenty minutes in, the mark scheme comes out and stays out. Real questions, attempted live, then worked against the exact wording the scheme rewards. Nobody gets put on the spot beyond what they volunteer, and here's the strange part students report: watching someone else's answer get re-worded teaches nearly as much as your own, because everyone's gaps rhyme. By the end there's a short list of exactly what to practise before session two, which takes two to three hours across the week, mostly past-paper work that should be happening anyway, now with direction.

That's it. No hazing, no homework mountain, one recorded session a weekend and a clear list. Week two builds on week one, and by week four the mark-scheme language starts appearing in answers without being summoned, which is the whole point.

On timing, said once and honestly: the cohort starts Sunday 13 September because the twelve weeks then sit exactly under the autumn term, when teachers form the impressions that become predicted grades. Join late and you're catching up on recordings; wait for January and the programme still helps the summer exams, but the prediction window has gone. That's the calendar talking, not a countdown timer. It's the same calendar whether I mention it or not.
