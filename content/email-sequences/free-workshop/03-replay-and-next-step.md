**Why now matters**

The year has only just started, and that's exactly why today was worth your Saturday. Fix how you study now, and every topic you cover from here gets learnt properly the first time.

Leave it until mocks, and you're relearning everything from scratch, under pressure. I've seen that in a lot of students, and it's why Year 13 gets so stressful.

**1. Decide where your hours go with the RAG list**

Open your exam board's specification for each subject. Mark every module you've covered Red, Amber or Green for how confident you feel in it. Then open the red modules and do the same for each topic inside them.

Put it all in a Google Sheet, not on paper, so you can update it every time you finish a module.

Red topics first, always. The topic losing you 15 marks matters more than the one losing you one, and you get the rest of the year to polish it.

It takes about five minutes per subject.

**2. Learn a red topic properly in 45 minutes: the blurt**

Pick one red topic. Photosynthesis, not the whole of energy exchange.

Blurt, 15 minutes: blank page, write the subheadings first, then everything you remember. Timer on, no notes. At the end, score yourself out of 10 for confidence and for performance, and write down three questions you had.

Review, 15 minutes: open the textbook and fill the gaps in a different colour. Answer your three questions.

Reblurt, 15 minutes: do it again, the same day. This is the step nobody does, and it's what makes it stick.

Repeat the cycle until your performance is 8 out of 10. One to three cycles gets most topics there.

**3. The 80% shortcut: strategy stacking**

Learning a topic through practice questions alone is trial and error. A topic with 100 facts can take about 2,500 questions to master that way.

Blurt first and you cover 80 of those facts in 45 minutes. The questions now only have to do the last 20, so you need about 500 instead of 2,500. Same topic, a fifth of the work.

Spend the time you save pushing that topic from an A to an A*, or on the next red one, or on having a life.

**4. Find out which of the four tiers is costing you marks**

Every lost mark comes from one of four places.

Knowledge: you didn't understand it. Recall: you understood it but couldn't remember it in the exam.

Application: you knew it, but your wording wasn't what the examiner wanted. Exam mastery: time, stress and silly mistakes.

School, textbooks and subject tutors all work on tier one. For almost every student I meet, the marks are going in tiers two, three and four. That's why more hours and more tutoring so often change nothing.

As a doctor, I'd never treat a patient before I'd diagnosed them. Your grades work the same way.
