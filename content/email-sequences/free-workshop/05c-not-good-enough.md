Hey {$name},

I want to tell you a quick story today, because it changed how I think about every grade.

**Year 7**

I didn't transition very well into secondary school. I was falling behind, particularly in biology, and my class tests were coming back with poor grades.

So my biology teacher, Miss Booth, asked me what I wanted to do when I grew up. I told her I wanted to be a doctor and study medicine.

She gave me this look. Then she told me I should maybe think about some other options...

At 11 years old, I was essentially told I wasn't good enough to study medicine.

Now I'm a doctor.

**This isn't about teachers being wrong**

It's about the phrase itself. There is no such thing as "being good enough".

Here's what actually happens. You get a grade you're not happy with, and the quiet voice in your head says the same thing every time.

"I'm not smart enough. I just need to try harder and spend more time."

But it was never about being good enough. It's that nobody ever taught you what works best for you.

**Same class, same content, same expectations**

Everyone at school sits in the same room, goes through the same curriculum, and is expected to do well from it.

But there's no one size fits all with studying.

As a doctor, I see this every day. Two patients can come in with the same diagnosis and still need different treatment, because they don't present the same way.

Studying works that way too. The same revision method doesn't work for everyone, and nobody at school is checking which one works for you.

**So if you're not at the grades you want**

Don't decide that you're not smart enough. How can you be expected to do well when nobody has taught you what to do?

That's a fixable problem. Being "not good enough" was never the issue, because it was never true.

Don't forget that!

**The one thing I wish I'd known then**

I didn't fix it in Year 7. Or Year 8, or Year 9. I kept working harder, and it kept not working, right up until I finally learnt how to learn in Year 12.

That was five years of effort that could have gone a lot further.

The best time to learn how to learn was five years ago. The second best time is now.
