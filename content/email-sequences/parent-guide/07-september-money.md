One more thing the school probably won't spell out for you, and it's about timing.

Predicted grades, the ones that go on the UCAS form and frame every university conversation, get set and reviewed in the autumn term. They're built from the evidence in front of teachers: early Year 13 tests, mocks, classwork. Which means the next twelve or so weeks aren't just another term. They're the term where your child's paper record gets written, by teachers looking at this term's work.

I can't promise what any teacher decides, and I never will. But predictions follow evidence, and autumn is when the evidence gets made. A student who fixes their method or their exam craft in September gives their teachers three months of better work to look at. The same fix made in January still helps the real exams, but the prediction window has closed, and with it some university doors that were open in October. Same fix, different price, purely because of the date.
