Hi {$name},

You can't sit in the room while your child revises, and you shouldn't. But you can learn to read the signs from the doorway, and it turns out the difference between revision that works and revision that doesn't is surprisingly visible from outside. Here's what to look for, no questions required.

**Messy pages are a good sign.** Genuinely. A desk covered in scrawled, half-crossed-out attempts means they're testing themselves, and testing yourself looks like a struggle because it is one. Beautiful colour-coded notes are the look of the method that doesn't work: copying, highlighting, making things neat. If the output looks like it could go on Instagram, be a little suspicious.

**Books closed at the end matters more than books open in the middle.** The strongest single habit in revision is finishing a session by writing out what you remember with everything shut. If you happen to pass the door in the last ten minutes and the books are closed but the pen is moving, that's the best sign there is.

**Short and regular beats long and heroic.** A student doing 45 focused minutes most evenings is doing it right. A student announcing a four hour session on Sunday is usually planning to feel productive rather than to learn. If the pattern you see is little-and-often, relax.

**Past papers appearing is the turning point.** The moment printed exam papers and mark schemes start showing up around the house, something real has changed. Marks live in exam practice, and most struggling students avoid it for months because it's confronting.

**And the phone tells you the truth.** Not whether they have it, every teenager does, but where it is. A phone in another room says someone decided to concentrate. You didn't have to enforce that. You just get to notice it.

None of this requires a single "have you revised". You're just paying attention to the shape of the thing, and the shape tells you nearly everything. If most of these signs are missing, don't announce an inspection regime. That's what this week's other emails are for: the diagnostic measures it, and the plan fixes it.

Tonight: just look, and say nothing. Knowing what you're looking at changes the conversations you'll have later.

Best wishes,

Waleed
