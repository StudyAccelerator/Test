Hi {$name},

You can't sit in the room while your child revises, and you shouldn't.

But you can learn to read the signs from the doorway. The gap between revision that works and revision that doesn't is easy to see from outside.

Here's what to look for. No questions needed.

**1. Messy pages are a good sign**
A desk covered in scrawled, half-crossed-out attempts means they're testing themselves. Testing yourself looks like a struggle because it is one.

Neat, colour-coded notes are the look of the method that doesn't work: copying, highlighting, making things tidy. If the notes look good enough to post on Instagram, don't be too impressed.

**2. Books closed at the end matters more than books open in the middle**
The strongest single habit in revision is ending a session by writing out what you can recall with all the books shut.

If you pass the door in the last ten minutes and the books are closed but the pen is moving, that's the best sign there is.

**3. Short and regular beats long and heroic**
A student doing 45 focused minutes most evenings is doing it right.

A student who says they'll do a four hour session on Sunday is planning to feel busy rather than to learn. If the pattern you see is little and often, relax.

**4. Past papers showing up is the turning point**
The moment printed exam papers and mark schemes start showing up around the house, something real has changed.

Marks live in exam practice. Most struggling students avoid it for months because it's hard to face.

**5. The phone tells you the truth**
Not whether they have one, every teenager does, but where it is.

A phone left out of the room says someone chose to focus. You didn't have to enforce that. You just get to notice it.

**Tonight, try this**

Just look, and say nothing. None of this needs a single "have you revised?"

You're just seeing the pattern, and the pattern tells you nearly all of it.

If most of these signs are missing, don't announce a new set of rules. That's what this week's other emails are for. The diagnostic measures it, and the plan fixes it.

Knowing what you're looking at changes the talks you'll have later.
