# How to organise the ad campaigns (6 August 2026)

Waleed's question from 6 August: should the creatives all go into one campaign pointed at
the revision diagnostic and another for the direct funnel into the paid programmes, or
should there be several campaigns, one for maths, one for biology, one for chemistry?

**The answer: organise by funnel, never by subject. Two campaigns.**

1. **"Parents Diagnostic"**: Leads objective, optimising to the Lead event. The volume
   engine. Every diagnostic creative lives here, including the subject callouts.
2. **"Paid callouts | Sep 2026"**: the direct funnel to Subject Accelerators, exactly as
   the launch checklist in `2026-08-05-paid-callouts/COPY-PACK.md` describes. Separate
   campaign so the two funnels never share a budget and never get judged by each
   other's numbers.

Subjects are creatives inside those campaigns, not campaigns of their own.

## Why subject campaigns lose at this budget

- **The learning maths is unforgiving.** Meta wants roughly 50 optimisation events per ad
  set per week before delivery settles. At the benchmark cost per lead of £16 to £28,
  that is £800 to £1,400 a week for ONE ad set. At £10 to £20 a day total, a single ad
  set already runs learning-limited. Three subject campaigns would starve every cell,
  none would ever settle, and the readout would be noise.
- **The audience does not split by subject.** The buyer is the same UK parent of a Year
  12 or 13 student, and Meta has no "parents of maths students" segment anyway. Subject
  is a message variable, not an audience variable. The standing principle from the July
  test holds: broad audience, the creative does the narrowing.
- **Meta already runs the subject test for you.** Load the subject callouts as separate
  ads inside one ad set and delivery shifts spend to whichever earns the cheapest
  results. That is the maths versus biology versus chemistry experiment, run properly,
  on pooled data, without fragmenting the budget.
- **You still get the per-subject read.** The ad-level breakdown shows CTR, cost per
  click and leads per creative. If the maths callout wins decisively, that is a
  creative insight to act on, and it costs nothing structurally.

The rule of thumb worth keeping: a new ad set or campaign is only justified when the
audience, the optimisation event, or the judgment rules differ. Diagnostic versus paid
differs on all three, so they are separate campaigns. Maths versus biology differs on
none, so they are ads in the same ad set.

## The shape from 8 August

The current two-campaign diagnostic test ends 7 August 23:59. From the 8th:

- **Diagnostic campaign, one ad set, broad parents, £10 a day.** Pick 5 or 6 creatives:
  the best performer from the July test plus a spread of the 3 August callouts (general,
  one year-group, one or two subject variants, one face variant). Not all 15 at once:
  delivery is winner-takes-most, and past about 6 ads the losers never spend enough to
  be judged.
- **Paid callouts campaign, one ad set, broad parents, £10 a day**, using the suggested
  opening batch of 8 in the COPY-PACK (trim to 6 if spend spreads too thin). Gated on
  the two claims only Waleed can verify: the fifth-of-the-cost ratio and the real number
  of September places. Judge it on cost per landing page view and time to first enquiry,
  not week-one lead volume; cold traffic to a paid page converts far below a free quiz.
- **Results week (13 August) belongs to the diagnostic campaign.** It is the cheapest
  education inventory of the year and the peak of parent attention; if either campaign
  gets a temporary budget lift that week, it is the free one, because panicked parents
  want help first and a purchase later.

## Later, not now

- **A warm ad set for the paid funnel.** Retargeting (site visitors 180 days, diagnostic
  takers, page engagers) is where direct-to-paid ads work hardest. The pools are too
  small to feed an ad set yet; add it once the pixel audience is meaningfully in the
  four figures. Until then, cold and broad, and the diagnostic campaign keeps filling
  those pools.
- **Physics creatives.** None exist in the paid pack and none should: Subject
  Accelerators sells Biology, Chemistry and Maths only.

every campaign himself.
