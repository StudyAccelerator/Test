## PRIMARY TEXT 1: the long one (pairs with `01-general`)

Use this with the general callout images. It is the closest to the long-form style
that works in this market, and it carries the whole argument.

> Is your child studying for their A-levels?
>
> If so, this is worth 3 minutes of your evening.
>
> I'm Dr Waleed Ahmad. I'm an NHS doctor, I got the grades I needed for medicine, and I have worked with over 1,000 A-level students.
>
> The pattern I see more than any other is this. The hours are going in. The marks are not coming out. And the parents watching it happen assume their child is either not trying hard enough or not clever enough.
>
> It is almost never either of those.
>
> It is that nobody ever taught them how to revise. They read, they highlight, they rewrite notes, and it feels like work because it takes hours. It just does not put anything in their memory that survives an exam hall.
>
> So I built a free diagnostic that finds exactly where their study hours are leaking.
>
> It takes about 3 minutes. You answer 20 questions about how your child actually revises, and you get an instant report:
>
> Which of 7 revision profiles fits your child
> Roughly how many of their weekly hours are going into low-yield work
> The one thing I would change first, and why
>
> No cost, and the report is on your screen the second you finish.
>
> Tap Learn More and see which profile your child gets.

## Launch checklist

1. New campaign, Leads objective, "Callout test | Aug 2026", £10 a day.
2. Ad set: UK, both parent segments, age suggestion 38 to 55, Facebook only,
   optimising the Lead event. No detailed-targeting interests: the creative is the
   targeting, and that is the point of this test.
3. One ad per image. Same headline, description, button and URL throughout.
4. Do NOT touch the running profiles campaign while it is delivering leads under £8.
5. After 4 days: keep anything under £15 a lead, cut anything over £30, rotate fresh
   variants in from `final/`.
