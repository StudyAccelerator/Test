# Round 2 creative strategy: three funnels, one market (27 August 2026)

Written to Waleed's brief of 27 August: no ads are currently live, tutor economics
now demand roughly four programme sales per tutored subject pair, and the next
round tests three different funnels at once: direct to a call with Waleed, fresh
angles into the proven diagnostic, and a first probe straight at the paid
programmes. Everything below is built on the real numbers from the 13 to 20
August test (`Meta Ads 11th Aug/AD REPORT FINAL 21 Aug.md`).

## 1. The economics this round has to serve

Real numbers, nothing invented:

- Programme prices (live site): one subject £339, two £629, three £849.
- Tutor cost: £1,000 per subject (Waleed, 27 August).
- Four double sales bring in £2,516 against £2,000 of tutor cost for two
  subjects: £516 gross before a penny of ad spend. Four triples: £3,396 against
  £3,000, so £396 gross.
- That sets the bar plainly: **at four sales, the entire ad cost of producing
  those sales has to stay under roughly £129 each (doubles) or £99 each
  (triples) just to break even.** Every sale beyond four is where the margin
  actually lives, so the honest target is five to six sales per tutor pair,
  not four.
- At the proven £14.79 per genuine parent lead, £129 buys about 8 to 9 parent
  leads. So the diagnostic funnel breaks even on a double **only if roughly one
  in nine parent leads becomes a sale.** Nothing measured yet says whether that
  holds; it is the single most important unknown in the business right now.

Three consequences drive this round:

1. **The cheapest sales available are the leads already paid for.** The August
   flight banked 40+ genuine leads whose CAC is spent. Calls to the CRM list
   convert at zero additional ad cost. Ads refill the top of the funnel; they
   do not replace working the existing pipeline.
2. **A call converts better than an email sequence ever will**, which is why
   the new book-a-call funnel exists: it collapses lead, nurture and close into
   one conversation.
3. **A small direct-to-paid probe is rational**, not because cold traffic buys
   £629 programmes at scale, but because the buyers who are ready need only to
   be found, and one sale pays for the whole probe.

## 2. The market, by awareness level

Sabri Suby's larger market formula, applied to UK parents of A-level students,
with each level mapped to the funnel that fits it:

| Level | Who they are | Funnel | Creatives |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3% buying now | Actively comparing tutors this week; the ones who found Krishna | Paid programmes direct | p1-september, p2-price |
| 17% gathering information | Know they want help, researching options, not yet chosen | Book a call: the A* Roadmap offer gives them the plan they are out looking for | c1 to c4 |
| 20% problem aware | Know the grades are stuck, have not framed it as a method problem | Revision diagnostic, pain callouts | d1-stuck-grade, d3-chemistry, d4-maths |
| 60% problem unaware | "It's fine, I'm on top of it." Child looks busy, parent has no evidence either way | Diagnostic, doubt-planting angles | d5-check, d2-mocks |

Two honest notes on the ladder. First, cost efficiency falls as you go down
it: the proven diagnostic engine stays the biggest budget line and the
unaware angles are an experiment on top, not the core. Second, the August
test already told us which words open which level: "struggling" and pain
phrasing beat neutral "studying" phrasing two to one on CPL, and year-group
callouts without a pain word died entirely. Every new callout in this round
carries a pain word or plants a doubt.

## 3. The three funnels

### Funnel A: book a call (new)

The offer, in one line: **a free 30 minute call with an NHS doctor, bring your
child, leave with their personal A* Roadmap.**

- The named deliverable matters. "Book a call" is a cost to a parent;
  "leave with your child's A* Roadmap" is an asset. The name recommended is
  **the A* Roadmap** (alternatives in the copy pack if it grates: the Grade
  Plan, the Route to Results plan, which echoes the site's own hero line).
- Destination: the Zoom scheduler (`https://scheduler.zoom.us/dr-waleed-ahmad/a-level`).
- **Tracking caveat, decided honestly up front:** the scheduler is not our
  site, so no pixel fires and Meta cannot optimise on bookings. Two ways to
  run it, and the recommendation is to test both against each other:
  1. **Traffic objective, landing page views, straight to the scheduler.**
     Cleanest experience; judged manually on bookings that appear in the Zoom
     calendar over the flight. UTM tags do nothing here, so the ad name is
     the attribution.
  2. **Instant Form (Meta native lead form), Leads objective.** Parent leaves
     name, phone and best time inside Facebook; Waleed rings to set the call.
     Meta optimises properly, CPL is measurable, and it feeds the same
     call-first motion the CRM already runs. Slightly lower intent per lead,
     far more algorithmic help.
- Capacity guard: the call slots are Waleed's evenings around hospital
  shifts. At even 5 booked calls a week the funnel is full, which is another
  reason this launches at probe budget, not scale.

### Funnel B: the revision diagnostic (proven engine, new angles)

The August winner keeps running exactly as proven: doctor photo, pain
callout, Primary Text 7. This round adds the four angles the test pointed at:

- **d1 stuck-on-the-same-grade**: the sharpest version of the pattern PT7
  describes, now as the headline hook.
- **d3 and d4**: chemistry and maths rewritten into the "struggling" phrasing
  that beat their "studying" versions two to one.
- **d2 mocks**: the September seasonal angle (mocks decide predicted grades,
  predicted grades decide UCAS). Replaces the dead results-week year-group
  callouts with a reason to act now.
- **d5 says-they're-revising**: the problem-unaware experiment. It sells the
  diagnostic as a way for the parent to check without an argument.

### Funnel C: paid programmes direct (probe)

For the 3%. Two new creatives with real prices from the live page (£339,
£629, £849), plus the existing 2026-08-05 paid-callouts pack as rotation
stock. The two claims in that older pack that Waleed never verified (the
fifth-of-the-cost ratio and "5 new students") stay out of this round; the
new copy uses only the printed prices and the true small-groups framing.
Judged on cost per landing page view and any sale or enquiry, with
expectations set at benchmark reality: cold traffic to a paid page converts
far below a free quiz. One sale pays for weeks of the probe; that is the bet.

## 4. Campaign structure and budget

| Campaign | Budget | Contents | Judge on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evergreen: Parents Diagnostic (relaunch first) | £15/day | Round 1 doctor-image winner + d1 + d3, one Advantage+ ad set | CPL under £12 blended |
| Test: Book a call | £10/day | c1 to c3 (scheduler) against an Instant Form twin | Booked calls and form leads over 10 days |
| Probe: Paid programmes | £5/day | p1 + p2 | Cost per landing page view, any sale |

- Total £30/day, roughly £900 a month at full tilt. The evergreen relaunch is
  the urgent piece: every dark day at proven £7 to £8 CPL is roughly two
  leads not captured, and nothing has been live since 20 August.
- **Kill rules, decided before spending:** any diagnostic creative over £30
  per lead after £25 of spend is switched off. Any call ad with zero
  bookings or form leads after £70 dies. The paid probe gets reviewed, not
  killed, at £50, because its sample sizes are tiny by design.
- Promotion rule: a test winner moves into the evergreen campaign and the
  test slot refills. One variable at a time stays the law: this round the
  variable is the funnel, so creatives within each funnel share the proven
  photo-led format rather than testing format and funnel at once.
- Seasonality: expect CPL 20 to 40 percent above results-week numbers in
  term time. A £10 CPL in September is not a failure of the creative.

## 5. What gets measured weekly

1. Spend, leads and CPL per ad set (Ads Manager) against MailerLite
   provenance, the same cross-check as round 1.
2. Booked calls in the Zoom calendar, matched by hand to the flight dates.
3. The GA4 funnel (now fixed and verified): visitors to starts to completions
   to route clicks to callback requests, split by taker. This is the lever
   that turns "8 to 9 percent complete" into a fixable number.
4. Sales. Every Stripe payment gets traced back: which lead, which source,
   which creative family. Four sales per tutor pair is the target on the
   board; the weekly question is only ever "how many, and from where".
