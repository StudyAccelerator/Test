Every post below is ready to paste. Titles go in Skool's title field, bodies underneath. Post them yourself at roughly the times given; a community whose founder posts daily for a fortnight forms a habit loop, and one that launches quiet dies quiet. **Post 2, 12pm. Category: General. Title: `The most expensive lie in revision`**

> "I read through my notes and I know them."
>
> So here's the thing. Recognising your notes and recalling them in an exam hall are two completely different skills. Exams test recall. Re-reading trains recognition. That's why you can revise all evening, feel amazing, and lose the marks anyway.
>
> Quick test, be honest: pick the topic you revised most recently. Close everything. Could you write half a page on it right now, from memory?
>
> If that question made you uncomfortable, you're in exactly the right place. Comment the topic you're scared to try it on. That's your first blurt.

## Day 7: Sunday 16 August

**10am. Category: General. Title: `Poll answer: it's not the hours`**

> Most of you guessed high. Here's what I've actually seen: the students at the top aren't the ones doing the most hours. They spend the same amount of time, honestly usually less, on higher-yield activities. Retrieval instead of re-reading. Past papers under time instead of "looking over" them. Fixing weak topics instead of polishing strong ones.
>
> Hours are the input everyone can see. The system is the part that's invisible. So when someone in your year seems to do less and get more, they're not a genius. They're just not paying the feelings tax on low-yield work.
>
> Do less, better. That's the whole philosophy, and everything in the classroom shows you how.

## Day 9: Tuesday 18 August

**10am. Category: General. Title: `The examiner is a person, and you've never met them`**

> Quick scene. Your examiner is going through dozens of papers in a day. They're not reading your answer like your teacher does. They're hunting: step one, seen it, tick. Step two. Oh. I don't see step two here.
>
> No step two, no mark, even if step two was obviously in your head.
>
> This is why students lose marks on topics they know. The exam isn't a conversation, it's a checklist, and the mark scheme is the checklist, published, sitting there, mostly unread.
>
> So next past paper: mark it yourself, against the scheme, line by line. It's uncomfortable, and it's the fastest exam-technique lesson that exists.
