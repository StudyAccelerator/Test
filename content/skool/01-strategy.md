# The Skool community: strategy and decisions

Written 5 August 2026. This is the thinking behind every decision in this pack. The paste-ready material lives in the numbered files beside it; this file is why those files say what they say. Nothing here is live and nothing goes live until you say so.

## 1. The verdict in one paragraph

Open one Skool community called **Top 1% A-Levels**, under your name and face, free to join, with a paid tier inside it called **Locked In** at **£29 a month** (founding rate, first 30 members, rising to £39). Free community opens to your email list on **Monday 10 August** and rides the results-day wave on 13 August. The paid tier opens **Monday 21 September**, after the Study Accelerator cohort push, not during it. The community is students only. It sells the method, never the subjects, and it is designed to feed your existing programmes, not to fight them.

## 2. Why this play, and why now

Three findings from the 16 July reaudit make this the right move rather than a distraction:

1. **Every pound this business has ever made came from email plus a live event.** All 18 payments landed inside workshop-and-follow-up cycles. The workshop was retired with no successor, so the funnel has had no live conversion venue since May. A community with a standing live call is that venue, rebuilt as a place rather than an event.
2. **The list is the proven asset and it has been silent.** 444 active subscribers on 16 July, most of them students who joined through your workshops, opened your emails at 46 to 84 percent, and have heard nothing since 6 May. The founding invite in this pack is a real reason to email them that asks for nothing but a click.
3. **Your revenue shape is the problem you named yourself.** Everything you sell is a one-off, so every month starts at zero. A membership gives the business a recurring floor and gives you a daily audience to sell the programmes to, which is where the real money stays.

One honest warning, because the audit earned it: the failure pattern of this business is building instead of sending. This plan is deliberately structured so that the whole build is one sitting at the Skool screen, and the first send to real people happens within days, on 10 August. If the community exists but the founding email has not gone out, the play has failed in the same way as before. The email is the launch, not the setup.

## 3. Positioning: you, not the company

The website sells subjects taught by a team, under a company brand. The community is the opposite on purpose:

- **The name on the door is yours.** Dr Waleed Ahmad, NHS doctor, former top-performing A-level student. The About page, the welcome video, every post: first person singular.
- **The promise is rank, not subject.** Not "get better at Chemistry" but "finish in the top 1 percent of your cohort". Method, systems, exam performance. This is the lane the Content HQ research found empty: no UK doctor does A-level revision content at scale, and the A-level-only lane has no mega account. The community is the owned home for that positioning, and TikTok, LinkedIn and the newsletter all get somewhere warm to send people.
- **"Top 1%" is already your language.** Your own workshop was called the Top 1% Study Systems Workshop, the site sells the Top 1% Study System and the Top 1% Mentorship, and 155 of your subscribers joined through a group with that exact name. The community name is continuity, not a new brand to build from nothing.

Brand architecture rule, so the two layers never blur: **Dr Waleed sells the method; A-Level Accelerators sells the programmes.** The community is his room. The programmes are advertised inside it the way a GP refers into a clinic: when the diagnosis calls for it.

## 4. The pricing model, argued properly

You said you are drawn to monthly recurring but want talking out of it if it is wrong. I tried to talk myself out of it. Here is the full argument, both directions.

**The case against monthly, stated fairly:**

- **The June cliff is real.** Year 13s sit their last exam and cancel the same week. A-level membership businesses breathe with the school year: strong September to May, hollow June to August. Whatever you build will lose a large slice of its paying base every June.
- **Sixth formers do not hold the card.** Every subscription is a parent conversation, and parents audit their direct debits. A membership that is not visibly used gets cancelled at the first quiet fortnight.
- **Small numbers at first.** Thirty founding members at £29 is £870 a month. Arithmetic, not a forecast: even 100 paying members averaging £35 is £42,000 a year. Useful, real, recurring, and still a fraction of the £1 million road. The membership is a floor and a funnel, not the engine.

**The alternatives, and why each loses:**

- **Termly passes** match how parents think about school, but Skool bills monthly or annually only. Termly means invoicing by hand, which puts you back in the admin seat this play exists to remove.
- **An academic-year pass** (one payment, September to results day) is just the one-off shape wearing a scarf. It recreates the exact problem you named: every September starts from zero again. It can exist later as an option for committed families; it is the wrong default.
- **Keeping everything one-off** is the status quo the audit already measured: 71 days without a sale because nothing renews on its own.

**Why monthly wins anyway:**

- It is the only model that actually changes the shape of the business, which is the brief.
- The price of entry is a parent's easiest yes. One hour of private tutoring typically costs more than a whole month of this. That comparison goes in the sales copy because it is true and parents already know it.
- Skool's native billing makes it frictionless: card in, cancel anytime, no invoices, no chasing.
- The school year gives you retention arcs for free: predicted grades in September and October, mocks November to January, the long grind to Easter, exam season. A member who joins in September has a reason to stay every single month until June.
- The June cliff is manageable if you plan for it instead of being surprised by it: Year 12s carry over into Year 13, leavers churn out, and each August's new Year 12 intake refills the base. Treat the year like a season ticket business. The paid-tier file includes a June play (pause instead of cancel for Year 12s) for exactly this.

**Decision: monthly recurring, £29 founding locked for as long as they stay subscribed, £39 standard afterwards, monthly only at launch.** No annual option until the community has survived one autumn term; revisit at Christmas. If you disagree with any number here, the only one I would defend to the wall is the model itself. The price can move; the shape should not.

**Why £29 and £39 and not the £47 to £97 that Skool gurus quote:** those numbers come from adult B2B niches in dollars. Your buyer is a UK parent of a teenager who already anchors on tutoring at £30 to £60 an hour and on subscription school tools at £5 to £30 a month. £39 sits where it reads as serious but under one tutoring hour. £29 founding rewards the people who trust you before there is social proof, and locking their rate for life gives them a reason to never cancel. No sale has ever happened at any currently published price on your site, so all of this is a first test, priced to make the test easy to pass.

## 5. How it sits next to what you already sell

This was your hard requirement, so here is the whole ladder as it will exist, cheapest to dearest:

| Rung | What it is | Price | Who delivers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free tools | Diagnostic, tracker, blog, newsletter | £0 | Automated |
| **Skool free tier** | Community, monthly Open Surgery call, Start Here course | £0 | **You** |
| **Skool Locked In** | Weekly Ward Round call, full system curriculum, accountability | £29 to £39 a month | **You** |
| Study Series | 4 live teaching sessions | £119 | You |
| Study Accelerator | 12-week implementation with 1:1 support | £499 | You |
| Summer / Subject Accelerators | Live subject teaching | £289 to £849 | Tutors |
| Top 1% Mentorship | Year-round personal mentorship | £2,000 a year | You |

**The guardrails that stop the membership eating your programmes.** The membership never includes, at any price:

1. **Any 1:1 anything.** No personal plan reviews, no private calls, no DM coaching. The moment a member needs individual attention, that is the Study Accelerator's job at £499.
2. **Any subject teaching.** No Biology content, no Maths help beyond method. Subject questions get answered with "that is exactly what the Subject Accelerators are for". This also keeps you out of the trap of becoming a free homework helpline.
3. **Any personal timetable or plan built for them.** The membership teaches the system and holds them accountable to it in a group. Building it with them is paid programme work.

**The one real overlap is the £119 Study Series** (4 live sessions teaching the system: the same material the membership curriculum drips). Recommendation: leave the Study Series exactly as it is through the autumn, because the constraint on this project is not touching existing programmes, and because the two serve different buyers (a one-off £119 decision versus a £29 rolling one). But look at it honestly at Christmas: if the membership works, the Study Series' natural future is to become the membership's first month, and its page becomes a door to the community. That is a decision for December with autumn data, not for now.

**What people already inside your programmes get:** every current paying student (Summer Accelerator, September Study Accelerator, Mentorship, the March cohort) gets Locked In included free until 1 January 2027. It costs you nothing marginal, seeds the paid space with your most engaged students, and makes the £499 purchase feel bigger, not threatened. Mechanics in the paid-tier file.

**The rank promise and the subject promise never compete** because a student who wants top grades needs both a method (the community, the membership) and subject mastery (the programmes). Your own four-tier model says exactly this. The community makes that argument for you daily, in public, to the warmest audience you have.

## 7. Students only, and the safeguarding posture

Members are 16 to 18. That shapes rules, not vibes:

- **Students only inside.** Parents get a sales page, a letter, and the parent guide; they do not get membership of the student space. Mixing the two kills the candour that makes a student community work.
- **All coaching happens in public.** Questions in the feed, answers in the feed, calls recorded into the classroom. You do not run 1:1 DMs with members on the platform, the same hard rule as the LinkedIn inbox agent. This is safeguarding-sound and it scales.
- **Real names, school-corridor standard.** The rules copy in file 02 sets the tone: if you would not say it in front of your head of sixth form, it does not get posted.
- **You are a doctor and know what a disclosure looks like.** If a member posts something concerning (self-harm, abuse, crisis), respond publicly with signposting (Samaritans 116 123, Childline 0800 1111, their GP), move the detail out of the feed, and keep a dated note. That posture is written into the moderation section of file 02.
- **Platform check on setup day:** confirm Skool's current terms permit 16 and 17 year olds as members (their public legal page does not state a minimum age; check the terms at signup and, if under-18s need parental consent, the joining questions and parent letter already cover the ask).

## 8. Risks, ranked, with the mitigation built in

1. **It becomes another built-but-unlaunched asset.** The named failure mode of this business. Mitigation: the setup is one sitting, the founding email is dated (10 August), and this pack contains zero work that waits on anything except your review.
2. **A quiet room.** A community with no activity reads as a failed party and a paid launch into silence would poison the offer. Mitigation: 14 days of seed content pre-written, a 20-minute daily routine (file 05), and a hard gate: the paid tier does not open unless the free community passes the activity bar in file 05. Date moves before quality does.
3. **The June cliff.** Covered in section 4; modelled, not wished away.
4. **Your rota.** An F1 rota and a weekly live call will collide. Mitigation: calls are term-time only, the slot is chosen against your rota on setup day, and a recorded fallback exists (file 07). Consistency beats the specific hour.
5. **Platform dependency.** Skool owns the room. Mitigation: keep pushing members onto the email list (the Sunday Session is inside the onboarding course as a step), so the audience is portable if Skool ever is not.
6. **Fee drag at scale.** Start on Skool's Hobby plan ($9 a month, 10 percent transaction fee). Past roughly £1,000 a month of membership revenue the Pro plan ($99 a month, 2.9 percent) is cheaper; the switch point is in the setup file so future-you does not overpay.
