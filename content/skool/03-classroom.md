### Module 3: Take the diagnostic (2 minutes, do it now)

> Before you change anything about your revision, find out what's actually broken.
>
> The diagnostic is 20 questions. It scores the five systems behind every grade (planning, focus, revision method, retrieval, exam technique) and tells you which one is costing you the most marks, and what to do about it.
>
> Take it here: https://alevelaccelerators.com/revision-diagnostic/?utm_source=skool&utm_medium=community
>
> Then come back and post your archetype in the Start Here thread. You'll see very quickly you're not the only one with your profile, and the fix for each one is different. That's the point. Diagnose before you treat.

### Module 4: The four tiers (why hard work isn't working)

> Every grade comes from four tiers, stacked in order.
>
> Tier 1 is knowledge: do you have the content in your head?
> Tier 2 is recall: can you retrieve it without the notes in front of you? Under pressure? Because exams test recall, not recognition. Re-reading your notes feels productive precisely because it never tests you.
> Tier 3 is application: can you use it on a question you've never seen?
> Tier 4 is exam mastery: marks, timing, mark schemes, what the examiner is actually looking for.
>
> Most students who feel stuck at a B are camped in tier 1, polishing knowledge they can't retrieve. Their notes are beautiful. Their recall is untested. And the exam only pays for tiers 2, 3 and 4.
>
> Everything in this community exists to move you up the tiers. The diagnostic tells you which tier you're stuck in. The Weekly System course gives you the week that fixes it.

## Course 2: The Weekly System (opens at Level 2)

Course description:

> The exact weekly routine I teach: rate your topics honestly, plan the week around your weakest ones, and use methods that actually test you. Opens at Level 2, which one decent introduction post will get you.

### Module 1: Rate your topics, honestly

> The system starts with an honest audit. Every topic in every subject gets one of three ratings: Struggling, Shaky, or Solid.
>
> The rule that makes it work: if you're hesitating between two ratings, take the lower one. Blurting is humbling, and so is this. Students rate topics Solid because they recognise them. Recognition is not recall. If you couldn't answer an exam question on it tonight, without notes, it is not Solid.
>
> Do the audit in the free Revision Tracker, it's built for exactly this: https://alevelaccelerators.com/revision-tracker/?utm_source=skool&utm_medium=community

### Module 2: Build the week around your weakest topics

> Here's what most students do: revise the topics they like. It feels good, and it's a waste of a week.
>
> The system is the opposite. Your Struggling topics get the most sessions, your Solid ones get quick reviews to stay Solid. The tracker does the maths for you: it caps your week at what's actually possible (school days and free days are different animals), spreads sessions across the day instead of cramming them, and if a week is genuinely too full, it parks a topic for next week instead of pretending you'll do 9 hours on Sunday.
>
> Build your week, screenshot the poster it gives you, and post it in Weekly Plans on Monday. Public plans get finished. Private plans get forgotten.

### Module 3: Blurt and Fix (the 90 minutes that moves grades)

> One session type does more for your grades than any other, and it's the one nobody wants to do.
>
> Blurt and Fix, 90 minutes: close the notes, write everything you know about the topic from memory, then open the notes and hunt the gaps. The blank spaces on that page are your actual revision list. Not what the textbook chapter says. What your brain couldn't produce.
>
> It's stressful. It's humbling. That's exactly why it works, and why the students who do it weekly stop being surprised by exams. The full method, with the template, is in the Blurting course here in the classroom.

### Module 4: The Sunday Reset

> Sunday evening, 15 minutes, three questions:
>
> 1. What did I plan versus what did I do? (No judgement. Data.)
> 2. Which topics moved a rating? Struggling to Shaky counts. Celebrate it in Wins.
> 3. What's the one thing next week's plan does differently?
>
> Post your answers in the Sunday Reset thread in Weekly Plans. Then build next week in the tracker while it's fresh.
>
> This loop, run weekly, is the whole system: audit, plan, execute, review. Everyone who's ever beaten their predicted grades was running some version of it, whether they knew it or not.

## Course 3: Blurting, Properly (open to everyone)

Course description:

> The single highest-yield revision method, taught properly, with the template.

### Module 1: What blurting is, and why it hurts

> Blurting is retrieval practice with the safety wheels off. Blank page, closed notes, one topic, everything you can remember, from memory.
>
> The first time is horrible. You'll write four lines about a topic you "knew", and sit there staring. Good. That gap between what you recognised and what you retrieved? That gap is where your grades have been leaking. Every blurt finds the leak before the examiner does.

### Module 2: The template and the routine

> Attach the Blurting Template PDF to this module (the same file the site gives away), then this text:
>
> How to run it: pick one topic, set 20 minutes, fill the page from memory. Then open your notes in a different colour and fix the gaps. The fixes ARE the revision. Finish by writing three exam-style questions you'd now ask yourself, and answer them in two days. That second pass, two days later, is where it becomes permanent.
>
> Once a week per weak subject is the dose. Twice for a topic you're rescuing. More than that and you're polishing, not learning.
