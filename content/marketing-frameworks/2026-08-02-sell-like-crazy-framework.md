# Sell Like Crazy: the standing marketing framework lens

**Date:** 2 August 2026
**Source:** Sabri Suby, "Sell Like Crazy" (2019), read cover to cover on 2 August 2026 from the full 312 page text.
**Status:** Standing operating knowledge on Waleed's instruction. From this date, any session advising on offers, ads, funnels, lead magnets, landing pages or sales copy pressure tests the plan against this framework and names the part it is applying. Where a Suby tactic clashes with the house rules (honesty rule, no invented numbers, voice rules, no grade-outcome claims before results data exists), the house rules win and the clash gets flagged to Waleed rather than quietly resolved either way.
**What this document is not:** an action plan. Waleed's instruction on 2 August was knowledge in, changes out only when he asks. Nothing in here has been built, changed or scheduled. The gap list in Part 4 is a diagnosis for him to act on when he chooses.

## Part 1: The framework itself

Suby's core claim is that businesses fail not from bad products but from weak selling systems. His doctrine, before the phases:

- **Sales trumps all.** The founder's number one job is selling. The business you are in is the business of selling your product, not making it. Most founder time should go on revenue producing activity.
- **The 4% rule.** Pareto applied twice: 4% of activities produce 64% of revenue. Identify them (for him: offers, sales copy, funnels, videos, webinars) and delegate the rest.
- **You do not have a traffic problem.** Traffic is a commodity you buy from the Google and Facebook duopoly, any volume, any time. What businesses actually have is a conversion problem: no offer strong enough to turn bought traffic into profit. "If you can't pay money to acquire a customer, you don't have a business." Relying only on free channels and referrals is fate, not a system.
- **Advertising is an investment, not an expense.** Billionaire investors celebrate 20 to 30% annual returns; a working funnel returns multiples on spend in days. Therefore no fixed marketing budget: if £1 in reliably brings more than £1 out, spend as much as cash flow allows. A budget is only for marketing that does not work yet.
- **Salesmanship multiplied.** An ad is a sales pitch delivered to thousands at once. Write it as if speaking to one dream buyer in person. Most ads ("we exist, buy our stuff", lists of features) would not survive one live sales conversation.
- **Long form copy works.** His split tests across large ad spend: entertaining long copy beats short copy for sales. The people who will not read are not buyers anyway; buyers are hungry for information.

### The eight phases

**Phase 1: Understand and identify your dream buyer.**
- The Larger Market Formula: in any market, only 3% are in buying mode now. 17% are gathering information, 20% are problem aware, 60% are not even problem aware. Everyone fights over the 3%; the money is in systems that educate and move the other 97% up the pyramid before competitors reach them. Education based marketing (attract, educate, nurture, then ask) turns chasing into being chased.
- The Halo Strategy: know the dream buyer intimately. Not demographics: their deepest pains, fears, hopes, dreams, the exact language they use. Research where they congregate (groups, forums, Reddit, reviews, comment sections), harvest word for word phrases, use Google autosuggest and question tools to read the questions the market is actually asking.
- The Power 4%: the top 4% of customers produce around 64% of revenue. Profile them and hunt for more of them.
- Nine questions build the avatar: where they hang out, where they get information, biggest frustrations, hopes and dreams, biggest fears, preferred communication, exact vernacular, a day in their life, what makes them happy. Fear motivates more than desire: people move away from pain faster than towards pleasure.

**Phase 2: Create the perfect bait (the High Value Content Offer, HVCO).**
- A free, genuinely valuable piece of content (report, quiz, video, cheat sheet, checklist) that answers the market's single most burning question. It appeals to the whole pyramid, not the 3%, and positions you as the educator so that when a prospect reaches buying mode, you are the trusted authority.
- His conversion arithmetic (his claims): a "get a quote" style page converts around 3% of visitors and those leads close well; an HVCO page converts around 25% of visitors and those leads close lower but net roughly triple the sales from identical traffic.
- Three rules: (1) the title is nearly everything, it must stop the prospect and burn with intrigue (his evidence: the same book retitled went from 2,000 copies to 2.3 million); (2) every point inside must touch a burning issue; (3) keep it simple, five to eight pages is plenty, but it must genuinely deliver, because it is the first value exchange and sets the "if the free thing is this good..." expectation.
- Title formulas he reuses: "X ways to achieve [desired thing] without [undesired thing]"; "[Do hard thing] in [timeframe] even if [shortcoming]"; "How to eliminate [problem] without [thing they hate] within [timeframe]"; numbers plus specificity plus a curiosity qualifier.

**Phase 3: Capture leads (the opt-in page).**
- One job: exchange the HVCO for contact details. Elements: headline that promises a specific vivid benefit, subheadline restating exactly what they get and how it arrives, curiosity driven bullet copy ("fascinations"), a visual mock-up of the thing, and a minimal form. Fewer fields, higher conversion; name and email is the default ask.
- Sending paid traffic straight at a sales page or homepage is "proposing marriage on the first date". Capture first, nurture after.

**Phase 4: The Godfather Strategy (the offer they can't refuse).**
- The single most powerful element in the whole system. A strong offer succeeds despite weak copy; strong copy cannot save a weak offer. "Great service, great quality, great team" are not offers.
- Seven parts: (1) **Rationale**, a believable reason why the offer is so generous; (2) **Build value**, anchor the real price with proof before any discount, reduce cost to the ridiculous (per day, versus a coffee); (3) **Pricing**, lead with the most aggressive low friction entry point, expand with upsells after the first yes, never many choices up front; (4) **Payment options**, instalments lower the perceived price; (5) **Premiums**, relevant free extras genuinely raise response (Sports Illustrated's football phone); (6) **Power guarantee**, specific, measurable, named, tied to the result, because the seller should carry the risk, and his claim is fewer than 5% ever call it in while honest buyers who would otherwise hesitate say yes; (7) **Scarcity**, genuine only, deadlines, limited places, or it corrodes trust.
- The offer test: "if the offer and the guarantee don't keep the founder up at night, they're not strong enough." Write the outrageous version first, then pull back to what you can truly deliver.
- For service businesses the Godfather Offer is usually a packaged, named, value anchored free consultation ("Free 45 minute [named] session, valued at £X") sold as hard as a paid product: what happens on the call, what they leave with, why it is worth real money whether or not they buy.
- His 17 step selling system for any sales message: call out the audience; big promise headline; back it up in the subheadline; intrigue bullets; floodlight the problem and agitate it; present the solution; show credentials; detail benefits (not features); social proof; make the Godfather Offer; add bonuses; stack the value; reveal the price against the stack; inject genuine scarcity; give the power guarantee; one clear commanding call to action; close with a P.S. that warns and reminds.

**Phase 5: Traffic.**
- Unit economics first: cost per lead, cost per acquisition, lifetime value. Know what a customer is worth and CPA becomes a dial, not a fear. Judge channels on earnings per click and total volume, not percentage ROI at tiny spend ("no point spending £100 to make £1,200; spend £1,000 to make £6,000").
- One channel at a time. Prove an offer converts profitably on it, reach a stable return (his threshold: at least 50% ROI), then stack the next channel with the profits. Never depend on a single channel; platforms change the rules overnight.
- Traffic temperature must match message temperature. Cold ("Tinder") traffic gets a low commitment ask: a quiz, a guide, a competition. Warm gets the consultation. Hot gets the buy button. Serving a hot message to cold traffic is the single commonest ad mistake.
- The ad has exactly one job: sell the click. Not the product, not the opt-in, the click. Google ads win on intrigue and differentiation from the sameness around them (he drafts 20 plus headlines before picking). Facebook is a native platform: ads that look like ads get tuned out, ads that read like news or an interesting article get engagement, so no logos, no glossy graphics, no price in the creative, headline of roughly 12 to 18 words, "Learn more" as the least threatening button.

**Phase 6: The Magic Lantern Technique.**
- For the majority who opt in but do not book or buy: a sequence of two or three genuinely valuable pieces of content (video works best, production quality irrelevant) that each move the prospect one real step towards their desired outcome, mapped as milestones on a timeline from where they are to where they want to be. Roughly 80% value, 20% invitation, with the Godfather Offer (book a call) as the standing call to action at each step. Each email teases the next piece so the sequence gets awaited rather than endured.
- The effect: scepticism falls as trust rises with each delivered result, so the eventual call needs no pressure. Prospects who never buy still stay on the list because the value was real, and buy later.

**Phase 7: Sales conversion (sell like a doctor).**
- "A prescription without a diagnosis is malpractice." The call opens with diagnosis, not pitch: why did you book this call; what have you tried; how long; why now; what would twelve months of success look like; on a scale of one to ten how important is fixing this. Get the admission that what they are doing is not working and they want help.
- A short survey or application before the call qualifies the prospect, changes the psychology (they asked you for help) and filters out bad fits. If they are not a fit, say so and do not sell to them, even if they want to buy.
- Deliver value on the call itself: splinter off a small piece of the service and solve something live, proving you can help by actually helping. Then prescribe: a two minute, rehearsed overview of the programme phrased in the prospect's own words, benefits not features.
- His close: test questions ("other than money, is there any reason not to start today?"), state the price with conviction and keep talking into the payment options, then a fast action incentive (waiving a set-up fee for deciding on the call), then silence.

**Phase 8: Automate and multiply (email).**
- Email is his highest earning channel and the only audience asset you own; platforms can change the rules on everything else. (His cited figures, roughly 40 to 1 returns, are marketing industry claims, not house facts.)
- Three jobs: get delivered, get opened, get clicked. Deliverability is engineering: sender reputation, engagement signals, warm-up, and above all sending things people want to open. Roughly two thirds value to one third offers.
- The P group and C group: every inbox is sorted into personal and commercial in seconds. Personal always gets opened. So: personal sender name, short lower case subject lines (his sweet spot: under 20 characters, or long, never the average middle), plain text over designed HTML (his and HubSpot's testing: design lowers opens and clicks), no logo in the header, preheader text that opens a loop.
- The number one email sin is being boring. Entertain, tell stories, be a person, then deliver the idea. Vague copy causes "email blindness"; visceral specific copy cures it. Tell the reader what to do, one call to action.

## Part 2: What translates to this business and what does not

The book's default case is a solo service business selling to the person who will use the service, with a tone calibrated for cold Australian and American direct response markets. A-Level Accelerators sells a high consideration purchase to UK parents spending real money on their child's future, with the student as the end user, under a published honesty rule. That changes the translation in specific places.

**Translates directly:**
- The entire funnel architecture (HVCO, opt-in, offer page, nurture, call, email). It is exactly the shape this business already half built.
- Diagnosis before prescription. Suby's Phase 7 is literally Waleed's own "diagnose before you treat" medical framing. The book strengthens the case for making the diagnostic the front door of everything.
- Traffic temperature matching. Parents from a cold Facebook ad should meet a quiz or guide, not a price. The live parents ad already does this correctly.
- Unit economics discipline, one channel proven before the next, judging small spend on leading indicators.
- The Godfather Offer discipline: rationale, value anchoring, named packaged consultation, genuine scarcity (real cohort dates), instalments, premiums.
- Email doctrine: personal, plain text, entertaining, value led, short subject lines. This is close to Waleed's existing voice rules already.

**Translates with adaptation:**
- **Two dream buyers, not one.** The book assumes one avatar. Here there are two (parent buyer, student user) with a standing rule never to mix them in one message. Every Suby exercise (Halo research, HVCO titles, offer copy, Magic Lantern) has to be run twice, once per audience, and the diagnostic's parent and student forks already embody this. The parent avatar is the buyer avatar; the student avatar drives product love and word of mouth.
- **Fear copy.** Suby is right that parents are moved more by fear of loss than hope of gain, and the hours-leak maths in the diagnostic is already an honest quantified fear. The adaptation: fear content must always be true, sourced and specific (real deadlines, real consequences of weak Year 12 grades for predicted grades and UCAS), never manufactured panic about their child.
- **Headline register.** Adopt his specificity, numbers, curiosity and "what nobody tells you" framing. Reject the tabloid register (ALARMING, SHOCKING, "will blow your mind"). A parent choosing help for their child is buying trust; National Enquirer wrapping torches it, and it fails the house voice rules anyway. The Merrill Lynch end of his spectrum (plain talk, public service announcement tone, "why are we publishing this") fits this brand; the Enquirer end does not.
- **Guarantees.** A power guarantee is the single biggest untried Suby weapon here, but it cannot be a grade guarantee: no results data exists until August 2026 and grade-outcome claims are banned. What can honestly be guaranteed is money and effort risk (satisfaction windows, refund terms on the programmes, "if the first session isn't worth it, full refund" shapes). Any guarantee needs Waleed's explicit sign-off because he carries it.
- **The close.** Keep the diagnosis structure, the pre-call survey, the twelve month question, delivering value live on the call. Drop the pressure mechanics (fast action bonus for deciding on the call, "the person who speaks first loses") when the buyer is a parent deciding about their child: a pressured parent churns, complains or warns other parents. Honest urgency (a cohort genuinely starts on a date, places genuinely limited by his teaching hours) does the same job cleanly.
- **"Make huge claims" offer drafting.** Use his write-outrageous-then-pull-back exercise privately as a creative tool, but the pull-back boundary is the approved proof points list, not "what we can get away with".

**Does not translate, or is rejected:**
- Manufactured scarcity (countdown clocks on evergreen offers, fake "only X left"). Clashes with the honesty rule. Real scarcity only.
- NLP "embedded commands" framing. The usable core (clear, direct, single calls to action) is kept; the covert influence framing is dropped.
- His claimed statistics (25% opt-in rates, 40 to 1 email ROI, client case revenue numbers). Recorded here as his claims; never quoted in our copy or dashboards as facts.
- "Borrow cash to pour into ads" aggression. The gated roadmap in the business audit governs spend, and paid spend decisions stay with Waleed.
- Fear mongering ad type in his ad taxonomy, where it means invented threats. The honest version (true stakes, made vivid) is already covered above.

## Part 3: Scorecard, where the business already matches the framework

Read against the eight phases, the business is unusually strong at the top of the funnel and thin at the bottom. That matches the known symptom (leads arriving, weak conversion to paid).

- **Phase 1 (dream buyer):** Strong. The audience split is codified, parent research exists (Facebook groups doc, lead radar, where parents ask), the diagnostic's archetypes are effectively seven researched avatars, and copy already uses harvested parent and student language. The formal Power 4% exercise has never been run on the 18 real Stripe payments, and honestly cannot say much yet at that sample size; revisit when there are more buyers.
- **Phase 2 (HVCO):** Strong. The revision diagnostic is a textbook HVCO, quiz format, personalised output, exactly the "answers the burning question" shape, with the tracker, parents' guide and newsletter behind it. Suby would call the free tool suite over-built if anything: three HVCOs and counting, with the conversion machinery behind them underweight.
- **Phase 3 (opt-in):** Strong. The gates ask little, optional fields never block the report, parent and student routing is clean.
- **Phase 4 (Godfather Offer):** Weak. This is the biggest gap. See Part 4.
- **Phase 5 (traffic):** In progress and correctly shaped. The first Meta ad sends cold parents to the diagnostic (right temperature), is judged on leading indicators at £10 a day, and the lead-gen map already carries break-even tables. One channel at a time is being respected.
- **Phase 6 (Magic Lantern):** Partial. The 16 email student and 13 email parent sequences exist and are value led, which is most of the technique. What they converge on is under-powered (see Part 4), and there is no video in the nurture path, which is his highest trust format and an obvious fit for a doctor with a camera-ready founder story.
- **Phase 7 (sales conversion):** Half built. The free 30 minute call exists and Waleed's instinct (diagnose, advise, never pitch a programme in outreach drafts) matches the sell-like-a-doctor chapter. But the call is not packaged, valued or sold as an offer, and there is no pre-call survey or application step.
- **Phase 8 (email):** Strong on assets (sequences in the repo, automations live and verified, Sunday Session cadence), untested on his open-rate doctrine (subject line style, plain text discipline, entertainment quotient). Worth an audit pass against the P group checklist when Waleed wants one.

## Part 4: The gaps, ranked by expected impact

1. **There is no Godfather Offer anywhere in the funnel.** The Summer Accelerator page sells a programme (curriculum, pricing, FAQs) but the offer is "here is the programme, here is the price". No rationale, no value stack, no premiums, no guarantee, no risk reversal beyond consumer law. Suby's central claim is that this one element doubles or triples response on identical traffic. Building one honestly (money-back terms, real premiums such as included diagnostics or parent check-ins, instalments, genuine cohort scarcity) is the highest expected-value move in the whole book for this business, and it needs Waleed's decisions on what he can carry.
2. **The free call is not sold.** It is the linchpin conversion event for a high consideration service, and today it is a calendar link. Suby's treatment: name it, frame what a parent leaves with whether or not they buy, anchor its value, sell it with the same energy as a paid product, and gate it with a short survey so Waleed walks in with the diagnosis half done and the parent has invested. This also matches the missing appointment layer diagnosis already recorded in the 17 July lead generation map; two independent frameworks now point at the same hole.
3. **The nurture sequences converge on a weak ask.** Value-led emails that end in an under-sold call are Magic Lantern with the lantern lit and no destination. Once the call is packaged (gap 2), the sequences' calls to action should converge on it by name.
4. **No guarantee exists on any paid programme.** Bounded by the no-grade-claims rule, but a service guarantee is available today and unused.
5. **The offer pyramid is missing its middle.** Free tools then a four figure programme is a big jump for a first-time buyer. Suby's pricing structure (aggressive entry offer, then ascend) suggests the gap the paid Error Log was already heading towards before it was pulled to become a paid add-on. When Waleed prices it, it is the natural low friction first purchase.
6. **No video anywhere in the nurture path**, despite the founder being the single most differentiating asset (an NHS doctor who did it himself) and video being the book's preferred trust builder.

## Part 5: Applied to the first paid traffic run

What the framework says about the campaign that went live on 28 July:

- The shape is right. Cold parent traffic to a quiz HVCO is exactly Phase 5 temperature matching. Do not let anyone (including a future session) "optimise" it into sending cold traffic at the Summer Accelerator sales page.
- Judge it like Suby, not like a dashboard. At £10 a day the only honest readings are CPL trend, click-through rate, landing page conversion rate and lead quality. Percentage ROI at this spend is noise in both directions. The decision to scale waits for cost per lead against the break-even tables in the lead generation map.
- The ad's only job is the click. Creative reviews should ask "does this look like an ad or like something a parent would read anyway", and "is it selling the click or trying to sell tutoring". His Facebook doctrine (native look, no price, no logo dominance, curiosity headline in parent language) is the review standard.
- Scaling rule when it works: this channel to stable profitability first, then stack the next (his order of operations), not three channels at once. And the whole system only deserves more money once the back end (Parts 4 gaps 1 and 2) can convert the leads, otherwise scaling buys more leads into the same leak.

## Part 6: Applied to the free-tools-to-paid conversion problem

Suby's reframe of the known weakness: the business does not have a lead problem or a traffic problem, it has an offer problem, in his precise sense. The diagnostic fills the top of the pyramid correctly; conversion is weak because the two conversion mechanisms his system depends on (the Godfather Offer and the sold, packaged call) do not exist yet. The email machine is nurturing people towards an ask that was never built. That is the diagnosis this document exists to record: fix the offer and the appointment layer before spending more to fill the top.

## Part 7: The standing pressure test

When Waleed asks "should I run this offer", "is this landing page any good", "review this ad", "why aren't leads converting", or anything in that family, the session answers through this framework and names the part. The short checklist:

1. **Dream buyer:** which of the two avatars is this for, and is it written in their harvested language? (Phase 1)
2. **Temperature:** does the ask match how cold this audience is? (Phase 5)
3. **The click:** if it is an ad, is it selling only the click, and would it pass as native content? (Phase 5)
4. **The bait:** if it is a lead magnet, does the title alone stop the right parent or student, and does the content genuinely deliver? (Phase 2)
5. **The offer:** run the seven parts. Rationale, value built before price, entry pricing, payment options, premiums, guarantee, real scarcity. Which are present, which are missing, which are banned here and why? (Phase 4)
6. **The ask it converges on:** does every nurture path end at a named, value anchored next step that has itself been sold? (Phases 3, 6)
7. **The call:** diagnosis before prescription, survey before call, value delivered live, no pressure close on parents. (Phase 7)
8. **The email:** would this land in the P group or the C group? Is it entertaining or dutiful? (Phase 8)
9. **The economics:** what CPL and CPA does this need to work, against the break-even tables, and is the read being taken at meaningful volume? (Phase 5)
10. **The honesty gate:** every claim against approved proof points, every scarcity real, every guarantee one Waleed has explicitly agreed to carry. House rules beat the book on any conflict, and the conflict gets said out loud.
