Why I start with a call, not a lesson

In medicine, you never treat before you diagnose.

When a patient comes in exhausted, I don't guess at a fix. We find the cause, then treat that. Revision problems work exactly the same way, and it's where most tutoring goes wrong: it starts treating without a diagnosis. More lessons, more content, more hours of the same. If the way your child studies is broken, more hours just leak out of the same holes.

So the call is a diagnosis. We look at how your child actually studies, across the five systems every grade is built on:

1How content goes in
2Whether it's ever retrieved, or just re-read
3When past papers happen, if at all
4How the week is planned
5What happens under exam pressure

When the grades are stuck, one or more of these is broken. Once you know which, the fix is usually obvious, and it rarely involves working more hours. Top grades are a system, not a talent. I've watched hard-working students change completely the moment someone finally showed them how to work.

Fair questions

Things parents ask before booking

Is this a sales call in disguise?

No, and you'll feel the difference inside five minutes. The first 25 minutes are diagnosis and plan. In the last few minutes, if one of my programmes fits your child, I'll name it, price it and explain why. If it doesn't fit, I'll tell you that instead. Either way the plan is yours to keep, and nobody chases you afterwards.

Which ages and subjects does this cover?

The call is about how your child studies, so it works for any GCSE or A-level student, Year 9 to 13, whatever their subject mix. On the programme side, I teach the study system myself, and our A-level subject programmes cover Biology, Chemistry and Maths.

We already have a subject tutor.

Keep them, if they're good. A subject tutor fixes subject gaps. I work on the layer underneath: how your child takes content in, gets it back out of memory, and performs under exam pressure. That layer is where most marks leak, and most tutoring never touches it. The two work well together, and on the call I'll tell you honestly whether your current setup is enough.

What does it cost if we carry on afterwards?

The call costs nothing and stands on its own. If a programme fits, I'll tell you the exact price on the call, before you decide anything, and there are options at different levels. I'll only recommend the one I honestly think fits, and I'll tell you if none of them do.

My child won't engage with anything we suggest.

Extremely common, and worth raising on the call, because part of what we'll cover is how to have this conversation at home. In my experience the same advice lands completely differently coming from a doctor in his twenties who sat these exams six years ago than from a parent. That's not a criticism of you. It's just how teenagers work.

Why is it free?

Because it's the best way I know to show you how I think, and I'd rather diagnose properly than advertise loudly. Some families join a programme afterwards. Plenty don't, and leave with a plan that helps anyway. Both outcomes are fine with me.

When do the calls happen?

You pick a slot from my live calendar. I work NHS shifts, so most availability is evenings and weekends. If the calendar looks thin, that's the rota, not a marketing trick.
