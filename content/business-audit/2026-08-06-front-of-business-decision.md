# Front of the business: the decision memo

Written 6 August 2026 at Waleed's request. The question: should the business stop leading with tutor-delivered subject courses and rebuild its front around programmes taught by Waleed himself? This is advisory only. Nothing on the site, the offers or the payment setup changes off the back of it. The decision rules at the end are proposals for Waleed to adopt or amend before 10 August, then freeze.

## 1. The recommendation

**Yes. Put yourself at the front of the business. You are right about the differentiator, and the evidence for it is stronger than you have been giving yourself credit for. But "the front" needs a precise definition, because the version where the tutor-delivered programmes get scrapped or buried is wrong, and your own sales history is the proof.**

- **The front becomes you:** the face, the method, the diagnostic, the content, the community, the sales conversations, and the entry-level paid products (Study Series, Study Accelerator, Locked In). All taught and voiced by you.
- **The tutor-delivered subject programmes stay as the back end,** re-founded as your system: designed and supervised by Dr Waleed, delivered by tutors trained in his method. They are the only products that have ever taken money, and the only verified £1m-shaped revenue engine in this market.
- **The rebuild does not start with the website.** It starts Monday 10 August with the Skool founding email, and the whole question gets settled with money by 6 October, through a test that is already built, dated, and waiting on your go.

## 3. Why you are right that the differentiator is you

Three independent lines of evidence agree:

1. **Every pound this business has ever earned arrived through you at the front.** Nobody ever bought off the tutors' CVs. They bought after you taught them live and asked. The product they bought happened to be tutor-delivered subject teaching, which matters below, but the selling force was you.
2. **Your audience's gravity is the doctor.** The 13 July audit read your signup records: dominated by Biology and Chemistry students with UCAT anxieties and medicine ambitions. They did not come to a tutoring marketplace. They came to a doctor.
3. **The market position is verified empty.** Three separate research passes (content, funnel, competitors) found the same hole: nobody in the UK combines A-level, multi-subject, parent-addressed and doctor-fronted. The A-level-only content lane has no mega account. The diagnostic is the only assessment-style capture tool in the nine-competitor set. The moat is you plus the diagnosis-first method. It was never the classroom roster.

One honest nuance from your own ad data: the winning creative was the ugly text card at £7.80 a lead, while your face at £35.08 got paused. Front-of-business does not mean your face on every thumbnail. It means you are the person they meet the moment they lean in: the report, the emails, the call, the community, the close. That layer is where the doctor converts, and it is the layer the tutors cannot supply.

## 4. What the two flops actually prove

### The Summer Accelerator zero

You listed the effort: student DMs, Facebook ads, group posts. Here is what that effort actually delivered to the offer: **five verified parent leads, days old, still mid-nurture; student conversations run on a playbook that is deliberately diagnostic-first, not sales-first; and a sales page with no testimonials, selling an 8 August start in the final fortnight, against MyEdSpace at £49 and PMT at £95 to £245.**

Meanwhile the two mechanisms that produced 100 percent of lifetime revenue were never pointed at it. No broadcast about the Summer Accelerator has ever been sent to your 454 actives. No live event has run since 6 May. The appointment layer designed in the 17 July map (call-first report page, archetype call script) was never installed; the personal outreach habit only began on 2 August.

At the break-even arithmetic in the 17 July plan, a considered £289 to £849 family purchase needs something like 1 sale per 10 to 30 leads even in a healthy funnel. Five leads were never going to produce a sale, for any offer, differentiated or not. **Zero sales from this input is not evidence about the product. There is no evidence about the product yet.** Your "no reason to pick us" theory may be right, but the funnel never carried enough people to the choosing moment to test it. What the summer does prove: cold channels alone, without the warm machine and without a conversation step, do not sell this. The 17 July map predicted exactly that before the test ran.

### Why the study products flopped in May: the serious answer

Four causes, in order of weight:

1. **The pitch reached almost nobody, and the campaign stopped mid-arc.** The follow-up to attendees went to 26 people. February's money landed across a week of follow-up emails (six of them; your two biggest click spikes ever were follow-ups). May got one follow-up email, then the account went silent for three months, because the silence knocked you and you stopped. You did not test a product in May. You tested half a campaign.
2. **Nothing manufactured the problem before the pitch.** Method is an unfelt need. Students believe they have a Chemistry problem; parents believe they have a grades problem; nobody wakes up wanting to buy a revision system. The diagnostic, the machine that converts "I work hard and it doesn't show" into "your system leaks hours a week, here is the treatment", did not exist until July. Diagnose before you treat is your own rule. The May launch broke it: treatment first, no diagnosis.
3. **The buyer was never addressed.** The pitch went at students, who do not hold the card. Parents got one summary email. The entire parent side (guide, sequences, ads, group plan) was built after the flop.
4. **The kernel that survives anyway:** even 26 to 48 warm attendees produced at most one sale. That is a real signal, and it should shape what you sell rather than whether you sell it: method moves as a low-friction recurring membership and as the wrapper that makes people choose your subject programmes. It moves worst as a naked mid-ticket one-off info product pitched at teenagers. The September design (Locked In at £29, Study Accelerator with 1:1 support at £499, subjects wrapped in the method) already reflects this.

**Conclusion: May tested launch conditions that no longer exist.** The proposition "will people pay for Waleed-taught method" has never had a fair test. It gets its first one in September, at close to zero build cost, into the warmest audience this business has ever had.

## 5. Your five arguments, stress-tested

1. **"One-to-many scales without limit."** True of delivery, but delivery has never been your constraint. Demand is. You have 454 actives, 952 LinkedIn followers and five ad leads; nothing you run is capacity-bound yet. Verdict: real, premature, and no reason to wait, because it also makes the test cheap.
2. **"It does not depend on my tutors; my schedule is the only constraint."** Backwards. Your schedule is the scarcest, least controllable resource in the whole business: FY rota, August changeover, nights. The only verified profitable £1m+ operator in this market (Justin Craig, £1.36m filed, audited) runs on tutors off payroll and 13 core staff; the 16 July reaudit's own line is that there is no version of the £1m table a single person teaches alone. Verdict: this is an argument for founder-fronted, and against founder-delivers-everything. The tutors are how the business survives your rota and scales past your diary. Keep them; re-found them under your method.
3. **"Monthly retainer, always something new to teach."** Right, and already decided: Locked In opens 21 September at £29. With eyes open about the physics: the June cliff is real, your entire customer base graduates out roughly every two years, and parents audit direct debits. The pack's own arithmetic: 100 members averaging £35 is £42,000 a year. A floor and a funnel, not the engine. Verdict: adopt as designed, size honestly.
4. **"Opens GCSE and university markets."** The trap in the list. Your own July research found the winnable lane is A-level-only plus doctor (empty), while GCSE method content is the most crowded corner of UK education and university students have no paying parent behind them. Widen to everyone and you are positioned for no one. GCSE enters as the feeder, exactly as planned (20 August GCSE results day targets the parents of next year's Year 12s). University is off the table this year. Verdict: rejected for now, on the strength of your own research.
5. **"Upsell path into the subject accelerators lifts LTV."** Right, and notice what it concedes: the upsell path requires the subject accelerators to exist and stay sold. This argument is for method-as-front-door, not method-instead-of-subjects. Verdict: correct, and it settles the architecture. Method at the front. Subjects at the back.

## 6. The strongest case against, at full strength

1. **All verified money, yours and the market's, is subject-outcome money.** Your 18 payments bought subject teaching. Every scaled UK comparator sells subjects or outcomes. No UK consumer brand sells study-skills-alone at scale: the biggest study-skills teaching operation in UK schools sells to the schools themselves, not to families, and Ali Abdaal sells method to adults. Parents buy grades; method is usually how you win the choice, not what the cheque says. If "rebuild the front" slides into method-only, you could end up with a warm, beloved, low-revenue community and a starved subject engine.
2. **The one time warm attendees heard a Waleed-taught method pitch, at most one bought.**
3. **The rebuild instinct is dangerous for you specifically.** Both audits documented the pattern: building instead of selling, through the exact weeks that reward selling most. "Rebuild the front of the business" is the most seductive build brief imaginable, arriving at results week.

What survives this case: point 1 is why the subject programmes keep the revenue-engine seat and September sells both layers, not one. Point 2 is answered by running the fair retest, and by selling method as membership and wrapper rather than naked info product. Point 3 is answered by the shape of the next step: **nothing new gets built.** The front-rebuild's first 60 days is pressing send on assets that already exist. The homepage does not move until October evidence exists.

## 7. The decision, precisely worded

- **Dr Waleed sells the method; A-Level Accelerators sells the programmes.** The Skool pack's brand rule, adopted business-wide.
- You are the front: content, diagnostic, community, Study Series, Study Accelerator, Locked In, every sales conversation, every piece of proof.
- Tutors are capacity, not positioning: "designed and supervised by Dr Waleed, delivered by tutors trained in his system". That one line is what re-founds the subject programmes and answers "no reason to pick us" without deleting anything. (Copy change for September/October, with your approval, not now.)
- GCSE is a feeder reached through parents. University is a no this year.
- The public re-platform (homepage leads with you and the method entry, subject programmes repositioned underneath) happens in October only if September's money says so.
