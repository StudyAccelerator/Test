## 2. What £100k a year actually needs

£100,000 a year is £8,333 of MRR held every month of the year. At £300 that is 28 students paying every single month, summer included.

But MRR is not held every month. Your own worry number three is correct: Year 13s stop paying after their exams. So a £100k year needs a spring peak well above £8,333 to cover a summer trough. The model below says the peak needs to sit around £12,000 (40 students) if the summer falls to a quarter of it.

I built a cohort model (assumptions and the full monthly table are in the appendix). Three scenarios, differing only in how many new students you sign in a peak month and how many leave each month after the four month minimum:

| Scenario | New students in a peak month | Monthly churn after month 4 | Revenue Sep 2026 to Aug 2027 | Revenue Sep 2027 to Aug 2028 | Spring 2027 peak MRR | Summer 2027 low MRR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slow | 4 | 15% | £43,000 | £57,000 | £6,100 (20 students) | £1,300 (4 students) |
| Base | 8 | 8% | £78,000 | £129,000 | £12,100 (40 students) | £2,700 (9 students) |
| Fast | 15 | 5% | £137,000 | £255,000 | £22,400 (75 students) | £5,200 (17 students) |

Read that honestly:

- **The slow line never reaches £100k on the mentorship alone.** Four a month at the September peak, falling with the season, with the churn the March cohort's refund rate implies (4 of 18 payments were half refunded, 22%), tops out near £60k a year.
- **The base line crosses £100k in the second year:** trailing twelve months passes £100k around March 2028. That needs eight new students in September and January, about five in a typical term month, and one student in twelve leaving each month once past the minimum.
- **The fast line crosses £100k in year one,** but it needs three to four new students every week through the autumn while you are working full time, and by spring 2027 it is 75 students. At two hours a student a month that is 35 hours a week of delivery on top of the hospital. That line is not available to you as a solo operator. It is only available with a second person delivering.

So my honest answer to "when is £100k realistic": **spring 2028 on the base case, and only if October and November hold up.** Two numbers decide which line we are on, and we will know both by the end of November:

1. New students signed in October and November. Base needs about six in October and four in November. Fewer than three in October means the September week was a backlog being worked, not a rate.
2. Month five retention of the September five. That is February 2027. Any of the five leaving before then is a churn signal we should treat as real, not as a one-off.

## 3. What £1m needs, and why it is not a bigger version of £100k

£1,000,000 a year at £300 a month is 278 students paying every month. At £400 it is 209. At a solo delivery cost of two hours a student a month, 278 students is 130 hours a week. There is no solo version of this. The 16 July reaudit said the same about the tutor programmes, and it is truer of the mentorship because you are the product.

Three shapes get there, and you can run them in this order:

1. **Coaches delivering your system (from September 2027 at the earliest).** You keep the diagnosis and the sales call; trained coaches run the fortnightly calls and the weekly follow-up under your name and method. At a coach cost of around £60 a student a month (two hours at £30) the margin per student is still about £240. This is the Justin Craig shape: founder-designed, staff-delivered. The gate to open it is one coach whose students retain as well as yours for a full term.
2. **A price rise on new students.** Your own sales anchor is one-to-one tutoring at £400 a month for ONE subject. £300 for every subject is cheap against your own argument. £350 to £400 from January for new sign-ups (the current five stay at £300) is the cheapest experiment in the business and it moves £1m from 278 students to 209.
3. **A group tier under the one-to-one tier.** A group mentorship at £120 to £150 a month (one coach to six or eight students) for the families who say no at £300. This is a different product and it comes last, once the coach layer works.

**Timing, stated plainly:** the earliest credible £1m year is 2029 to 2030, and only if the base case holds through 2027, the first coach works in the 2027 to 2028 academic year, and you have four to six coaches by September 2028. Nothing verified in this market went from a £6k lifetime base to £1m in under three years. Justin Craig took decades to sit at £1.36m, and MyEdSpace burned through a £9.5m accumulated deficit getting to an undisclosed revenue in four years. Plan £1m in years and gates, not quarters. Do not spend a minute on it until the £100k trailing figure is real and a second person has delivered a term with your retention.

## 4. Your three worries, answered without sugar

### 4.1 Churn and the four month minimum

**Challenge first: a recommendation is not a minimum.** Unless the four months is written into the payment terms (a minimum term on the Stripe checkout, or four months paid up front), the parent can cancel in month two and you have no lever except goodwill. Tell me how the five are actually billed. If it is a plain monthly Stripe subscription, the minimum exists only in your head.

**The mocks argument cuts both ways.** You reason that parents will not leave once their child is being assessed. True for some. But parents also leave after mocks for two other reasons: mocks went well, so "it worked, we can stop", and mocks went badly, so "it didn't work". Both are common in tutoring. And the timing is tighter than four months: a Year 13 who joins in September hits mocks in late November, at month three. Predicted grades and UCAS pressure ease for many Year 13s by January, which is a natural leaving point right at your month four.

**What the data says:** nothing yet. The only churn-like evidence this business has is the March cohort's refunds, 4 of 18 payments half refunded. That is 22%, and it is why the slow scenario uses 15% monthly churn. I am not saying that is your churn; I am saying it is the only number we have and you should want to beat it visibly.

**What I would do this week:**

- Make the four months contractual for new sign-ups: "four month minimum, then monthly" on the checkout, or a small discount for four months paid up front. The current five are grandfathered.
- Log every leaver's reason, in one line, on the Sales panel. Ten leavers with reasons is worth more than any theory.
- Send parents something they can see each month: the grade ladder from the report, what got fixed, what is next. Parents keep paying for progress they can see. Assessment alone does not do that; a badly explained mock result is a cancellation.
- The KPI I will report: month two and month five retention, by cohort month.

### 4.2 Is this week's rate a September effect?

**Yes, partly, and it is also a backlog effect.** Two things are true at once:

- Demand for tutoring in the UK is seasonal and September is the peak. Your own lead counts show it: 10 in April, 25 in May (the first ad), 2 in June, 10 in July, 76 in August (results week plus the ads), 44 in the first twenty days of September. January is the second peak (mock results, predicted grades), March to April is a smaller third one (exam panic), and June to early August is dead except results week.
- The five did not come from one week of leads. They came from a pool of about 120 leads gathered since 1 August that you only started ringing in earnest this month. A backlog converts fast and then it is gone.

**So expect October to be slower per lead, and expect the same call effort to produce fewer sales.** That is not failure. It is the number that tells us which scenario we are in.

**The test, so we do not argue about it later:** for the next four weeks I will report sales per week against leads per week and calls made per week. If October delivers three or more new students at the same calling effort, the machine converts and we scale ads. If it delivers two or fewer, the September week was the backlog and we fix the funnel before spending more. Do not raise the ad budget before that answer exists.

### 4.3 The summer, and whether we start from zero every year

**Straight answer: MRR does not go to zero, but it falls hard, and it falls at the worst possible moment for you personally.**

- Year 13s finish exams by late June. Expect cancellations from mid May, not June; parents stop paying when the last paper is in sight, not after it.
- Year 12s carry over. They become Year 13s in September and, if they are still with you, they are the base of the next year's book. In the model, summer MRR is 20 to 25% of the spring peak, and almost all of it is Year 12s.
- So no, you do not start from zero. You start from the Year 12 carry. The size of that carry is the single most controllable thing about next summer, and it is set by who you recruit between now and April.

**Will there be months with almost no new revenue?** Yes: June, July and the first half of August, every year, on this product. And your NHS income drops to 60% in April 2027 and to nothing in August 2027. Those two dips land on top of each other.

**How the gap gets covered, in the order I would do it:**

1. **Skew acquisition to Year 12 from January.** Ad copy, the diagnostic's routing and your sales calls should favour Year 12s and pre-A-level students from January onwards, because a Year 12 signed in January can pay for eighteen months; a Year 13 signed in January pays for four. This one change roughly doubles next summer's floor in the model.
2. **Sell the summer.** A paid summer track for rising Year 13s ("get ahead before Year 13" at a lower monthly price) and a bridge for Year 11s after GCSE results (results day 2027 will be in the third week of August). The Summer Accelerator page still exists in git and can be brought back as the vehicle.
3. **Pull cash forward without changing the headline price.** Offer a term paid up front, or a year at a discount, as an option on the checkout, never as the advertised price. Parents who take it fund your summer.
4. **Build the reserve now.** From this month, move 25% of every month's revenue into a separate business savings pot until it holds three months of your living costs. Under the base case that pot reaches about £10,000 by April 2027. That is your summer.
5. **The Medic Life contract** (£5k for the interview series plus a share of A-level tutoring, February to April 2027) lands in the run-up to the summer. Sign it and treat it as summer money, not spending money.
6. **Do not go to 0% NHS in August 2027 unless the trailing three months, including a summer month, covered your take-home.** The 60% LTFT from April 2027 is the right shape for the 2027 to 2028 year. The decision on going fully into the business should be taken on 1 May 2027 with April's numbers in hand, not in a good week in September.

To turn that into actual pounds I need your monthly take-home and your monthly living costs. Give me those and the reserve target and the LTFT decision become numbers, not sentences.

## 5. The things you did not ask about, which I would challenge anyway

## Appendix: the model

Monthly churn applies from month five (the four month minimum honoured).
